## Further discovery targets

Find and verify the necessary primary works rather than guessing identifiers: Peirce's sign/interpretant writings and editions; Wiener, von Foerster and relevant second-order cybernetic work; Maturana/Varela and relevant critiques of autopoietic transfer; Shannon's original scope; common-ground/repair and pragmatics; Gaver's auditory icons; Blattner/Sumikawa/Greenberg's earcons; Walker and colleagues' spearcons; relevant auditory-display and sonification studies; formal semantic-information/agency accounts including Kolchinsky and Wolpert; human-factors studies of urgency, workload, alarms, uncertainty, intelligibility and anthropomorphism.

These are bounded search directions, not an instruction to read every work in every tradition. The pilot must identify the actual primary publications, contrary perspectives, access needs and relevance to RQ1–RQ6. No fixed reading quota or mandatory positive conclusion applies.

## Prior-claim corrections to carry forward

Avoiding performed human emotion is the project's constraint, not an established conclusion of Brier's research. The earlier examples assigning every pitch or pulse neatly to icon/index/symbol are hypotheses to reconsider in relation to resemblance, causal connection and convention. A machine can communicate something useful without this programme resolving whether it possesses intrinsic meaning or consciousness. Bibliographic dates and paper attribution must be verified at the source, not inherited from conversational confidence.
