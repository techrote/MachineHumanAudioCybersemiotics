## 2. Decisive contrary reading: the five levels persist in 2013

A superficially attractive chronology says that the 2003 five-level ontology was abandoned and replaced by the four-armed 2013 star. The consulted primary passages **refute that narrative**:

- **2003, *tripleC*, printed p. 81 (§4)**: Brier sets out five proposed ontological levels from Firstness/potentiality through mechanical causation, cybernetic-information signals, living sign relations and human conscious language. This is an **ontology of distinct explanatory levels**, not a survey of four academic disciplines.
- **2013, *Integral Review*, printed p. 224, “The Four Views of the Cybersemiotic Star,” Figure 1**: the four arms identify knowledge domains (physical, biological, subjective, intersubjective/social). This is a **perspectival/epistemic model**, not an enumeration of all ontological levels.
- **2013, printed p. 222, argument overview**: Brier separately announces a conclusion with **five ontological levels**. Critically, this is not just a forward reference: **printed p. 255, “The Ontological Basis of Cybersemiotics,” explicitly enumerates five levels**, and **p. 256 Figure 5** sketches their semiotic relations. The p. 255 account distinguishes Firstness/quantum vacuum, physical efficient causation, chemical/formal protosemiosis, living signification and self-conscious language.

**Result:** It is unsupported to say that the five levels became four by 2013. Both analytic devices coexist in one 2013 publication, demonstrable from rendered p. 224 and p. 255. There can still be *revision within the five-level ontology* between 2003 and 2013; the 2013 account gives chemical protosemiosis a prominent role. Whether this is a substantive new commitment, a clarification already present in 2006, or a rephrasing **cannot be decided** from 2006's abstract alone. Do not infer an isomorphism between individual star arms and ontology levels.

**Second caution on version ancestry:** The 2013 publication also refers to earlier Brier work and its book in prose, including an apparent `Brier (2006)` reference near printed p. 223; the reference list independently identifies `Brier (2006)` as a Danish-language information-science book and `Brier (2008a)` as the English *Cybersemiotics: Why Information Is Not Enough*. Without adjudicating authorial citation conventions and manifestations, do **not** infer that the 2013 prose establishes a textual derivation from the *Semiotica* 2006 article specifically. This is a bibliographic warning, not an assertion of a newly discovered error in an author-approved edition.

## 3. Scientific interpretation and falsification boundary

**Author's ontology is not empirical observation.** The five levels are Brier's philosophical proposal, not five experimentally established degrees of machine intelligence or auditory comprehension. The 2013 p. 255 description of quantum Firstness and chemical formal causation must not be treated as established physics or converted to a code variable with no operational argument.

**Adverse, remove-Brier test:** If a conventional HCI or semiotic-engineering analysis already distinguishes source emission, content representation, recipient interpretation, organizational authority and possible repair, the four-view/five-level terminology makes no distinctive practical prediction by itself. Brier might force an *audit of neglected explanatory standpoints*, but that payoff is hypothetical until an independently selected case yields a materially different conclusion after applying the best comparator. Conversely, a technically accurate alarm may still evoke listener affect; Brier does not establish an emotion-free machine voice as desirable.
