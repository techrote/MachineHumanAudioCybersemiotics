## 1. The author-claimed problem and target

**Brier's claim (not an established fact):** at p. 220 abstract, he proposes an explicitly non-reductionist integration of third-person natural/life-science knowledge, first-person phenomenological experience, and second-person intersubjective communication. In the heading **“The Four Views of the Cybersemiotic Star”**, printed pp. **224–225**, he treats physical, living, phenomenological and social investigations as four non-interchangeable knowledge interests, with autopoietic semiotic practice at the center. Figure 1 on p. **224** visibly displays the four arms and an embodiment/language/consciousness center. This is a **map of epistemic perspectives and an ontological proposal**, not an observed causal model of acoustic recognition.

**Interpretive reconstruction (provisional):**
- Premise P1: physical explanation alone does not settle first-person experience or culturally situated meaning (pp. **220, 230**).
- Premise P2: scientific inquiry presupposes embodied, linguistically interacting observers (pp. **220, 225**).
- Premise P3: Peircean signs and Luhmannian autopoietic communication offer resources for treating relations across the knowledge interests without collapsing their descriptive levels (p. **221** “Overview”; pp. **223–225**).
- Move M1: put semiotic-communicative practice rather than physics or language alone at the organizing center (p. **225**).
- Proposed payoff C1: a transdisciplinary account attentive simultaneously to physical constraints, organisms, social discourse and experience (pp. **224–225, 230**).

**Inference warrant still unproven:** the premise that disciplines have distinct irreducible explanatory targets does not by itself show that Brier's proposed synthesis is *necessary*, uniquely coherent or operationally superior. This is a target for #7/#8 criticism rather than a proof. Page **230** explicitly frames the project as resisting all forms of reductionism, from scientism to radical constructivism; it also expresses the author's strong view about conventional science and consciousness. Record that as *Brier's view*, not as a settled finding.

## 2. Concepts and important non-equivalences

| Object | What the consulted passage supports | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Four arms of the star**, Fig. 1, p. 224 | A proposed division of inquiry: matter/energy, living systems, sense/meaning in culture, inner life/consciousness | Four independent, validated variables or disjoint research domains |
| **First/second/third-person**, pp. 220, 225 | A distinction between subjective experience, intersubjective meaning and natural-scientific description | That a machine must possess or simulate those perspectives to emit a useful message |
| **Sign/interpretant**, p. 239 (not independently checked in this run) | No decisive source-bound interpretation admitted in this tranche | A preselected icon/index/symbol classification for auditory motifs |
| **Autopoiesis**, p. 221 overview and pp. 224–225 | Author proposes putting it in dialogue with Peircean semiotics and linguistic praxis | That an MHAC software component is literally an autopoietic living or social system |
| **Meaning versus information**, pp. 220, 230 | The research question is broader than transmitting machine states | That engineering information measures and interpretive meanings are the same quantity |

The **2003 abstract** describes a five-level schema involving Firstness, physical action, cybernetic/thermodynamic information, sign games and conscious language games. The **2013 article** instead foregrounds four knowledge perspectives (star diagram). These are *two different descriptions in two dated publications*. Whether Brier treats them as compatible, superseded, or addressing different analytic dimensions cannot be settled by comparing the 2003 abstract with selected 2013 pages. The required follow-on is direct full-paper, then book/chapter, source/edition comparison.

## 3. Strongest charitable and critical readings

**Charitable reading:** the star is useful as a discipline-check: before claiming a speaker has “communicated,” identify what is known physically about a signal, biologically about a recipient, socially about the context and phenomenologically about experienced meaning. This is **our hypothesis about use**, not a claim Brier makes about this application.

**Critical reading:** a map that insists on several perspectives could be obtained from pluralistic cognitive science, pragmatics, human factors and HCI without positing the whole Peirce–Luhmann–autopoiesis synthesis. The integration adds broad philosophical commitments, but their incremental explanatory or predictive value for a particular machine-to-human alert is unspecified. A system can correctly encode a hazard yet fail interpretation; that distinction is already accessible to non-cybersemiotic frameworks.

**Internal tension to test rather than resolve by assertion:** p. **225** combines a reality independent of knowledge with an emphasis on signs and observers; p. **230** rejects scientific monism while proposing a central coordinating practice. Is this a modest epistemic pluralism, an ontological thesis, or both? Which alternative accounts are actually ruled out? Reading the Peirce/Luhmann arguments in full is required.

## 4. Negative-case and emotion checks

Brier's integration of first-person experience and embodied life does not imply that **emotion-free synthetic speech is ethically or cognitively best**. The project preference for non-performed emotion is external to his argument. Indeed a framework explicitly retaining felt experience should encourage tests of listener affect even when the emitter does not simulate concern. Candidate negative case: under acoustic stress a deliberately flat voice might impair priority comprehension, while controlled expressive prosody might improve it. This is an **untested design hypothesis**, not a literature result.
