## 2. Reconstruction of Brier's 2003 argument (author claim versus inference)

| Proposition / move | What the consulted Brier passage says | Analytical status and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem P1: semiotic thresholds** | In §1, **p. 72**, Brier explicitly asks how physical interaction, information processing, living sign processes, language and machine communication differ. He associates human–machine interfaces with the distinction between digital computation and living perception. | **Author's explanandum**; not an empirical result about interface usability. |
| **P2: neither pan-information nor pan-semiosis suffices** | §§1–2, **pp. 72–75**, and §4, **p. 80**: he argues that the former fails to explain lived signification and the latter can erase the specificity of living sign-use or objective resistance. | **Author's critique**; objections to those *maximal* accounts do not refute every contemporary cognitive or information-theoretic account. |
| **P3: productive thresholds instead of a single substrate** | §4, **p. 81** lists five layers: potentiality/Firstness; matter and causal force; cybernetic information/signals; living sign games; human conscious language games. The article calls computers/robots **quasi- or proto-semiotic** at this point. | **Author's proposed ontology/architecture of description**, not five verified independent biological or psychological variables. |
| **P4: a machine's signs depend on an interpreter** | §5, **pp. 82–83**, Brier distinguishes a physical thermometer/instrument from a *living* recipient's triadic reading, relaying quotations attributed to **Peirce via Nöth (2002)** and to **Nöth (2002)** about computer-produced messages. | **Brier's argument using second-hand authorities**. Do not attribute the nested quotation directly to Peirce or Nöth as an independently checked passage. |
| **P5: meaning arises in coupled embodied/social life** | §7, **p. 86**: Brier *hypothesizes* that sign and language games emerge via interpenetration among biological, psychic and socio-communicative systems, linking motivation, feeling, perception and social language. | Explicitly **a hypothesis in Brier's source**, not experimentally demonstrated by the article; connecting this to a software component would be additional inference. |
| **M1: specialized forms of semiosis** | §8 and Figure 3, **p. 88**: endosemiotics (within the body), phenosemiotics (prelinguistic experience) and intrasemiotics (relations between bodily and psychic organization) are differentiated and shown schematically. | **Brier's conceptual distinctions**; Figure 3's drawn positions are not anatomical localization or measurable dataflow. |
| **M2: organize several forms in one model** | §9 and Figure 5, **pp. 90–91**: Brier connects socio-communicative language games, sign games, structural coupling, external ecological relations and internal semiosis. Figure caption itself warns that its split-person layout is a simplification. | **Proposed synthesis**, not a derivation or falsified competing theory. |
| **C1: claimed payoff** | §10, **p. 92**: Brier advances a transdisciplinary explanation retaining mechanical, informational and semiotic descriptions and claims that mind/body/social description requires integration rather than collapse into one explanatory level. | **Claimed philosophical payoff**, no prospective auditory predictions or comparative listener outcomes in this paper. |

**Inferential chain reconstructed, with the gap exposed:** If (i) signals can operate without a living interpreter but (ii) interpreted signs and conscious language depend on biological/psychic/social organization, then (iii) an explanation of **meaningful communication** cannot be exhausted by a causal model of the transmitter. Brier proposes unifying cybernetic/autopoietic closure and Peircean sign relations to describe this broader event. The missing warrant is whether the union itself adds explanatory power beyond the separate fields, or whether his strong thesis that only living systems have full triadic semiosis is necessary. The first two premises do not logically prove either conclusion.

**Strongest charitable reading:** the categorical distinction between a machine *producing a signal*, a person *taking it as a sign*, and an institution *treating the message as authoritative* blocks a common modelling equivocation. The source's claims about intrinsic semiosis still need defence; a practical auditor need not endorse their metaphysics to retain the distinction.

**Strongest objection:** contemporary adaptive systems, cognitive science, situated pragmatics and semiotic engineering may already represent referential grounding, interpretation, repair and designer intention without accepting Brier's categorical living/nonliving boundary. Brier criticizes particular reductionist extremes, not every possible pluralist alternative. The paper does not provide an operational test showing when a modern machine would cross from quasi-semiosis into true semiosis.

## 3. The 2003 five-level model is not a superseded 2013 four-level model

| Question | 2003 source | 2013 source (consulted extent) | Comparison and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is counted? | **Five proposed levels of existence / kinds of causation or semiosis**, §4 p. 81. | **Four knowledge interests or perspectives**, Figure 1 p. 224 and “Center of the Star” p. 225. | **Different analytical axes, not numeric disagreement.** The five levels are not a list of four academic disciplines. |
| What is the centre of analysis? | Threshold from signals to living signification and human language, §§1,4–5, pp. 72, 81–83. | Situated social/embodied semiotic knowledge generation at the centre of the four-armed star, pp. 224–225. | **Conceptual compatibility hypothesis** only; do not claim strict identity of objects or a proved historical development. |
| Did Brier abandon five levels by 2013? | Five levels are explicit in 2003. | The 2013 *own overview*, printed **p. 222**, also says the article concludes with a model of **five ontological levels**, despite earlier presenting a four-armed star. | **No evidence of replacement.** The tempting “five became four” narrative is contradicted by the 2013 article's programme statement. Check its later ontological-level passage and the monograph before mapping each 2003 level onto 2013 terminology. |
| Are machines literal autopoietic signers? | The 2003 author argues machines are quasi/proto-semiotic, pp. 81–83. | The 2013 consulted star pages situate knowledge in embodied and intersubjective practice; they do not alone determine any new stance on computational semiosis. | **Unresolved later position**, not a licence to attribute modern AI-machine claims to Brier. |

This directly tightens the earlier [2013-star preparatory note](R007-STAR-PREPARATORY-20261110.md), whose abstract-only 2003 contrast could not settle historical compatibility. The 2003 **primary body** now supplies its real distinction, while the 2013 overview itself rules out a simplistic replacement story. Neither item can stand in for reading Brier's complete 2008 monograph or the 2006 *Semiotica* expansion.

## 4. Counter-positions, emotion objection and remove-Brier test

The following table compares **hypotheses** against already-landed R008 preparatory notes; it does **not** pretend to have reread Peirce, Luhmann, de Souza, Harnad or Clark–Brennan primary texts during this R007 tranche.

| Brier-2003 claim | Comparator / relation | What could distinguish the accounts? | Disconfirming / negative case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonliving devices process signals as **quasi-signs** (pp. 81–83). | **Semiotic engineering**: compatible as an interface-authorship distinction, but **not ontologically equivalent**; de Souza can model a designer's delegated message without asserting an intrinsic-life criterion. | Does a Brier-led audit raise any distinct, testable warning-origin or accountability question missed by designer–user metacommunication? | If both derive the same traceable human/institutional authorship, Brier adds at most an ontological framing. |
| Living signification depends on embodied motivation and social coordination (§7 p. 86; §8 p. 88). | **Grounding, pragmatics and HCI/human factors**: broad compatibility; mechanisms for recipient comprehension and repair may already be sufficient. | Does a cybersemiotic audit uncover a neglected contextual interpretation error *after* conventional task, training, social, accessibility and repair checks? | If the standard HCI/grounding audit captures all errors, distinctive applied Brier gain is **not shown**. |
| No single physics/information/semiosis vocabulary captures the entire event (§10 p. 92). | **Formal information / Kolchinsky–Wolpert**: different explanatory target; measured viability of a system is not recipient understanding or first-person experience. **Analogy**, not contradiction of a formal theorem. | A source-defined semantic quantity could be necessary for one task yet insufficient to predict a listener's correct action. | If conventional information and decision models account for all measured outcomes, Brier's vocabulary is **not a new empirical predictor**. |
| Phenosemiotics and intrasemiotics explicitly include feelings and bodily reactions (§8 p. 88). | **Project preference for unperformed machine affect**: no inference to emotional neutrality as superior. **Possible tension** with ignoring listeners' felt urgency or fear. | Compare message understanding, response and adverse affect across controlled non-performed vs expressive prosody, holding task and semantics constant. | Nonhuman/flat voice provokes misinterpretation or anxiety; a carefully expressive cue improves action. Either can happen, and neither is established by Brier's philosophy. |

**Remove-Brier counterfactual (explicit):** A conventional HCI/HF account can state (1) issuer/authority and system state; (2) represented hazard, uncertainty and permitted action; (3) recipient model and acoustic environment; (4) confirmation, missed-notification and repair; and (5) training, safety and affective costs. There is **not yet evidence** that adding the label *cybersemiotics* forces a further design decision or predicts a different listener outcome. Brier's distinct contribution *might* be an audit **organization** that ensures physical, bodily, experiential and social accounts all have independent standing. That is a hypothesis about knowledge organization, not a demonstrated interface benefit. Drop it if a mature conventional audit already meets that coverage.

**Adverse interpretation case (fictional thought experiment, not data):** Two trained operators hear the same correctly encoded alert; one assumes an automated diagnostic, the other a supervisor-approved order. A signal-level decoder succeeds for both, while action authority differs. This illustrates the explanatory gap Brier wants to foreground, but source attribution/repair conventions already predict the same failure; it therefore does **not** independently establish Brier's necessity.

**Criticism of the preferred aesthetic:** An emitter need not perform anthropomorphic emotion for a recipient to experience urgency, comfort, confusion or irritation. The 2003 source itself repeatedly invokes affect, feeling and motivation (**pp. 71, 86, 88**). It neither endorses emotion-free speech nor ranks human-like versus nonhuman alerts. An evaluation that deliberately ignores perceived affect would discard a variable emphasized by the theoretical framework being invoked.
