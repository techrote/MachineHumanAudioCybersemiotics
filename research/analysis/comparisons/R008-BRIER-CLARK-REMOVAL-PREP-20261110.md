## 1. Clarify the competing explananda first

**Brier's question (author claim):** How can scientific accounts of physical and living systems coexist with first-person experience and socially situated meaningful communication without reduction to one explanatory order? His **“Four Views of the Cybersemiotic Star”**, printed p. **224**, and **“The Center of the Star”**, p. **225**, place autopoietic semiotic praxis and intersubjective meaning in an integration of natural, living, cultural and experiential descriptions. His p. **230** expressly opposes exclusive reduction to physics, biology, society or phenomenology.

**Clark and Brennan's question (author claim):** What coordinated evidence and reciprocal activity are sufficient for interlocutors to treat a contribution as understood for the current purpose? The *grounding criterion* is stated at printed p. **129**; the model treats communication as collective, not mere transmission. Their pp. **132–133** discuss relevant-next-turn and continued-attention evidence; pp. **134–135** introduce least collaborative effort and repair; pp. **138–139** show verbal repetition/installments for precise content. Later **“GROUNDING CHANGES WITH THE MEDIUM”** examines costs of acknowledgment, timing, repair and medium differences.

**Key distinction:** Brier's ambitious ontological/epistemological integration and Clark–Brennan's account of conversational coordination are **not equivalent theories**. The latter may nevertheless be a *more economical explanation* of a narrow applied problem: a machine delivers a true alert, but the listener does not understand the intended task or needs a repair path. A literal analogy between a machine emitter and two human conversational partners is not assumed; it is an extrapolation to test.

## 3. Explicit remove-Brier trial (research design, not validation)

**Target use case:** an authorized workshop operator hears “coolant temperature exceeded threshold; stop operation”; the message is objectively correct but masked and misunderstood. Design hypotheses must distinguish *machine truth*, *auditory delivery*, *listener's interpretation*, *action validity* and *evidence of understanding*.

**Baseline without Brier:** represent truthful machine state, delivery evidence, source/addressee identity, repeat/clarify affordance, observable acknowledgment and task-time budget. A Clark–Brennan-style grounding model and conventional alarm/HCI principles already suggest repair and context sensitivity. The 1991 chapter does **not** by itself certify these extensions to one-way/broadcast audio or safety. It provides a conceptual rival.

**Add Brier as a competing research lens:** force explicit recording of the levels of explanation used when evaluating failure: physical cue, organism/attention, first-person experienced urgency, and socially negotiated action context. This may reveal missed variables in the baseline. But the cited 2013 pages supply **no unique testable auditory metric or selection rule**. It remains a candidate **conceptual audit discipline**, not a demonstrated improvement in design.

**Removal criterion:** if an adequately specified conventional model retains every distinct variable, test, objection and remediation decision, record **“no demonstrated applied increment from Brier”**. If Brier identifies a new measurable failure mechanism not derivable from that baseline, predeclare the prediction, distinguish it from relabeling and expose it to adverse outcomes. Do not count a more elegant narrative as evidence of accuracy or listener benefit.

## 4. Counterposition, category mistakes and negative cases

- **Overreach objection to Brier:** his pluralist, nonreductive metaphysics may be defensible in philosophy without being necessary for usability research. A richer framework can increase vocabulary without altering correct-action decisions. Do not attribute this objection to Clark and Brennan; it is the researcher's critique.
- **Overreach objection to grounding:** Clark and Brennan study cooperative human interlocutors; an alarm has asymmetrical source authority, may be broadcast, cannot always know if a specific actor heard or understood, and may need to interrupt before conversational feedback is possible. An uncritical transfer of the grounding criterion to alarms would also overclaim.
- **Embodiment/affect negative case:** “non-performed machine emotion” is a project choice. A neutral voice may still elicit anxiety, or expressive prosody may improve urgency discrimination. Neither project preference nor Brier's discussion is a substitute for outcome evidence. Keep urgency, hazard severity, correctness, task consequence, listener affect and attribution of machine emotion separate.
- **Semiotic classification caution:** do not assign persistent icon/index/symbol labels to arbitrary sound motifs based only on their shape. Evidence about learned mappings and usage context is required; a conventional HCI account may capture the relevant phenomenon without a triadic ontology.
- **Source-gate negative case:** Harnad (1990) is an **abstract-only comparator**; de Souza / semiotic engineering, Peirce's own texts, Luhmann, and empirical warning/prosody sources were **not read for this tranche** and cannot be presented as adjudicated.

## 5. Bounded next-study questions (for eventual R011–R014 planning only)

1. **Repair:** Among listeners with verified task context, does an explicit recipient-initiated repeat/clarify path reduce wrong first actions relative to a one-shot alert at the cost of extra time? Grounding suggests the question, not its answer. Measure correct *first action*, deadline failures and workload, not only preference.
2. **Attribution:** Does separating machine-state certainty from operator-action authorization reduce wrongly inferred permission when a message is overheard? This is an applied hypothesis, not proved by p. 225.
3. **Brier increment:** Does adding the four-perspective audit reveal *previously uncoded, independently verifiable* failure categories after a fully specified conventional review? Blind adjudication would need authorized human participants/experts or else remain an internal synthetic rehearsal; no independent reviewer is asserted here.
4. **Affect:** Is perceived urgency separable from emotion attributed to the machine, and does the answer vary with prosodic design and noise? Require actual stimuli and participant evidence only after gates.

All prospective studies are explicitly **not performed**. No acoustic vocabulary, safety protocol or production requirement is proposed.
