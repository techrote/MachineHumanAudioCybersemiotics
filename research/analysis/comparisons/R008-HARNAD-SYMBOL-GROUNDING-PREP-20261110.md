## 2. The rival theory on its own terms

**Explanandum (Harnad's own problem).** Harnad does not ask whether a listener understands a warning or how scientists should relate disciplinary knowledge. He asks how a formal symbol system could have meaning that is intrinsic to its own capacities rather than solely assigned by an outside interpreter. In §1.2, he narrowly defines a symbol system using tokens, explicit syntax/rules, combinatorial structure and systematic semantic interpretability. The objection in §§2.1–2.2 is that manipulating arbitrarily shaped tokens and consulting more tokens cannot *by itself* make their interpretations intrinsic. His dictionary thought experiment is argumentative, **not experimental evidence**.

**Proposed constructive move.** Harnad separates the ability to *discriminate* similar/different sensory inputs (§3.1) from assigning the correct *category* (§§3.1–3.2). “Iconic” representations in §3.2 are sensory analogues; categorical representations capture invariant features that distinguish a category from confusable alternatives. His example in §3.3 combines already grounded basic names into more complex descriptions. In §4, connectionist pattern learning is a candidate mechanism for learning category invariants; the proposal is deliberately **hybrid**: sensory/categorical grounding plus systematic symbolic composition. The formal-system and behavioral tests in §5 are conditions Harnad **proposes** for interpreting dedicated symbols as intrinsically grounded; he explicitly acknowledges that passing them does **not guarantee subjective meaning**. These are theoretical model claims, not demonstrated results in audio interfaces.

**Internal tension worth preserving.** Harnad's §3.2 says that even sensory icons, by themselves, no more intrinsically *mean* the objects than a camera image does, and §3.3 says isolated category responses are not yet a fully symbolic semantics. His stronger intrinsic-grounding proposal requires a systematic, behaviorally capable integrated system. Therefore: (a) a microphone or sensor does not automatically make a machine's spoken tokens intrinsically meaningful; (b) an acoustically similar “icon” is not automatically a Peircean icon, or evidence that a human associates the intended state with it; (c) behavioral grounding tests still leave the issue of phenomenal experience open (§5). These are restrictions **within** Harnad's own treatment, not objections falsely attributed to him.

**What the argument does not supply.** Sections 1–5 offer neither a listener study nor an acoustic mapping, source certainty vocabulary, urgency metric, recipient-authorization rule, acknowledgment criterion, or safety guarantee. A sensor-driven announcement could be technically correct while remaining unintelligible, misleading or unactionable for a human recipient. Conversely, an external designer can assign words to telemetry and a human can reliably understand them without demonstrating intrinsic semantic content in the emitting program. This distinction is a *researcher inference about engineering scope*, not a Harnad-reported observation.

## 3. Comparison without false equivalence

Brier's **2013 p. 224/Figure 1** maps four interrelated but nonidentical inquiry perspectives: physical nature, living systems, first-person experiential life and socially meaningful cultural practice. On **p. 225**, he assigns embodied sign-using and intersubjective praxis an integrating role; on **p. 230**, he argues against exclusive physicalist *and* radical social-constructivist reduction. He presents a transdisciplinary ontological/epistemological programme. Neither those pages nor Harnad's main argument establish a validated human-audio model.

| Analytic question | Harnad (1990), author HTML | Brier (2013), printed pages | Relationship and research judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What needs explanation?** | How formal tokens might acquire internally grounded referents (§§1.2, 2.1–2.3) | How differently situated scientific and lived perspectives coexist without ontological reduction (224–225, 230) | **Distinct explanatory targets.** An apparent common word, “meaning,” is not a common test variable. |
| **What is the proposed substrate?** | Sensory projections, feature/category learning and compositional tokens (§§3.1–4) | Embodied semiotic praxis, with physical, biological, phenomenological and social inquiry (Fig. 1, 224; 225) | **Partial thematic compatibility**, no demonstrated identity of architecture. Harnad's candidate algorithm cannot be imported as Brier's complete metaphysics. |
| **What counts as a sign or icon?** | “Iconic” is an internal sensory analogue; isolated analogues are not yet semantics (§3.2) | Four-perspective semiotic integration, Peircean orientation (224–225) | **Potential category mistake.** Harnad's technical representation labels are not a warrant to classify arbitrary alarm sounds as fixed Peircean sign types. |
| **How is knowing established?** | Category discrimination/identification, systematic descriptions, formal and behavioral tests (§§3.1, 3.3, 5) | Situated knowledge constituted across observer, embodied experience and social discourse (225) | **Complementary emphases, unresolved translation** from a cognitive architecture to a social knowledge framework. |

| **What would distinguish success?** | A formally systematic and broadly capable system grounded in category discrimination (§5) | An adequate, non-reductive integration of the four knowledge interests (224–225, 230) | **Different evaluation levels**: cognitive performance versus philosophical comprehensiveness. | **Where is a listener's body and culture?** | Inputs, sensorimotor categories and learned invariants matter (§§3–4), but social pragmatics is not central here | Living, experiential and discursive dimensions are integral (224–225) | **Brier may guide scope audits** but could duplicate established ergonomics, accessibility and pragmatics when applied to sound. It must demonstrate a new *consequence*, not just an extra label. |
| **Could neutral or expressive speech be preferred?** | No listening or voice-affect comparison (§§1–5) | No head-to-head neutral/prosodic experiment on cited pages (224–225, 230) | **Neither resolves the project preference** for deliberately non-human audio. Perceived urgency, intelligibility, human-likeness and attributed emotion need independent evidence. |

The three rival senses of “grounding” must remain separately indexed for R008/R011: **referential/sensorimotor grounding** (Harnad), **conversational common-ground/acknowledgment** (Clark and Brennan), and **transdisciplinary ontology/epistemology of meaningful life** (Brier). They can inform the same applied case without being interchangeable, and a schematic “all communication is grounding” does not constitute a theoretical synthesis.

## 4. Countercases and subtraction test

The following are **constructed logical counterexamples**, not observations of machines or people:

- **A. Externally assigned yet intelligible alarm.** A deterministic controller samples a correctly calibrated threshold, a designer maps the event to a spoken phrase, and trained operators repeatedly act appropriately. No test of intrinsic symbol meaning inside the controller has been performed. Its successful *human-facing* semantics cannot be dismissed solely because Harnad's harder cognitive question remains open. **Falsifier:** show a materially better action outcome uniquely requiring an independently tested grounded-machine representation, rather than calibrated input and conventional user-centred validation.
- **B. Grounded controller, misunderstood announcement.** A hypothetical robot genuinely discriminates and identifies environmental categories via embodied sensing, yet its spoken state label is masked or interpreted as permission to act by the wrong operator. Harnad-style category competence would not by itself establish recipient comprehension, addressee authority, or situational validity. **Falsifier:** identify an explicit bridge from the proposed representation to correct human action, then measure it; do not infer that bridge from grounding alone.
- **C. Rich audit with no new decision.** A review team checks physical audibility, biological hearing/attention, perceived affect, workplace roles and communication repair. A cybersemiotic four-perspective diagram organizes the checklist, but an independently specified human-factors/grounding review reaches exactly the same remedial decisions. **Result under subtraction:** Brier has not shown a distinct applied increment. It may remain philosophically interesting.
- **D. Sensor correlation mistaken for user meaning.** A rising-frequency cue tracks a machine measurement monotonically; its mapping is physically non-arbitrary but listeners mislearn which direction is urgent. Neither Harnad's sensor-category grounding nor Brier's layered ontology licenses the prediction of perceptual comprehension from a cue's causal origin. **Required evidence:** real mapping, training, noise and cross-population tests under future authorized protocols.

**Predeclared remove-Brier comparison for R011 planning:** Start with a conventional baseline whose *separate constructs* are source/event truth, state confidence, audio reception, learned cue mapping, participant interpretation, addressee authority, acknowledgment/repair and appropriate action within a deadline. Harnad supplies an optional **machine-referent grounding question**; Clark and Brennan supply a **participant coordination question**; ordinary perception/HCI addresses **human outcomes**. Add Brier's four-perspective analysis as a *predeclared* review method. If it produces no previously omitted causal mechanism, novel variable, correction, or contrary prediction after fair baseline coverage, record **“no demonstrated applied increment from Brier”**. If it does, specify which observable outcome could differ, avoid post hoc relabeling, and retain credible null and adverse outcomes. Philosophical value is not equivalent to empirical incremental predictive value.

A separate **remove-Harnad** thought experiment is equally necessary: a fixed telemetry-to-speech device may be evaluated without supposing its software possesses intrinsic meaning. If Harnad is removed, calibration, intelligibility, receipt, repair and action checks remain; his *cognitive architecture* may add nothing to that narrow evaluation. But for an adaptive robot that learns referential categories and generates claims, misgrounded categories could make the *source truth* wrong. This is a candidate boundary condition, not a result or a proposal to build an autonomous system.

## 5. Judgment, counterarguments and admissibility

**Current bounded judgment:** reading Harnad's main paper beyond its abstract strengthens the **negative distinction** in R004 concept register K04: symbol grounding is not interactional grounding. It also supplies a stronger counterposition against asserting that Brier's theory uniquely teaches the necessity of embodied or non-syntactic meaning. Harnad already provides a specific representational alternative, including clear internal limits. Neither theory is thereby refuted or established, and no audio-design choice is validated.

**Strongest objections to this memo:** (i) a source-text HTML transcription might differ materially from the printed 1990 publication; (ii) treating Brier's philosophical ambition only as a design-variable proposal unfairly narrows his evaluation target; (iii) applying Harnad's *cognitive* symbol grounding to a simple public-warning device may be irrelevant; (iv) the particular Harnad connectionist assumptions are historically situated, not a complete modern account; (v) the accepted R006 selection may exclude, reclassify or reweight these sources.
