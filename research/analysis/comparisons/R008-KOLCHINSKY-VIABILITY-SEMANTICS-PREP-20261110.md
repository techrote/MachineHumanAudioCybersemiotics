## 2. What the rival paper actually proposes

**Explanandum (author claim, §1.1–1.2, printed pp. 1–3):** Statistical correlations are not automatically meaningful. Kolchinsky and Wolpert propose an operational kind of meaning: those system–environment correlations whose causal preservation matters to the system's *continued existence*, under a specified dynamical model. This is not a definition of what a word means to a human interlocutor.

**Premises and construction (author claims, §§1.2, 4, 5.1.1, 5.2):**

- Let `X` be the system, `Y` its environment, `τ` a time horizon, and `V` a viability functional. Their main example takes `V(p(Xτ)) = −H(p(Xτ))` (**equation 4.1, printed p. 6**). *Low entropy is not equivalent to organism survival*: §4 explicitly notes that a low-entropy distribution may assign little mass to an actual viable set and may fail to preserve system identity. The authors discuss alternative viability functions.
- For *stored* information, compare the actual joint initial distribution with a counterfactual fully scrambled product distribution; the viability-value difference is **equation 5.3**. Intermediate coarse-grainings of the environment erase irrelevant correlations while retaining only enough syntactic information to reproduce actual viability (**equations 5.5 and the viability-optimal intervention in §5.1.1**). Thus total mutual information and their *semantic amount* are distinct.
- *Observed* information is instead defined by interventions on ongoing information flow, using transfer entropy, with a corresponding optimal intervention (**§5.2, equations 5.17–5.19**). Stored and observed information must not be conflated. Neither is defined as listener-rated usefulness or the Peircean interpretant.
- The paper expressly says that `V` is **chosen exogenously by the analyst** (**§4, printed p. 6**) despite its aspiration to an intrinsic dynamics-grounded definition. The authors defend this choice relative to externally specified utilities and discuss partially more objective selection in §6. This is a *real methodological qualification*, not grounds to declare the theory incoherent.
- The author's modeled food-seeking system (§5.1.4; appendix B) distinguishes about **2.32 bits** of initial mutual information from about **1.37 bits** of stored semantic information. The **food-avoiding** variant still has about **1.37 bits** of semantic information while the *value of information* is approximately **−13.7 bits** (appendix B, discussion of figs. 3–4). These are **reported outputs from a constructed model**, not participant outcomes, direct measurements of auditory meaning or an independently reproduced computation. A **negative value of information does not imply a negative number of semantic bits**.

**Strong charitable reading (our interpretation):** The intervention test answers a useful causal question missed by bare bit counts: which distinctions have *consequences for a stipulated self-maintenance criterion*? It can formalize harmful information use and distinguish correlation from task relevance. Its carefully limited mathematical targets deserve comparison on their own terms.

**Strong criticism (our inference, anchored to the authors' §4 caveat):** The definition remains conditional on boundary choice, horizon, model, intervention family and the analyst's viability functional. A model might operationalize “the listener interpreted a warning correctly” only by importing task/action criteria not contained in the original viability functional. Without those additions, “semantic bits” do not establish the cultural, normative or conversational content of an audio message. The criticism is **ours**, not an author admission about auditory interfaces.

## 4. Hard negative cases and explicit remove-Brier test

These are **hypothetical analytical counterexamples only**. No sounds were generated, no listeners tested, no hazard policy evaluated.

| Case | Conventional rival model without Brier | What Brier could add (hypothesis, not observed gain) | Adverse result / revision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correct high-information warning, wrong action.** An accurate alert carries many state bits but the operator misconstrues its required action. | State-truth validation + recognition test + authorized-action semantics + repeat/repair mechanism. K–W-style intervention could identify bits relevant to a *specified* viable outcome, but requires importing the correct-action definition. | Require explicit separation of physical state, lived interpretation and social authorization before declaring communicative success. | If a mature HCI/grounding model already captures all failure categories and corrective actions, assign **no unique applied increment** to Brier. |
| **Hazardous yet information-rich cue.** The receiver systematically acts away from safety after a correctly encoded alert. | Compare action outcomes under preserved vs scrambled cues; distinguish causal relevance from positive outcome; K–W's negative viability-value example is an analogy, *not* evidence for human behavior. | Insist on documenting the observer's and community's interpretation of what counts as an acceptable outcome. | If a task-contextual action-loss model performs identically, Brier adds at most organizing vocabulary. |
| **Neutral speech triggers affect anyway.** Identical state meaning is conveyed with prosody varying attributed emotion and urgency. | Separate lexical accuracy, urgency calibration, correct action, machine-emotion attribution and listener affect (protocol outcomes O2/O3/O9/O12). | Prevent treating subjective reception as irrelevant to correctness, *without* assuming expressive speech necessarily wins. | If affect/recognition effects are fully explained by conventional psychophysics, no unique semiotic prediction follows. Neither theory supplies an effect estimate. |
| **Boundary instability.** The analytical system is defined as speaker device, then receiver/operator, then operator plus workplace; identical emitted bits acquire different viability relevance. | Declare `X/Y` boundary, `τ`, intervention, harm/correct-action criterion and sensitivity; test robustness. | A pluralist audit could make boundary choice explicit. | If the formal sensitivity audit already reveals every consequential omission, Brier is redundant for that use case. |

**Remove-Brier verdict for this bounded comparison:** K–W offers a *more formal account of some causal-information questions*; Clark/HCI offers a *closer account of an interpreted task message*. These jointly cover much of the proposed interface problem without a demonstrated need for Brier's ontology. The Brier star may remain useful as a **multi-perspective audit checklist**, but no extra quantified prediction or actionable design rule has been shown here. A negative finding about **unique applied value** does not refute the general philosophical project.

**Remove-K–W counterfactual:** Grounding/HCI can specify truth, action and repair without quantifying self-maintenance-relevant bits. Unless adding K–W changes a falsifiable prediction or identifies a consequential omitted correlation under an explicitly justified `V` and `τ`, do not treat its mathematical apparatus as a required interface component.
