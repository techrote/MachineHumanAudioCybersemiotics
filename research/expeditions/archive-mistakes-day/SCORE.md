# An Attempt at Filing a Room — original performance score

For one sound performer, one archivist and one listener (roles may be shared). All instructions are original; this is not a reconstruction of Perec or Neuhaus.

**Carrier:** twenty equal pulses. Make twelve pulses of low sustained hum, then tap / silence / tap / silence / tap / silence, then two pulses of chime. Prefer quiet comfortable sound; the silent slots are deliberately observed rests. Keep this carrier for every movement.

**I. Clock.** Number the twenty pulses. Choose three distinct numbers uniformly, arrange in original time order and replay one pulse from each. Mark the gaps in between as omitted material, not as additional rests. Perform this selection again.

**II. Diary.** Draw eight boxes around the episodes, including the three one-pulse rests. Select three distinct boxes uniformly. For the hum and chime boxes, choose one pulse uniformly within the selected box. Render three equal-duration excerpts in chronological order. Do not imply that this is a time sample.

**III. The clerk's new rule.** Without altering the carrier, divide the hum into twelve boxes. Repeat the diary. The clerk has transformed the distribution of possible miniatures without playing a note.

**IV. Cabinet.** Write the four category names on cards. Draw three without replacement. Select a pulse within each category uniformly and replay in the drawn order. This is a category anthology, not a temporal portrait.

**V. An unrecorded room.** Keep the carrier playing but set aside the catalogue. Ask the listener whether they prefer another miniature, the whole carrier, or no further performance. Respect the answer. There is no requirement to report what was heard.

**VI. The museum's plaque.** Display three labels: “sampled duration,” “sampled episodes,” and “sampled categories.” The archivist may rename the work but may not interchange the labels. Do not call the three miniatures an empirical demonstration of perception.

### Coda (fiction)

The museum had a cupboard full of Tuesday afternoons. One contained a long argument, another a kettle that hummed until evening. The keeper counted the arguments and found the kettle almost absent. She counted the minutes and found the argument almost absent. A third clerk proposed one shelf for each kind of thing.

At the opening a visitor asked which cupboard contained Tuesday. The keeper handed her the catalogue of cupboards. Outside, the kettle was still humming.
