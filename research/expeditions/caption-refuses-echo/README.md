# The Caption That Refuses to Be an Echo

26 November 2026 · Exploratory humanities and original artistic research · [#727](https://github.com/techrote/MachineHumanAudioCybersemiotics/issues/727) · Draft/HOLD

**Question:** Can a work have several equally authoritative sensory compositions, while keeping the information necessary to participate independently available? This is a proposal about the design of a score, not a claim that sensory experiences can be made identical.

## The theatre with three manuscripts

A theatre commissions an electronic piece and asks a writer to describe it. The writer returns a second piece, consisting of eight little scenes about a room. The composer asks for descriptions of the notes instead. The writer supplies those on a separate sheet.

Now there are three manuscripts: the sound, the poem, and the description of the sound. The manager insists that the poem belongs beneath the sound. The writer asks whether the sound might instead be the accompaniment to the poem.

A fourth document contains the four cues which the actors actually require. The stage manager does not permit those to be ambiguous.

This fictional encounter distinguishes three relationships: translation, independent invention, and participation in a common action. The same material can be used in each relationship, but its authority changes. A caption can be a description without being exhausted by description. A poem can be a component of a musical work without being a transcription of music.

## Interpretation: an artwork is not an original plus accommodations

The usual engineering language of encoding, channel and decoding may already make the sound into the original and the text into its degraded reflection. Yet a writer can make a verbal object that has no sound-only inverse. A composer can write an oscillator part which is not the phonetic reading of the text. Neither need be the defective version of the other.

O'Daniel's account suggests an especially interesting inversion: a description can originate in the body of the describer rather than in an acoustic measurement. It can also produce a new narrative. Lazard's layering supplies a different inversion, making the apparatus of mediation itself a compositional medium. Kim's account of notation asks who establishes the conventions by which these materials are admitted into culture. None of these positions is reducible to a channel-capacity calculation.

I propose **parallel authority** as a provisional design term: artists may edit independent expressive layers, and no one layer is presumed to contain the entire work. This does not mean that an inaccurate caption is an equally valid account of a spoken sentence. It means the work may explicitly contain both faithful descriptive access material and a second, independently authored text. Both can be visible without confusing their claim classes.

There is a further political problem. The artist who controls the channel also controls who can enter the work, who has to wait, and who is asked to perform extra interpretive labour. It would be easy for an engineering project to turn this into an attractive metaphor for dropped packets and forget that people already live with material barriers. Do not equate Deafness, blindness or any human body with a failed communication channel. The erasure operation below removes a digital representation, not a sense or a person. A meaningful future design process must involve the people whose access it purports to support, subject to the programme's human gates. Neither these notes nor the synthetic artifact can stand in for them.

The creative freedom to make asymmetric experiences also does not establish an entitlement to withhold an urgent instruction. The theatre is free to make the narrative mysterious. Its emergency exit must remain intelligible through appropriate alternatives. The artistic and operational requirements should therefore be separate, and the latter should be tested before any proposed use.

## Finite construction: common core, independent surplus

Let the event be (c,a,t), with four cue states c, two sound variants a, and two textual variants t. An authored audio symbol carries (c,a); an authored text symbol carries (c,t). Each view determines c by construction. Neither view determines the other expressive variable. Each valid common core therefore admits four joint performances, rather than requiring one unique translation.

Under a uniform distribution over the 16 events, each view has 8 symbols, each symbol has 2 compatible joint events, and the complete joint event has 4 bits. Each view contains 3 bits; the 2 shared bits are c. The conditional entropy of either expressive variable given the other view is 1 bit. These are properties of a stipulated uniform symbolic code, not measurements of information in an aesthetic experience. At length n, each single view has 2^n compatible complete scores. An audio-only view has full cue coverage yet lacks the independent poem; the inverse applies to artistic text alone.

The distinction between redundancy and equivalence is useful here: duplicating the common core does not require duplicating the work. By contrast, a split payload (e.g. send only one of two cue bits per channel) cannot recover the cue from either single view. A literal one-to-one translation ties the artistic variables together and reduces the possible joint score to 8 events. Neither alternative is inherently wrong for art, but a split critical payload would be inappropriate when either channel must be independently sufficient.

The reconciliation rule reports CONFLICT when two supplied core symbols differ and ABSENT when both are missing. It cannot detect a shared incorrect core, nor tell which disagreeing source is correct. For a real interface, add explicit event IDs, version, timing, provenance and an independently justified safety policy. This toy assumes alignment, reliable availability flags and a known vocabulary; it provides no authentication, no timing recovery, no error-correcting channel and no safety assurance.

## Original performance: A room in four editions

The score lasts four seconds. Each half-second tile has the same amplitude envelope and a selected fundamental (180, 240, 300 or 360 Hz); the second timbre adds the third harmonic at half the fundamental's coefficient. These are arbitrary musical conventions. The cue words ENTER, WAIT, TURN, EXIT belong to a fictional stage, not a real machine. They are also available as text.

| Cell | Cue | Poem A | Poem B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ENTER | A room acquires a hinge. | A room acquires a hinge. |
| 2 | WAIT | The clock declines its invitation. | A chair keeps an appointment. |
| 3 | TURN | The corridor changes grammar. | The window rehearses a corner. |
| 4 | WAIT | The clock declines its invitation. | A chair keeps an appointment. |
| 5 | EXIT | An address outlives its occupant. | An address outlives its occupant. |
| 6 | ENTER | A room acquires a hinge. | The threshold lends its name. |
| 7 | TURN | The window rehearses a corner. | The corridor changes grammar. |
| 8 | EXIT | The key leaves its shadow. | An address outlives its occupant. |

Four editions: **Room** (sound A, poem A), **Other Caption** (sound A, poem B), **Other Timbre** (sound B, poem A), **Other Both** (sound B, poem B). Read the poems at your own pace before listening, or read independently without audio. The generated .art.vtt files are timed compositional notation, not validated readable captions: 500 ms is too brief for ordinary reading of the sentences. The separate .description.vtt track gives exact authored frequency and harmonic content, not a full human-accessible audio description. The static score is available for untimed reading.

A performance instruction: two performers independently read the poems; a third performs the oscillator part. On each cue, the stage actor may make an appropriate fictional gesture. Reverse the institutional hierarchy: begin by reading the poem, then let the sound be its annotation. Allow a performance with no audio at all. The work is not a simulation of anyone's sensory experience.

## Executed software checks and limits

From this directory, use Python 3.11+ without -O: `python laboratory.py --out /tmp/mhac-caption-score`. The script emits four original mono WAVs (16 kHz, 16-bit, 64,000 frames), eight VTT files and a JSON digest receipt. Python 3.13.5 execution on 2026-11-26 passed exhaustive enumeration of 16 events, all 64 pairs of channel symbols (16 agree, 48 conflict), all 4,096 length-three joint scores and both single-view fibers (512 views each, exactly 8 compatible joint scores per view). Both single views recover the symbolic core in every enumerated case. Negative controls demonstrate common-mode error and absent inputs. All eight clean PCM tile types passed a known-grid frequency-projection decoder. WAV format, sample payloads, amplitude bounds, hash relationships and identical rerendered outputs passed. No noise, misalignment, channel drift, caption parser, browser playback, assistive technology, tactile hardware, perceptual or accessibility qualification occurred. The native source blob was 85b0483d40b4677c10a97cddd2c912ec5fb417c4; the local Git blob matched. Single assistant authored and checked the model and source notes, not an independent reviewer. Full repository CI remains a separate gate.

The useful digital equalities are: Room and Other Caption have byte-identical WAVs; Room and Other Timbre have identical art VTTs but different WAVs and technical descriptions. Thus the artistic text can stay fixed while the sound changes, and vice versa. This is deliberately unsurprising as coding theory; the artistic implication is the question, not mathematical novelty.

## Instrument directions and counterarguments

**Parallel Authority — VSTi:** a sequencer with common cue IDs and independent synthesis and text-event lanes. Each has its own clocked editing, mute/solo and export; a cue ID links them but never reconstructs one from the other. Keep descriptive metadata derived from actual DSP parameters as a third, clearly named lane. Text export must survive DAW state restoration. An optional text-driven modulation patch may link the expressive lanes, but this is an authored coupling rather than an inherent correspondence.

**Countercaption — CYD:** a 320 x 240 panel with a persistent cue label, independent sound and text editors, and an untimed text review page. Do not require color, waveform position, touch or sound alone for an action. The design is only a concept; no hardware or screen-reader capability is assumed. A VST host editor is not automatically accessible because it has visible words.

**Third manuscript — future tactile lab:** an opt-in tactile part with independent patterns, documented physical constraints, and no claim that a given vibration recreates pitch or emotion. It could be composed first, with a visual score and audio later. Begin with a declared task and hardware qualification; no physical vibration is rendered in this expedition.

A counterargument: separating independent artistic surplus from necessary shared information may reintroduce the very hierarchy these artists contest. Some works require their different layers to be irreducibly entangled. An artist must be able to write such a work, including a piece in which no two audiences encounter the same narrative. The proposed architecture is only one possible option; it is not a definition of access, art or the Deaf experience. Conversely, declaring all layers independent can abandon a person who wants a faithful account of the sound. The description track must not be silently replaced by poetry. The controls to request additional description, change pace, mute, pause and review should remain in the audience's hands.

**MHAC hypothesis:** for a well-specified non-safety-critical task, independently complete representations of operational content may preserve action accuracy when one presentation is unavailable, while distinct expressive layers remain available for voluntary exploration. Compare plain text alone, redundant core, split core, and redundant core plus optional artistic material. Test cue identification, confidence, wrong actions, abstention, time, workload, and voluntary preference separately; counterbalance training and include mismatched/stale cues. Human study, accessible co-design and authorization remain outstanding. A plain text interface could be sufficient and preferable. No acoustic advantage or universal accessibility is established.

## Speculative coda — The programme note

The composer asked the writer to describe what the room sounded like.

The writer described the room before the instrument arrived.

The composer said that this was impossible to hear.

The writer turned the page.

The technician printed the four cues on both manuscripts.

For the first time, the actor could enter without waiting for the orchestra.
