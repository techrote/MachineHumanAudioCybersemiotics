This is **not a claim to have critically read the complete article**. No paper PDF or figures were copied into the repository.

- **What Lamport actually supplies (p. 559, “The Partial Ordering”):** within-process precedence, sent/received messages and transitivity define a *happened-before* relation; two events with no such relation either way are concurrent in that formal model. Acknowledges that physical-time order may differ from process knowledge. These are Lamport's *distributed-system* premises, not an auditory-interface experiment.
- **Boundary in Lamport himself (p. 559):** the physical possibility of communication versus messages actually sent differs; our finite DAG makes an additional explicit assumption that its graph is an adequate representation of the dependencies in the invented trace. An incomplete real log is weaker still.
- **Historical negative control (p. 562, “Anomalous Behavior”):** Lamport already describes a discrepancy between system serialization and a user's externally known order, and gives routes to inject ordering information. **Do not claim X003 discovers the general anomaly.** The original contribution here is a small bounded *audio counterpoint* presentation and machine-checkable contrast with a declared graph.

**Inferences independently constructed for this expedition:** the linear-extension reversal proof, five-node fictional fixture, finite enumeration, stereo PCM generator and suggested epistemic mapping of unresolved chronology to a conflicting stereo pair. The elementary proof is not attributed to Lamport as a novel theorem of his; it is an ordinary consequence of DAG topology.

**No empirical references or claims:** We did not search for or read controlled studies of whether sound-order implies perceived causal order. There is no warrant for a psychological effect size, audibility under masking, action improvement or human safety benefit. Even two audibly different tracks may be undecodable, distracting or misleading. An audio perception hypothesis would require separate sound calibration, preregistered outcomes, participant consent, ethics/access assessment and comparisons against explicit verbal descriptions.
