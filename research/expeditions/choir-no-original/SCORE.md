# Four Copies of a Missing Song
Original electronic performance score — unperformed by humans.

Four performers or synth voices, one shared clock, six quarter-second slots per loop.
Pitch offsets from A3: 0, 4, 7, 2, 5, 0 semitones. Reading 1 raises the local pitch by two semitones; reading 0 leaves it unchanged.
For exact acoustic control use the laboratory's reset-phase slot waveforms with identical timbre, timing and gain, and integer addition. The live score may use other timbres but then no claim of sample identity is made.

## I. The single branch
A and B: 111000. C and D: 000111.
Play as one mono sum, then expose all four individual lines sequentially. Leave an empty stand for a hypothetical base voice. Do not play the base as if it were an actual recovered ancestor.

## II. The crossings
A: 111000; B: 100011; C: 010101; D: 001110.
Repeat the mono presentation, then expose the individual lines. Keep the same tempo and gain. There are two instances of each reading at every moment and three raised notes for each performer. The line changes are the material, not a difference in the sum.

## III. Two histories
A silent reader shows either "borrowed a reading" or "invented it again" while the musicians repeat movement II. Switch labels without changing sound. These labels are fictional possible histories, not conclusions extracted from the recording.

## IV. The editor joins
Using three new witnesses 110, 101, 011, play a fourth voice 111 as a conjectural edition. Introduce it as an added part rather than a recovered witness. The musicians may prefer the new line; this preference is not a provenance claim.

## V. Refusal of the apparatus
Any musician may stretch a note across the next boundary or insert a fragment that does not align to a site. The score inspector must mark the resulting passage outside the binary model, not assign it a false reading. End by allowing the four parts to leave the common clock independently.

The score is not a reenactment of an oral musical tradition or a real manuscript history.
