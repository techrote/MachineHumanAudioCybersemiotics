## 1. Model

Four phase-locked unit oscillators sit on the unit circle at angles (0, pi/2, pi, 3pi/2). Reflect all four in the horizontal axis. Every pairwise ordinary interference intensity stays exactly the same, including pairs that are not neighbors. Yet the direction of every nonzero quadrature changes.

Let z_i=exp(i theta_i), for n>=2 named active unit phasors, with unknown common phase. The state is static throughout measurement. An ordinary pair probe is

    C_ij = cos(theta_j-theta_i) = (|z_i+z_j|^2-2)/2.

An oriented probe is

    S_ij = sin(theta_j-theta_i) = (|z_i-i z_j|^2-2)/2.

The latter needs a known quadrature shift and a declared order of endpoints; it is not obtained by merely repeating the former. Rotating every phasor by a common angle is unobservable and is quotiented out. Reflection (complex conjugation) is initially retained as a distinct state. When discussing *shape*, quotient out reflection too. Individual powers are known; no unknown amplitudes, time variation, arbitrary multi-coordinate projections, external phase reference, or real participant data enter the model.

This differs from #372 (real coefficients and absent vertices). Here every vertex is present, but a measurement can still discard the direction of the relation. Singer's angular synchronization model retains the signed angular offsets, not merely cosines; its noiseless connected-graph theorem therefore does not directly apply.

## 2. Generic fibers

Let G be a connected simple graph with n>=2. Define b as its number of blocks containing edges, counting each bridge as a block. Equivalently, group edges under the equivalence relation generated by lying on a common simple cycle. For a fixed configuration, each observed cosine restricts an oriented phase difference to its actual value or its negative modulo 2pi.

**Theorem A.** For almost every configuration, the number of compatible configurations modulo rotation is exactly 2^b, or 2^(b-1) modulo rotation and reflection. Exactly b signed probes, one measured edge from each block, suffice and are necessary to identify the oriented configuration.

Proof. Exclude the finite set of angle-relation hyperplanes modulo 2pi relevant to graph cycles: (i) any edge offset equal to 0 or pi; (ii) any signed sum of the offsets around a simple cycle using signs not all equal that is zero modulo 2pi. Each excluded condition is nontrivial. In (ii), expressing the signed sum in vertex angles gives coefficients given by differences of successive signs, with at least one nonzero coefficient. Thus the exclusions have measure zero. This is a sufficient genericity condition, not a claim that every excluded state is ambiguous.

A candidate has an edge sign choice on each edge. The cycle sum must vanish, so on a simple cycle all choices must agree. Consequently choices are constant on each block. Conversely any assignment of a sign to each block yields zero sums on every simple cycle, hence on every closed walk (decompose into simple cycles and canceled reverse traversals). Integrate the prescribed offsets along a spanning tree with theta_0=0. Every edge matches, and distinct sign assignments give distinct rooted solutions since no edge is self-negative. Global reflection negates all b choices and has no fixed point under the genericity condition. This proves both cardinalities. A signed edge identifies exactly the sign of its block; b edges suffice. Fewer leave one block unknown even with adaptive selection among G's edges. The last necessity statement is specifically for probes on G: a new pair between different blocks may convey more than one bit of block-choice information.

A tree therefore has 2^(n-1) oriented solutions and 2^(n-2) shapes generically. A simple cycle on n>=3 has exactly two orientations of one shape. Generic shape identification needs at least n edges for n>=3, since a connected n-1-edge graph is a tree; an n-cycle attains the lower bound. A disconnected graph permits independent continuous rotations. At n=2 a single cosine suffices for shape.

Established ancestry: Connelly and Whiteley's spherical transfer of generic global rigidity (2009 manuscript, Theorem 12) places the generic circle-shape condition within rigidity theory. The block count above is a direct finite-fiber derivation specialized to this measurement model; no priority claim is made.

## 3. Worst-case identification

**Negative result: even a cycle can fail.** In fifth-turn units, use the four-cycle with edges 02,03,12,13. The rooted states (0,0,1,1) and (0,2,1,1) have equal cosine on every edge; they are neither rotations nor reflections of one another. The exact grid census finds six rooted solutions, hence three shapes. Every oscillator remains active. The ambiguity is not a numerical failure; it comes from coincident angles at different labeled vertices.

**Theorem B.** A fixed cosine-only graph determines shape for *every* configuration iff it is complete.

Proof. With all cosine probes, the Gram matrix of the real two-dimensional unit vectors is known. Any two vector systems with equal Gram matrix are related by an isometry on their spans, extendable to an orthogonal transformation of the plane. In rank two, these are exactly rotations and reflections; rank one admits the same conclusion. Conversely let uv be any missing edge. Give all other vertices angle zero, u angle alpha and v angle beta. Compare to a state replacing beta by -beta. Every recorded cosine is unchanged. Choose alpha=beta=pi/3, so the two worlds disagree on the missing cosine (1 versus -1/2) and cannot have the same shape. This covers n>=3; at n=2 with no edge the result is immediate. Therefore generic O(n) cosine sufficiency coexists with quadratic fixed worst-case cost n(n-1)/2.

A two-stage adaptive plan gives a useful contrast (upper bound, not claimed optimal). Query all n-1 cosines to root 0. If all angles are 0 or pi relative to the root, the state is already oriented up to rotation. Otherwise choose a second anchor whose cosine to root is strictly between -1 and 1. Set its relative sine positive as a provisional choice of reflection. Query its cosines to the n-2 other non-root vertices. The root cosine determines x_i; the second anchor dot product determines y_i by a single linear equation with nonzero coefficient. This needs at most 2n-3 cosine probes and one signed anchor measurement if orientation is required. Refusal: the proof supplies no universal numerical conditioning guarantee when both anchors are nearly collinear.

For full cosine data and noncollinear vectors, the only remaining ambiguity is a common reflection: one informative signed probe resolves it. For collinear vectors reflection does not create a distinct rooted state, so zero suffice.

## 4. Orientation under corruption

Assume complete and exact cosine data; all uncertainty now lies in the two possible orientations of a rank-two shape. Choose a fixed signed-probe graph H. A sine vanishes precisely when its endpoint angles agree modulo pi. Thus the informative edges cross between classes of the partition of vertices by angle modulo pi. The Hamming distance of the two oriented transcripts is the number of informative edges.

**Theorem C.** The minimum such distance over all rank-two shapes is the minimum edge-cut size lambda(H), including zero for disconnected H. Proof: any nontrivial partition has at least as many crossing edges as the cut separating one of its classes, hence at least lambda(H). Conversely realize a minimum cut using just two projective angle classes, for example 0 and pi/2. Every cut edge is informative and every other edge is not.

It follows that fixed signed probes identify orientation uniformly iff H is connected. A spanning tree gives the sharp n-1-probe cost. Against t arbitrary erroneous signed values and e known erasures, uniform exact recovery is possible iff lambda(H)>2t+e. For necessity take the minimum-cut configuration, erase up to e informative edges and split the remaining at most 2t disagreements between the two putative worlds' error budgets. For sufficiency use the standard disjoint Hamming-ball argument on the two predictions. No distributional error assumptions are needed.

If full exact cosine data are available before selecting signed probes, informative edges can be identified without learning their signs: |S_ij|=sqrt(1-C_ij^2). There are at least n-1 informative distinct pairs in every rank-two shape, attained for a partition of sizes 1 and n-1. Hence for 1<=d<=n-1, selecting d informative pairs gives distance d at cost d; fewer than d probes cannot give distance d in this two-stage model. Taking d=1, fixed worst-case n-1 contrasts with adaptive one. Repeated probes and unrestricted sequential adaptive corruption strategies are not included.

## 5. The geometry of noise

Keep the complete cosine data exact. On a signed edge allow additive error of magnitude eta. Its two predictions are +S and -S. Their closed error intervals are disjoint iff |S|>eta. Consequently an orientation can be guaranteed from a single honest signed probe on H iff m_H>eta, where m_H is max over H of |sin(theta_j-theta_i)|. If every available edge is weak, the common zero transcript is compatible with both orientations even when all signed edges are measured.

Let M denote the maximum |sin| over all pairs. The best complete-availability adaptive signed probe attains M. Assume H is connected and has graph diameter D.

**Theorem D (sharp conditioning frontier).** For any shape,

    m_H >= sin(arcsin(M)/D).

Proof: let rho(a,b)=min over integers k of |a-b-k*pi|, taking values in [0,pi/2]. This is the geodesic metric on angles modulo pi, and |sin(a-b)|=sin(rho(a,b)). Take endpoints achieving M; their shortest path has at most D edges. The triangle inequality gives arcsin(M)<=D arcsin(m_H), and sine is increasing on this interval. For equality, choose a vertex u of eccentricity D and set theta_i=dist_H(u,i)*arcsin(M)/D. All differences are between 0 and arcsin(M), and every graph edge changes distance by at most one. Some edge changes it by exactly one, and a farthest vertex achieves the global maximum. This attains equality for every M in (0,1], on every connected H. The collinear M=0 case has no orientation ambiguity.

In particular the exact infimum of m_H/M for M>0 is 1/D. The lower bound follows also from sin(x/D)>=sin(x)/D on [0,pi/2] by concavity. The infimum is approached as M tends to zero. For D>1 and M>0 the ratio is strictly larger than 1/D. The nonlinear frontier is stronger than the ratio bound when M is large.

This produces a graph-design distinction which noiseless identifiability cannot reveal. Among n-1-edge connected graphs on n>=3 vertices, the star has diameter two and is optimal for worst-case relative orientation margin (1/2). The path has diameter n-1 and worst-case relative margin 1/(n-1). At n=64 and M=1 the exact sharp signed margin for a path is sin(pi/126), about 0.02493, whereas a star guarantees at least sin(pi/4), about 0.7071, and adaptive complete availability gives 1. The star is not the same as the amplitude-aware star in #372: here the criterion is projective angular geometry with unit magnitudes, not amplitude products.

There is no strictly positive uniform absolute orientation margin even for the complete graph: choose a noncollinear shape arbitrarily close to collinearity. Nor does exact cosine recovery guarantee robust shape estimation from noisy cosine data. The theorem conditions on exact cosine data; using it to certify a physically noisy two-quadrature instrument without another analysis would be invalid.

## 6. A witness and an instrument

The native Python generator produces four original mono 8 kHz 16-bit PCM files with one 100 ms slot per observed value. A 250 Hz square wave uses amplitude 1200*(value+2) for cosine or signed sine values in {-1,0,1}. There is no normalization. The pair order is lexicographic. The worlds have phase vectors (0,pi/2,pi,3pi/2) and its conjugate. Both ordinary observation sequences are (0,-1,0,0,-1,0); their extra oriented observations are +1 and -1 respectively.

**Design hypothesis: the Looking-glass Organ.** Let a performer control a bank of phase-locked voices, a cosine observation graph and a separate quadrature patch. Render the ordinary relational score without revealing handedness. A second performer can reflect the latent chord or individual generic blocks, leaving the score untouched. A third may purchase one orientation witness or rearrange the graph to improve its signed margin. The compositional material is the difference between invariant shape and directed relation, not a promise about listeners' interpretation.

The bridge to semiotics is conditional: a sign system can preserve similarity or symmetric relation while discarding which way a relation points. For telemetry, retain the relation type, ordered endpoints, orientation witness provenance, and applicable uncertainty rather than infer direction from a symmetric statistic. Whether this design improves communication would need separate controlled evidence. A symmetric measure may be entirely sufficient when direction is deliberately irrelevant. That is not a failure.

**Fiction, explicitly:** A technician plays two chords through a hall whose meters all agree. The organist insists that the second chord has turned inside out. The technician attaches a new meter between two pipes; the new display changes sign. The old meters were not broken. They had never been asked to distinguish the two hands.
