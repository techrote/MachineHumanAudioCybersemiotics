# Four Spellings for a Single Room
Original unperformed text score, 2026-11-25. For four sustained electronic voices and an optional fifth performer reading labels. All pitch classes relative to a chosen base; frequency for step p in N divisions is f0*2^(p/N). Choose comfortable volume and octave placements; no audience response is required.

## I. The four doors

Use twelve equal octave divisions. Sustain pitch classes 0, 3, 6, 9. The reader presents root labels r=0,1,2,3 in that order, one per four beats. Do not change voicing, phase or amplitude when the label changes. The label is an instruction about possible interpretation, not a claim that a listener hears the corresponding root. If desired, end on one of four separately composed continuations.

## II. The translator's seam

Use nineteen divisions, initial pitches 0,5,10,15. At each successive label replace the pitch belonging to the previous root by one step higher, holding the other three voices. Use a slow glide or discrete rearticulation, chosen in advance:
0,5,10,15 -> 1,5,10,15 -> 1,6,10,15 -> 1,6,11,15 -> 1,6,11,16.
The fifth performer is silent during the glides. At the fourth replacement, speak the first label again. The word has returned; the collection has moved. Repeat using thirty-one divisions and initial pitches 0,8,16,24 (replace 0 with 1, then 8 with 9, then 16 with 17, then 24 with 25). Do not compensate the final displacement.

## III. The unfaithful translation

Choose one of the following: retain the twelve-division equality in the new medium by detuning some target intervals; retain the target interval spellings at the expense of coincidence; or construct a different symmetric collection, with a different interval size, whose four labels again share a pitch set. Name the chosen fidelity only after the sound. The other performers may decline to prefer it. In twenty-four divisions, use 0,6,12,18 as a negative control: an increase in pitch resolution has not broken the fourfold symmetry.

## IV. The joke that becomes a chord

Write a pair of short spoken phrases with a common sound but different continuations. Perform only the shared sound. Let the four oscillator voices supply continuations unrelated to the words. Translate neither the words nor the sounds: translate the opportunity to continue in more than one way. End with a sound that has no assigned continuation. This movement is speculative artistic research, not a decoding exercise.

## Afterword — The translator's invoice (fiction)

The conservatory sent its four doors abroad. The customs officer found only one in the crate.

The translator was asked whether she had lost three. She showed him four drawings, all laid exactly over one another. He insisted that the doors be counted separately, and had a new ruler made.

When the doors arrived, their hinges had shifted by a little. Each opened onto a different corridor. The conservatory complained that the translator had changed the building.

She replied that she could either repair the coincidence or preserve the hinges. The architect asked her to invent a fifth door. The customs officer stamped the invoice: FOUR DOORS, ONE LOSS.

The concert programme listed the loss as a performer.
