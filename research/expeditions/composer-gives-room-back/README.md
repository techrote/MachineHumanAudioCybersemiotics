# The Composer Who Gives the Room Back

**Status:** original exploratory humanities and artistic research, 2026-11-24. Refs #342. Not screened Track E evidence, accepted design, or empirical listening study.

## A composition with a return address

An electronic musician moves into a railway waiting room. She introduces a tone which resembles the ventilation system just enough to become part of the room's apparent machinery. Six minutes later she turns off her amplifier. A passenger hears the ventilation system for the first time that morning. The machine has not become louder.

This is a constructed scene, not a reported encounter. The musician has added a sound, temporarily modified an encounter with other sounds, and then withdrawn her addition. None of those acts is identical to making silence. Suppose the passenger asks for a recording of the work. The recording may preserve the amplifier's output, the waiting room, or the two together. None necessarily preserves the passenger's change of attention.

Call this **acoustic restitution**, in a narrow artistic sense: relinquishing one's own contribution to a sound field without asserting a right to control the remainder. It is not a legal term here. The premise of restitution is itself suspect: the room was never empty, and the composer may never have had title to its sonic material. Returning it does not undo every consequence of occupying it.

I distinguish three operations: addition (offer a new sound), substitution (alter a sound already being supplied through one's own channel), and relinquishment (remove one's own layer). The fourth, silencing another party, is categorically different. These operations can sound identical in a recording and differ radically in their practical and ethical significance.

## Historical encounter: Neuhaus's second departure from the concert hall

In [Walks](https://www.max-neuhaus.estate/en/sound-works/vectors/walks), Max Neuhaus describes his dissatisfaction with importing mundane sound into the sanctified setting of a concert. Listeners might respond more to the transgression than to the sounds. His response was to move the audience rather than move the sounds. The artist's first independent Listen work is listed in 1966. Read the artist's two paragraphs and the dated work entries; this is an autobiographical account, not independent audience evidence.

His [Berlin talk, 26 May 1995](https://www.max-neuhaus.estate/en/bibliography/publications/max-neuhaus-berlin-26-may-1995), paragraphs beginning “If we look at early communities,” “But actually the ideas started much earlier,” and “The first large scale realization,” opens a second departure. He discusses communal signals as sharing time across physical separation, then describes an alarm clock based on cessation rather than onset. He describes recolouring live sound in the Whitney sculpture garden in 1983, progressively adding the modified material and abruptly withdrawing it. The [estate's Archetype page](https://www.max-neuhaus.estate/en/sound-works/vectors/moment/time-piece-archetype-1983) juxtaposes the artist's retrospective account (ten minutes) with Carter Ratcliff's contemporary description (twenty minutes). Keep that timing discrepancy unresolved; the argument here needs no precise historical ramp duration. Read both excerpts and the relevant talk paragraphs, not Ratcliff's complete essay. Neuhaus's friends' reported reactions to his clock do not establish alarm effectiveness. His claims about universal sound character and the psychology of change are artistic/theoretical assertions, not accepted psychological facts.

What interests me is the difference between the two departures. The first relinquishes the concert hall as a necessary frame. The second relinquishes a previously introduced acoustic layer. In both cases the intervention attempts to make the encounter with the world itself into material. In the second the artist briefly borrows the world as an instrument. The borrowing creates a responsibility that is not discharged simply by switching the amplifier off.

A reverse bell can still demand attention. One should not confuse the subtlety of a signal with permission to occupy another person's listening environment. Nor does a signal heard by two villages make those villages share an interpretation, a language, or an agreement. A sound can bind a moment without binding a polity.

## Listening is not the same as clearing a channel

Hildegard Westerkamp, [Linking soundscape composition and acoustic ecology](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1355771802001085), Organised Sound 7(1), 2002, pp. 51–56, §1 and the visible footnote (publisher extract only), situates her composition with environmental recordings in the World Soundscape Project and Vancouver Co-operative Radio. Recording, electronic studio techniques, and alternative broadcasting contributed to her artistic language. Her footnote restricts the term in that article to tape pieces made from environmental recordings, excluding site installations; I therefore do not retroactively classify Neuhaus under her specific definition.

At her 2024 SFU convocation, as reported in the [university account](https://www.sfu.ca/fcat/news/news/news-archive/summer-2024/communication-alumnus-and-renowned-acoustic-ecologist-hildegard-.html), paragraph beginning “Westerkamp conducted her first soundwalk,” she invited people to consider which sounds might cease and which might become audible during a power outage. She also connects listening to slowing down. Read those paragraphs and the closing remarks; this is an institutional report incorporating her words, not research establishing therapeutic benefits.

This is an unusual inversion of signal engineering. An engineer may treat background as a nuisance to remove to improve reception of an intended signal. A soundwalker may treat the engineer's intended signal as part of the environment, and background as the material worth attending to. Neither partition has universal priority.

Nimalan Yoganathan's [Sounding Out Normative and Colour-Blind Listening in Acoustic Ecology](https://doi.org/10.3138/cjc.2022-0025), Canadian Journal of Communication 47(3), 2022, pp. 462–487, version of record dated 2023-05-13, provides a necessary counterposition. Read the indexed abstract, introduction and visible passages in “(Mis)hearing silence in pandemic times,” not the complete article; direct DOI opening returned 403, but indexed publisher text was available. The author challenges the cultural neutrality of prevailing soundscape hierarchies, including celebrations of lockdown quiet that omit unequal lived experiences. The discussion distinguishes desirable quiet from imposed quiet, and community sound from harmful noise. This is the author's critical argument, not an independent audit of acoustic ecology conducted here.

This changes the proposal. Acoustic restitution cannot mean establishing an ideal quiet room. A room in which nobody is permitted to speak is not a liberated acoustic environment. Nor is removing an assistive cue or a necessary warning an act of liberation merely because the result is quieter. The designer may control the system's own sound; they do not thereby become the room's arbiter. A soundscape can contain relations that neither designer nor listener has a complete account of.

There is an experimentally grounded objection to the reverse bell too: Eramudugolla et al., [Directed attention eliminates 'change deafness' in complex auditory scenes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15964275/), Current Biology 15(12), 2005, pp. 1108–1113, abstract, reports missed disappearances in complex scenes and improvement when attention was directed towards the relevant object. This abstract-level source was already identified in MHAC #36. Do not infer that all sound removal is conspicuous, that directed attention always solves the problem, or that the artwork's success can be inferred from the experiment.

## A restricted acoustic construction

Imagine two stems, b (the rest of the room) and a (the artist's contribution), represented as equal-length real sample vectors. In a linear monophonic mixing model, removal changes mean-square energy by

    E(b+a) - E(b) = E(a) + 2 <b,a>/N

where E(x) = sum(x_i^2)/N and the inner product is the unnormalized dot product. This identity is a direct expansion of the square. It does not measure loudness, annoyance, intelligibility, acoustic exposure, or listening effort. In particular, removal can increase energy if the cross-term is sufficiently negative. Equal power of a does not establish equal effect on the room.

The attached renderer constructs a fictional room bed of 90 and 150 Hz sinusoids, plus a target voice at 300, 450 or 600 Hz. Four identical 12-second base scenes receive distinct treatments in the first six seconds. The first is untreated; the others add a terraced gain of 1/6 through 1: a copy of the target, an equal-amplitude 750 Hz tone, or an inverted copy of the target. All overlays disappear at second six. This is an original synthetic composition, not an attempt to reconstruct a site-specific Neuhaus work. No microphone or field recording is used.

At the sixth one-second frame, exact ideal orthogonality on the 16 kHz grid yields the following values (the implemented floating-point measurements agree to the displayed precision):

| Treatment | Overlay mean square | Mix mean square | Target sine projection amplitude |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Room only | 0 | 0.0050625 | 0.08 |
| In phase | 0.0032 | 0.0146625 | 0.16 |
| Disjoint frequency | 0.0032 | 0.0082625 | 0.08 |
| Opposite phase | 0.0032 | 0.0018625 | 0 |

The projection is a known-frequency, known-phase measurement, not a source separator: in-phase overlay and original target cannot be distinguished by that projection. The target stem is invariant by construction. The opposite-phase case cancels it in the ideal digital sum. Removing the overlay restores the target projection while *increasing* mix power from 0.0018625 to 0.0050625. Removing the in-phase overlay reduces mix power; removing the disjoint overlay changes power without changing the target's sine projection. Same intervention power, three different results.

This is not a model of active noise cancellation over a real room. Propagation, delay, reflections, listener position, hardware and perceptual grouping are absent. It is not a claim about which of the four sounds is easier to hear. It does, however, falsify a purely algebraic assumption that deleting a source necessarily reduces the energy of its summed scene.

Run:

    python research/expeditions/composer-gives-room-back/render.py build/room-back

Python stdlib, PCM16 mono, 16 kHz. The final 96,000 samples of every file are byte-identical. The script checks 12 equal-overlay-power cases, 48 projection equations, the full-gain energy ordering, cancellation, the invariant suffix and four reopened WAV formats and data blocks, and peak bounds for every frame. Two local renders reproduced all hashes. See [execution receipt](RECEIPT.md). No actual playback or listener study.

## Exaptation: a relinquishment bus

Most mixers already have mutes, fades, sidechains and priority ducking. The contribution is not another name for these mechanisms, but a suggested difference in their contract. A relinquishment bus would maintain separate routes for (i) sound under the instrument's ownership, (ii) a dry, independently provided reference when available, and (iii) any genuinely required status/event channel. It would manipulate only (i). It would never purport to know all of (ii), and never use the end of (i) as proof of world-state resolution. Unlike ordinary sidechain ducking, the artistic control is independently playable even when no competing signal has been detected.

Imagine a modular performance in which each performer has a named contribution, a voluntarily chosen withdrawal curve, and a return condition. Another control, labelled LEAVE UNCLAIMED, disables the instrument's automatic attempt to fill holes. The machine is permitted not to classify the remaining sound. A procedural synthesizer could apply this to its own generative layers: a dense swarm of virtual oscillators gradually relinquishes portions of a spectrum, and a separately authored melodic voice is exposed without that voice's amplitude being modulated. Compose the ordering of releases rather than the ordering of notes. The unreleased layers remain free to persist; not every arrangement must converge to silence.

A digital implementation can record its own contribution, withdrawal schedule, requested fade and actual execution. It cannot by that ledger alone establish what a listener heard or who owns the space. Do not make ambient capture a prerequisite; no covert recording, automatic silencing of others or unapproved microphone permission. Use consented stems or no sensor. Accessibility, user-selected alternatives and critical alerts must remain independently available. This is a sketch, not a validated feature.

For MHAC the question is a complement to #36: instead of only asking how to encode a warning within a mixture, ask how much of the mixture the system ought to contribute at all. Candidate comparison: fixed machine status layer; voluntary timed release of nonessential texture; equal-energy spectral relocation; discrete-only no continuous layer; optionally user-initiated muting of owned nonessential sound. Match informational availability with a separately accessible state view, and leave necessary explicit event cues intact. Outcomes must separate target identification, timely correct action, missing status, burden and voluntary preferences. Reject or narrow if withdrawal harms any critical task, reduces accessibility or produces misleading all-clear inference. No participants or deployment are authorized by this expedition.

## Objections and unresolved directions

- Withdrawing may itself be intrusive. A sudden disappearance can be as salient as an arrival; subtlety and consent are independent.
- A composer who declares all noncomposed sound to be the score has annexed the room conceptually even after withdrawing acoustically. The score should permit unclaimed events and dissent.
- Cancellation may produce acoustic energy elsewhere. The mono example is deliberately not a spatial soundfield controller.
- Room bed is not a single invariant real-world object. The renderer holds it fixed for experimental clarity. Actual soundscapes evolve and differ across listeners.
- Formal superposition is not politics; the energy cross-term is neither a measure of power in society nor a proof of any ethical claim.
- A temporary return to the old waveform is not a return to the old encounter. The audience may have changed, left, or never noticed. A previous MHAC expedition on perceptual aspect deals with this explicitly; here the focus is relinquishment of material participation, not listener reset.
- Future source lead: examine the complete Yoganathan and Kelman arguments and Neuhaus's own Moment texts alongside sound artist critiques of imposing interventions in shared space. Do not promote these scout notes into the systematic corpus.
