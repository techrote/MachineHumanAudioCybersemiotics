**Exploratory humanities/art expedition — 2026-11-27.** [Issue #756](https://github.com/techrote/MachineHumanAudioCybersemiotics/issues/756). AI-assisted source reading, interpretation, mathematical construction and original synthetic sound art by ChatGPT (GPT-6.2). Not a frozen Track G extraction, Track E study, privacy certification or legal opinion. No participants, personal records, third-party audio, external reviewers or license grants.

## The auditorium with a second score

*Original fictional construction.* A concert has three performers. One contributes only to the present gathering. Two also agree to an archival edition. Their agreement is about a specific use of their contributions, not the value of their work. The technician records the entire concert and promises to remove whatever is not meant for the archive. A second technician makes a distinct edition as the concert happens. Which technician has actually understood the score?

The first conceives of the recording as the whole work, and its editing as the removal of defects. The second understands that the live work and the archival work are different things. Perhaps a participant whose sound cannot enter the archive should still be capable of determining the concert's form. Perhaps the archival composition should have a conspicuous lacuna, or an entirely different passage rather than a simulation of the missing singer. The disappearance could be a legitimate aesthetic event rather than an omission to conceal.

The concert has two scores: the organization of sound, and the organization of its circulation. Neither is an adequate substitute for the other.

## Intellectual encounters and reading boundaries

**Édouard Glissant.** *Poetics of Relation*, trans. Betsy Wing (University of Michigan Press, 1997), “For Opacity,” printed pp. 189–193. [Publisher/edition](https://press.umich.edu/Books/P/Poetics-of-Relation2); [course-hosted chapter transcription](https://englishes-mooc.org/media/pages/course/module-03/c65603fc2f-1571655966/glissant.pdf). Read extracted chapter and translator's note; PDF pages 1–2 visually inspected. Opening discussion of reduction through the demand for transparency, passage distinguishing opacity from obscurity, and final discussion of solidarity without grasping. Glissant's argument challenges the condition that another person must become comprehensible according to the observer's categories before relation is possible. His account is rooted in Caribbean history and the violence of colonial universalization, not in signal processing. The translator's note on comprehension/grasping makes the metaphor of possession particularly relevant.

**Interpretation:** a collaborator may be present without becoming an object of comprehensive capture. Glissant's opacity is irreducibility, not a setting on an export dialog. A deliberately omitted audio stem is a much narrower, engineered analogy. Calling the two equivalent would domesticate the philosophical argument into precisely the kind of reduction it resists. There is also a danger in assigning an artist an opaque identity that the artist never requested. In this construction, permission is explicitly stipulated rather than inferred.

**Helen Nissenbaum.** “Privacy as Contextual Integrity,” *Washington Law Review* 79 (2004), 119–158. [Institutional publication record and author abstract](https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/wlr/vol79/iss1/10/). Read abstract, not full article. Nissenbaum locates the appropriateness of information collection and dissemination within contextual norms. **Interpretation:** participation in one event does not, by itself, establish the appropriateness of all downstream information flows. This is an invitation to make destination and purpose explicit, not a claim that two Boolean flags satisfy contextual integrity or law.

**Trinh T. Minh-ha.** Nancy N. Chen, “Speaking Nearby: A Conversation with Trinh T. Minh-ha,” *Visual Anthropology Review* 8(1) (1992), pp. 82–91, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1525/var.1992.8.1.82). Publisher page exposed metadata only; the interview was not fully accessible. [esea contemporary, Speaking Nearby, 22 July 2023](https://www.eseacontemporary.org/events/speaking-nearby), event-description paragraphs 1–3, read the reproduced interview passage and programme context. The excerpt describes a form of speech that approaches its subject without appropriation. **Interpretation:** an archive may speak alongside a performance rather than speak as the performance. The artist's documentary strategy is not a technical consent protocol. Full primary interview retrieval remains a worthwhile follow-up.

**Tino Sehgal.** [Guggenheim artist account](https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/artist/tino-sehgal), paragraphs 1–2, and [This Progress, object 22502](https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/22502), opening paragraphs. Read institutional descriptions, not an observed performance. Sehgal's constructed situations refuse conventional photographic/film documentation while allowing repeated enactment and exchange of the works. **Counterexample to recording as the only persistence mechanism:** an artwork may persist through reenactment rather than through a definitive recording. The absence of a photograph does not mean the absence of a work. [Vivian van Saaze, “In the absence of documentation,” 2015, pp. 55–63](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/in-the-absence-of-documentation-remembering-tino-sehgals-construc/), institutional abstract only, identifies embodied transmission and conservation as issues for museums acquiring these works. These practices precede this proposal; no historical priority is claimed.

A counter-reading is essential. Opacity may be imposed by a powerful curator to exclude a public or prevent scrutiny. A refusal of recording can prevent access by people unable to attend. Documentation can protect performers from misrepresentation. An independently authored edition should be negotiated rather than imposed, and access arrangements need explicit consideration. None of these sources makes machine opacity a virtue for safety-critical decisions. A machine need not disclose irrelevant human material to provide sufficient accountable evidence about its own actions.

## Finite laboratory: a live sum does not identify its permitted edition

**Exact mathematical construction, not a theory of consent.** Let each of three labelled contributors supply an integer PCM vector x_i. L is the set admitted to the live mix. G is the set granted inclusion in a particular archival edition. For the narrow purpose of archiving this live performance (not creating a new studio composition), the maximal permitted set in the model is P = L intersect G. Permissions are stipulated and truthful; no identity, authority or entitlement is inferred. The live sum is y = sum over L; the edition is z = sum over P. We assume independent dry additive stems, no clipping, no cross-stem effects and no other observations.

For any one sample with n = |L|, r = |P| and each contribution in {-1,0,1}, the possible z given y are exactly the integers in [max(-r, y-(n-r)), min(r, y+(n-r))]. Necessity follows from the bounds on the two disjoint sums. Sufficiency follows because every integer within the sum bounds can be realized by a suitable number of +1 or -1 contributions and the rest zero. Independent sample coordinates give a Cartesian product of these intervals. If 0 < r < n, the interval has multiple values at every non-extreme y. At y = +/-n the interval collapses. If P is empty or all of L, the projection is unique everywhere.

Example: A and B are live; only A is granted archival inclusion. For one sample, histories (A,B)=(1,0) and (0,1) both give live sample 1, but archive samples 1 and 0. Even a perfect mixed master and complete permission mask do not resolve which history occurred. This is an elementary non-injectivity proof, not a claim of novel mathematics or that practical source separation never works. Additional priors, stems, recordings or restrictions can change recoverability.

For two independent samples, there are 3^6 = 729 labelled three-stem histories. For all 64 pairs of masks, the oracle independently constructs all possible archives for each live sum and compares with the interval formula; all 46,656 cases pass. There are 1,216 receipt classes counted across permission pairs, 522 ambiguous. Eighteen permission pairs contain both included and excluded live voices. Among these, for a live mask of size n, exactly (2n+1)^2 - 4 distinct two-sample receipts are ambiguous. The four corners are unique. These are census counts, not estimates of actual audio recovery or people's behaviour.

The general linear-algebra interpretation: a desired projection is recoverable from a linear observation on an unrestricted vector space exactly when the observation's kernel is contained in the desired projection's kernel. An additive sum loses the antisymmetric difference between two stems. That difference is audible in one selected stem when the other is excluded. The renderer deliberately exploits it.

## Four editions of an unrecordable concert

Original synthetic six-second mono composition. A is a pair of low sustained tones, B two overlapping higher tones, C a set of bass pulses. A fourth phrase, Crossing, consists of three short bright notes. In world I, Crossing belongs to A. In world II, Crossing belongs to B. The phrase itself is identical. All three performers are admitted live; A and C alone are permitted in the archive.

- I — Live I: A + Crossing + B + C.
- II — Live II: A + B + Crossing + C.
- III — Edition I: A + Crossing + C.
- IV — Edition II: A + C.

The two live WAVs are identical. The archival editions differ exactly by Crossing, sample for sample. Thus there are two possible authorized editions of an acoustically identical live master. The authority to select is not contained in the waveform. Here the provenance assignment is intentionally synthetic; these are not real people's contributions or rights.

*Original score for a future performance:* Give each player independent live and archival scores, and an opportunity to decline particular uses without being expelled from the live work. Play both editions without identifying individual absences unless that disclosure is separately appropriate. If an absent part changes the shape of an archival edition, compose that change openly. Do not replace an absent player by an imitation while presenting it as the original contribution. Let the recorded edition have its own ending. Consider a third edition consisting only of an agreed new composition, not material from the live mix.

The possible objection: an individual who did not consent to archival inclusion might have influenced the other players. The archive can carry their indirect traces. An ensemble's relationships cannot generally be partitioned into independent stems. A shared reverb driven by a withheld source, feedback, sidechains, a performer adapting their notes or simply an audience member remembering the event all violate any naive promise of total erasure. The current lab assumes separable dry stems and does not test these forms of causal influence. A revocation is prospective for copies the system controls; it does not erase copies already released, audience memory or downstream recordings. No guarantee of confidentiality or exclusion of indirect influence is established.

## Instruments, interfaces and a second rabbit hole

**The Edition Organ (VSTi concept):** separate live and publication bus graphs, with per-source destination and use declarations. Real-time sound continues when an explicitly stipulated archive route is absent. Render the authorized edition from the permitted upstream sources rather than pretend a master can always be repaired after capture. A conservative system refuses an unresolved export rather than silently guessing contributors. The technical route graph is separate from the actual human authorization process.

**The Room That Does Not Travel (VSTfx concept):** multistem input, separately instantiated dry/effect graphs for live and archive, explicit routing provenance, a check for downstream sends from excluded stems, and an independent artistic edition. It must not claim that post hoc source separation is proof of absence. Future adversarial tests would include reverb tails, sidechain gain, feedback, cached renders, offline bounce, and a difference between audio routing and permission.

**Guest Field (CYD concept):** an oscillator landscape with two independent monitors. XY selects local textures; a mode switch selects the live or archival projection. The performer can create different musical interiors with the same live sound. On an embedded platform these should initially be fictional permissions on synthetic generated voices, not human consent management.

**A second rabbit hole — the debt of the unrecorded player:** If B's sound is withheld, but A's improvisation is inspired by B, what remains of B in A? The mathematically safe answer is only about the direct digital stem. The artistic answer may require acknowledging an unrecorded relationship without naming or reconstructing the person. An intentionally nonliteral new piece could respond to the event, without purporting to be a record. Distinguish direct inclusion, causal influence, attribution, description and resemblance; none implies the others. The question opens towards joint authorship, performative memory and the limits of machine-readable provenance, without pretending to resolve them.

## MHAC transfer, negative cases and revision criteria

A command receipt and a comprehensive transcript need not contain the same material. Specify the information actually necessary for the intended action and disclose the limits of the recorded scope. Human opacity and machine accountability must not be conflated: it can be appropriate to restrict personal data while preserving action identity, authorization, material failure evidence and the uncertainty required to judge an operation. If a missing contribution is necessary for accountability, the system should disclose the insufficiency or refrain from making the relevant verified claim, not manufacture a reassuring substitute.

Future synthetic test: compare ordinary explicit permission/routing tables, post-mix labels, source-scoped export and source-scoped export plus an optional acoustic cue. Independently vary permission changes, destination, stem availability, shared effects and release timing. Outcomes: unauthorized direct inclusion, incorrect omission, false claims of complete reconstruction, downstream contamination, operator workload and decision correctness. No human studies here. A simple textual routing table plus separation at capture may be sufficient; no acoustic advantage demonstrated. If the whole master is licensed for the intended use or no live contribution is included, the present ambiguity disappears. If known source constraints make the selected projection unique, the non-injectivity example does not apply to that constrained system.

*Speculative afterword:* The archivist asked the guest for the missing song. The guest said the song had not been missing from the concert. The archivist opened a second catalogue, not for a deficient copy, but for a different work.
