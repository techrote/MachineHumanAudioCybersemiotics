# The last stop
*Original speculative fiction, not an empirical or historical account.*

In the city of Ravel, the public organ had been commissioned to play a note whenever the council agreed to hear one. The first generation of organ builders were anxious about tuning. The second were anxious about the council. The third had inherited neither anxiety, only a cabinet of brass labels.

One label read SOUND. Beneath it were two sockets and a little window in which the number of necessary hands was engraved.

Another read CHANGE THE NUMBER OF HANDS. There were three sockets below that one.

When the winter came, two councillors decided that the third should not have to climb the steps. They voted to reduce the number of hands needed for a note. The organ refused. They tried to reduce the number needed to reduce the number. The organ refused again.

The third councillor arrived in spring, leaning on a stick. She had spent the winter composing a piece for the instrument's refusal. She brought a copy of its circuit diagram, and a small reed whistle.

The other two listened. They asked how she had managed to write music on an instrument whose keyboard she could not reach.

She handed them the circuit diagram. Every time the organ's relay had refused to close, she had drawn a dot. Every dot was now a rest. The piece was nearly silent.

At the next meeting, a visitor proposed an organ in which the hands that set the numbers could also set the number of hands that set the numbers. Everyone agreed that this was an elegant solution.

A child asked whether the visitor might have brought the old organ's whistle instead.

The council voted to hear the whistle.

It sounded before the vote was counted.
