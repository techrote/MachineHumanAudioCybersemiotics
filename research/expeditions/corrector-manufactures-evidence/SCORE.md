# Two Archives, One Mouth

Original conceptual performance score. Four people or four software voices; 2–8 minutes. No imitation of a human dialect or tradition.

Choose two clearly distinguishable sounds X and Y, at comfortable level. A pair is two evenly spaced sounds. The same relation repeats, the different relation answers. Neither is inherently superior. For this work only, same is A and different is B.

Roles: Source plays pairs; Editor may alter the second sound of a B pair into the first; Archive plays the resulting pairs; Witness keeps separate counts of raw A/B, emitted A/B and edited B. The Source may stop at any time. The Witness must not infer a performer's intentions from absence.

## I. The census
Source presents ten pairs: four A and six B, with five first tokens X and five Y, in any order. Archive repeats each pair unedited. The Witness counts the relations. Repeat the exercise with a different arrangement but the same counts. A census is not a score.

## II. The evidence factory
Source repeats the previous set. Editor converts three B pairs to A, chosen by lot, without telling Archive which. Archive performs its received set. Witness first announces the archive B count (three), then the raw count (six) and the intervention count (three). Do not label the archive report false: it accurately reports a different object.

## III. Two archives
The two archive voices share one fresh source. Start their B-share controls at zero and 0.6. Use q'=0.15+0.375q below 0.5, q'=0.15+0.75q otherwise. Each update is one bar. Play an independently chosen first token and use the current q to select the proportion of B over the following phrase; use a marked probability or proportional pitch controller if exact realized pair counts are impractical. Do not present finite randomized counts as equal to q. The two proportions approach 0.24 and 0.60. At bar 12 increase fresh source fraction to 0.75; now q'=0.45+0.125q below 0.5 and 0.45+0.25q otherwise. Listen to the branches approach one another. For a silent performance, show the q values and preserve the two columns.

## IV. The right to a second convention
Disengage learning. The Source chooses A or B freely. Editor offers an alternate realization but cannot replace Source. Archive may play either or both; record the choice as an editorial decision. End with a pair that neither requires correction nor requests imitation. If the performers disagree about whether the last pair is satisfying, that is a valid ending.

## Fiction: The Ministry of Regular Pairs

The Ministry kept a recording of every citizen's second note. One morning an examiner noticed that a peculiar habit had vanished entirely. The citizens had once been fond of answering a whistle with a knock, but the Ministry's archives contained only whistles after whistles.

An archivist found a small apparatus behind the cabinet. It listened to the first sound and made the second resemble it. Each night the apparatus was taught by the cabinet, and each morning the cabinet received the apparatus's work.

The examiner ordered the apparatus to be removed. The archivist asked to leave it in the concert hall. It was, after all, a splendid instrument for playing echoes.

They disconnected only the wire that led back to the school.

*This is fiction, not an empirical claim about linguistic communities or human institutions.*
