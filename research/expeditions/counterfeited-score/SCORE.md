# Six Doors, One Room

Original text score for a cue performer, a phrase performer or sampler, and an optional witness. No one must demonstrate virtuosity or submit to an undisclosed test. Use two comfortable pitches (L and H), spoken syllables, or silent cards. Four equally spaced beats per phrase; choose a comfortable tempo and volume. Every phrase contains two L and two H. Cues and all modes are disclosed before any audience participation.

| Cue | Phrase |
|---|---|
| 0 | LLHH |
| 1 | LHLH |
| 2 | LHHL |
| 3 | HLLH |
| 4 | HLHL |
| 5 | HHLL |

## I. The catalogue

Play the six phrases in any order, once each. No cue is given. The witness is invited to remember the collection, not to guess a hidden law. It is a complete movement.

## II. The rehearsal of doors

The cue performer presents cards 0,1,2,3 in that order. The phrase performer answers by the table. Repeat, with an alternate table that swaps the responses to 4 and 5. The two movements have the same intended sounds. Now the cue performer selects either of the previously unused cards. Play both alternate responses, in a disclosed order. The difference resides in the connection, not the inventory.

## III. A concert with its future removed

The phrase performer prepares a tape or written sequence of four phrases before the cue performer chooses four cards. In another performance use the table live. The witness knows which rule is in use, and may listen for accidental coincidences. Do not stage this as a deception or a human test. The score requires no statistical success criterion.

## IV. Deferred hospitality

The cue performer may present up to six cards while a fixed sequence plays. The cards are retained without effect on the first voice. After the fixed sequence ends, the retained choices become the new score. Either performer can cancel the queued material. The final movement may therefore be silent. A listener is not obliged to supply a card.

## V. The alternative that remains unwritten

Choose a single phrase already played. The cue performer may ask what would have occurred had another card been selected. Audition that alternative if desired, explicitly as an alternative; do not replace the historical performance with it. End with either phrase, both, or neither.

### Fiction: inventory of the theatre

The theatre had purchased all six doors from a demolished building. The carpenter installed them in the correct order and the inspector counted six handles. The doors opened into one long corridor, with no relation to the rooms written on the labels.

A visitor asked for the kitchen. The caretaker opened the kitchen door and, by coincidence, a cook walked past.

The inspector wrote: FUNCTION CONFIRMED.

A second visitor asked for the courtyard. The caretaker opened the same door. This time nobody walked past.

The carpenter did not remove the doors. She installed a second panel on which the route behind each handle could be changed. The theatre advertised two evenings: the first for listening to the doors, the second for choosing where they opened.

The catalogue recorded the same six doors in both evenings. For the first time the programme also described the corridor.
