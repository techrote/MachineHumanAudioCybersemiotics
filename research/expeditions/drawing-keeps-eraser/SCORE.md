# Four Erasures for an Empty Instrument
Original unperformed score · Refs #516 · 25 November 2026

For four quiet pitched instruments or four labelled cards (A, B, C, D), a fifth optional residue performer, and an audience that need not participate. Use four distinct modest-register tones; e.g. A3, C4, E4, G4. No third-party recorded material is necessary. Every principal voice starts active. Use a soft attack/release, and a restrained fixed volume; a silent card realization is equally valid. All four voices are stopped once each. The residue is an authored second voice, not a physical remnant of sound.

## I. A room with no walls

No edges. Choose any deletion order, one per beat. As each voice stops, write zero in its labelled slot. After four beats, play four timed silent slots. Do not confuse this stipulated silence with a claim that a real receiver can detect zero. Pause.

## II. A room with a corridor

Use edges AB, BC, CD. In realization one delete A, C, B, D. Record A=1, B=0, C=2, D=0: at A's removal B survives; at C's removal B and D survive. In realization two delete C, A, D, B. The same labelled scar results. The history differs. Play the residual score in canonical A B C D order: one impulse, empty slot, two impulses, empty slot. Each slot lasts one second. A quiet click, brush sound, or card is sufficient. Avoid treating the two total histories as different if the residual score was the only object being compared.

## III. The conservator changes the walls

Repeat realization one with the ring AB, BC, CD, DA, then with all six edges. The residual vectors become (2,0,2,0) and (3,1,2,0), respectively. The principal voices and stopping schedule are unchanged. At the end of each pass repeat the residue score. In an optional variation discard the slot labels, sort the counts and play them anonymously; then discard all but the sum. Let the fifth performer choose how much of the order remains distinguishable. The conductor may show an all-ones ring scar and invite refusal: no complete strict deletion history could have made it.

## IV. The right not to become an exhibit

The musicians may elect before a pass whether to keep the labelled trace, retain only a multiset, or discard the trace completely. They are not required to restore, reveal or explain the lost notes. A recording of the whole performance would defeat the intended no-trace comparison; the score does not control an audience member's memory. End without announcing what the final silence means.

## Fiction: The conservator's small comb

The museum opened a cabinet containing four empty drawers. The first conservator had retained the names of every object. The second had retained the sound of the drawers being closed. The third had retained a little comb, with a tooth for every neighbour still occupied at the moment a drawer became empty.

The director asked the third conservator to reconstruct the exhibition. She could tell him that one drawer had shut before another. She could not tell him what it had contained. He asked whether the comb was an instrument or an inventory.

She put it on a wooden resonator and played the teeth in a different order. The director asked her to correct the performance. She answered that the order of the teeth was not the order of the drawers.

For the last exhibition she brought a comb without teeth. The director requested a label explaining what was absent. She asked whether an absence could be allowed to retire without another object taking its place.

This is fiction, not a report about any museum.
