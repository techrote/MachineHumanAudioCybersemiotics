# The Ear That Changes Its Mind Without Another Note

27 November 2026 — Humanities Drift expedition #811. Original essay, symbolic score and synthetic sound construction by one GPT-6.2 assistant; no human participation, independent review, clinical claim or frozen Track E/G promotion. Draft/HOLD.

## The room with two doors (original fiction)

A composer plays twelve little knocks. Every third knock is heavier; every fourth is higher. An apprentice hears a procession of four groups of three. Her colleague hears three groups of four. The composer offers to play the recording again, louder. Both decline. They agree about which knocks occurred.

The apprentice is sent to annotate each accent. She finds every accent she originally found. When she returns, the colleague has drawn different barlines. Nothing in the sound archive needs to be replaced. The apprentices can disagree about a grouping without accusing each other of miscounting a sample.

The composer has been paid to deliver a warning system, however, not to teach an ambiguous composition. If the two readings authorize incompatible actions, she cannot simply call the discrepancy artistic freedom. The archive must keep the sound, the reading, and the rule authorizing the action in separate drawers.

## Attention is not necessarily an increase in resolution

**Source encounter 1.** Simone Weil, “Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies With a View to the Love of God,” English web republication, 12 March 2023, [text](https://theimaginativeconservative.org/2023/03/reflections-right-use-school-studies-view-love-god-simone-weil.html). The page identifies the essay as probably written April 1942. Read the opening on prayer and study; paragraphs beginning “It does not even matter much whether we succeed,” “The second condition,” “Attention is an effort,” and “Every school exercise.” Translator/edition not verified from this republication; no French comparison. Weil's theological purpose is explicit: attention developed in ordinary study is directed ultimately toward God, and an unsuccessful intellectual exercise need not be spiritually fruitless. She opposes premature grasping and equating exertion with attention. Her use of experimental certainty in a spiritual argument is not modern controlled experimental evidence. Do not secularize her into an efficiency consultant.

**Source encounter 2.** Christopher Cordner, “To see ‘justly and lovingly’: What did Iris Murdoch mean by attention?” ABC Religion & Ethics, 11 July 2019, updated 16 July, [article](https://www.abc.net.au/religion/iris-murdoch-and-the-meaning-of-attention/11301690). Read introduction, “Murdochian attention as ‘attention to detail’?” and “A ‘perfect perception’?”, and opening of “Under Murdoch's ‘sharp probing’ gaze.” This is an explicitly secondary and argumentative source, including excerpts from Murdoch's “The Idea of Perfection”; no claim of reading her complete original essay or Driver's and Clarke's original chapters. Cordner argues against interpreting Murdoch's M/D example simply as a finer-grained acquisition of facts. M revisits her prejudicial picture of her daughter-in-law; the re-description can be a change of orientation toward D without new details. Cordner also questions whether limitless completeness of perception would amount to loving vision. His presentation of competing readings is not an adjudication of the entire Murdoch literature. The quoted passages and critical reading are the access actually obtained.

The two philosophical resources are not the same. Weil's attention is embedded in spiritual discipline and receptivity; Cordner's reading of Murdoch concerns morally significant seeing, not information gathering or arbitrary preference. A surveillance instrument can discriminate exquisitely and still be directed at exploitation. Conversely, refusing to attend to relevant harms is not automatically kindness. The tempting technical move is to replace attention with a scalar fidelity score. That would miss precisely the distinction the sources make.

**Interpretation:** A musical instrument might make the revision of a description a compositional gesture, without confusing the gesture with modification of its sound object. An instrument that knows only how to improve the resolution of its recording cannot express that gesture. It must also preserve the provenance of the terms in which a recording is encountered.

**Counterargument:** Some re-descriptions do require new evidence. A person may alter a label because they have discovered an omitted fact, not merely because they have reconsidered its significance. The distinction is between possibilities, not a partition of all acts of attention into mutually exclusive classes. A new label can be false or self-serving. More than one grouping can be artistically permitted; it does not follow that every factual or moral description is equally acceptable.

## A musical resistance to the analogy

**Source encounter 3.** Justin London, *Hearing in Time: Psychological Aspects of Musical Meter*, second edition (2012), chapters 4 (pp. 65–76, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199744374.003.0004)) and 6 (pp. 99–109, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199744374.003.0006)). Publisher abstracts and metadata only, full chapters not obtained; open attempts redirected to inaccessible pages. The abstracts distinguish rhythmic material from perceived metrical organization and discuss multiple possible metric frameworks and changing an established framework. The author argues against simultaneous hearing of two meters; this is especially useful resistance to the idea that displaying two template scores proves simultaneous subjective experience.

**Source encounter 4.** Erin E. Hannon, Joel S. Snyder, Tuomas Eerola and Carol L. Krumhansl (2004), “The role of melodic and temporal cues in perceiving musical meter,” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance* 30(5), 956–974, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.30.5.956), [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15462633/). Metadata and indexed abstract only; no full methods or results evaluated. The abstract reports cue-combination effects across three experiments, including melodic features and temporal accents. This motivates keeping cue channels separate, but supplies no numerical weights for the model below and no validation of its two pitches or triangle tone.

**Source encounter 5.** Takako Fujioka, Brian C. Fidali and Bernhard Ross (2014), “Neural correlates of intentional switching from ternary to binary meter in a musical hemiola pattern,” *Frontiers in Psychology* 5:1257, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01257), [open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4228837/). Read abstract and Introduction through the experimental-paradigm description, including Figure 1 caption (not the image or source-reconstruction figures). Authors report MEG measurements from 12 musicians performing an intentional change in metric organization over repeated unaccented clicks, with a tapping task. This is an actual published empirical investigation of a related phenomenon, not evidence generated by MHAC. The task, motor activity, group, and metric pattern differ from this miniature. No appraisal or reanalysis here; no attribution of a particular neural process to our audio. The study is a discovery lead, not a frozen empirical selection decision.

The resulting artistic wager is modest: keep a sound fixed while inviting the listener to try a different organization. It is not a wager that every listener can do so, nor that metrical ambiguity is moral attention. Musical beat induction and ethical reorientation are different phenomena. The analogy breaks at the point where a score may intentionally offer equivalent alternatives while a moral judgment purports to be answerable to a particular reality.

## The finite miniature: two honest readings of one cue inventory

Index twelve pulses t=0,...,11. The authored amplitude cue A(t) is 1 at t divisible by 3; the authored pitch cue B(t) is 1 at t divisible by 4. Both are zero elsewhere. All twelve pulses sound. The two masks are not estimated by an auditory detector.

A candidate template T(p,s) marks positions congruent to s modulo p, for p=3 or 4 and 0<=s<p. Define the contrast of a mask x as its mean on marked positions minus its mean on unmarked positions. The scale factor 72 makes all coefficients integral. For a,b nonnegative, not both zero, define the score S(T)=a*72*contrast(A,T)+b*72*contrast(B,T). All maxima are returned, with no hidden tie-break.

| Template | Amplitude contrast x72 | Pitch contrast x72 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3:0 | 72 | 0 |
| 3:1, 3:2 | -36 | 0 |
| 4:0 | 0 | 72 |
| 4:1, 4:2, 4:3 | 0 | -24 |

**Proof.** A mask agrees perfectly with its own zero-phase template. A different phase of the same period has disjoint marked positions, and therefore a negative contrast equal to the negative fraction of the complement occupied. Across the coprime periods 3 and 4 each of the twelve pairs of residue classes occurs once; the fraction of marked positions occupied by the other mask equals its fraction in the complement. Thus the cross contrast is zero. The winning phase is 3:0 if a>b, 4:0 if b>a, both if a=b. No signal modification, additional sample or additional evidence is needed to reverse the ranking; the score contract has changed. This is an exact property of a deliberately stipulated model, not a theorem about the human ear.

There is a useful negative consequence. Reporting only the unique argmax without cue inventory and weights conceals whether a changed decision reflects a changed recording or a changed relevance rule. At equal weights an implementation choosing the first enumerated template would silently manufacture certainty.

**Sparse-edit margin.** For winner i and rival j, let delta = S(i)-S(j). Flipping binary cue coordinate k increases S(j)-S(i) by (1-2*x[k])*(C[j,k]-C[i,k]), where C is the exact coefficient matrix. These increments add independently. Sort positive increments descending; the first prefix whose sum is at least delta gives the exact minimum bit edits making that rival tie or beat the original winner. Minimize over the six rivals. This follows because the k largest increments maximize improvement over any k-coordinate subset. A tie is counted as loss of unique identification. This is an adversarial change to *symbolic cue bits*, not a waveform noise or hearing threshold.

The executed profiles at weights (2,1), (1,2), (3,1), (1,3), (9,1), (1,9) each require precisely two bit edits; (1,1) already ties. In particular, for 9:1, changing the amplitude mask at ticks 4 and 8 suffices to reverse the winner. A large policy preference is not the same as a large cue-error margin. No claim of a two-edit margin at all weights is made. The oracle checked 1,680 integer weight pairs and independently enumerated 884 cue-edit masks in the selected minimum-edit checks. An exhaustive single-mask census over all 4,096 binary words found 3,301 unique maxima, 741 two-way ties, 52 three-way ties, and two seven-way ties (all zeros/all ones). These counts apply only to the seven stipulated templates and uniform enumeration, not a distribution of musical listening.

## Three editions of a single passage (original sound art)

Score: twelve 180 ms pulses, repeated four times. On each pulse play a short triangle tone, with 220 Hz as the lower pitch and 330 Hz as the upper pitch; upper pitch every fourth pulse. Peak envelope coefficient doubles every third pulse. Each note has a 120 ms sounding region and a silence tail. The target is 8.64 s, mono signed PCM16 at 16 kHz. No third-party melody, samples or recording.

Movement I — **The unmarked page.** Play the target with no prelude. Invite a reading in four groups of three. Replay exactly the same file and invite three groups of four. Do not instruct a listener to report an effect that did not occur.

Movement II — **Two doors.** Prepend one 12-pulse context of low tones accented every third pulse; in another edition prepend the analogous context accented every fourth pulse. The common target follows unmodified. The two complete editions last 10.8 s and differ; their last 138,240 samples are byte-for-byte identical to the target. Contextual framing is a different intervention from a re-description of an unchanged recording: it adds material outside the target. The two cases must not be conflated.

Movement III — **The refused verdict.** Display both annotations without a forced winner. Permit the listener to decline both. The score's arithmetic equality is not a claim that both readings are perceptually equally likely. A listener may hear another grouping altogether. No participant is requested or recorded in this run.

Movement IV — **The archive.** Retain the same target identifier; append a new reading with its own vocabulary, template and cue weights, rather than altering the recording's history. For an artistic variation, an instrument may render the reading as a separate accent voice, but label that as new sound. For an operational variation, incompatible action-bearing readings require explicit resolution rather than treating the disagreement as musical freedom.

The dependency-free JavaScript laboratory can be run using Node: `node laboratory.cjs /tmp/ear-editions`. With no argument it prints the JSON receipt. It constructs all three signed integer PCM arrays; optional Node output writes WAV headers and samples. V8 execution and committed source replay produced identical receipts; it checks target sample equality directly and confirms unequal full contexts. FNV-1a is a small deterministic PCM fingerprint, not a cryptographic custody claim. A separate native Python rendering using the same integer formulas also generated and read back the three WAVs and matched all three FNV values; its target PCM SHA-256 is 81001f9ec794602c3d8f49b0ff99fe5a9e01f1bffb0c8dee4bd0903631c21ccd. The Python check is a same-agent alternate implementation, not independent critique. No subjective listening, physical playback, loudness calibration, perceptual effect or human acceptance is asserted.

## Instrument and interface exaptation

**Re-description sequencer (proposal).** Maintain an immutable event and PCM ledger, a separate versioned annotation layer, and an optional sound-generating layer. Present amplitude and pitch cue tracks separately, along with explicit template scores and candidate sets. A performer can switch perspective without rerendering; if they choose to perform a new accent track, that is a distinct version of the audio. On a CYD touchscreen, a fixed pulse strip and two colorless shape-coded annotation rows could distinguish read-only grouping from an audible editing gesture. A bounded VST control-rate scorer can precompute the seven templates; no allocation or file I/O in the audio callback. Not implemented as a plugin.

**Operational receipt (proposal).** Keep evidence identifier, cue extraction algorithm/version, relevance weights, candidate set, selected interpretation if any, and authorization as separate fields. An evidence digest alone does not identify the rule used to interpret evidence. A changed rule is not a changed measurement. A conventional typed ledger can implement all of this without cybersemiotics; no incremental Brier-derived mechanism has been established. Do not infer consent, care, or moral authority from a musical classifier.

**Falsification design, not executed.** Separate three conditions: identical target and different annotation instructions; different contexts with identical target; acoustically modified target. Include neutral framing and randomized order to mitigate carryover. Ask for grouping, confidence and optional abstention independently of preference; record response latency only with authorized participants. A null result, dominance by pitch or amplitude, or failure of the seven-template vocabulary to cover reported groupings is meaningful. Do not use a human's agreement with an authored template as evidence of moral improvement. No recruitment, submission or human validation is authorized.

## Checkpoint and negative sediment

The mathematical model has fixed coherent indices, binary authored masks, additive contrast and only seven templates. It does not infer a clock from audio, model cognition, remove uncertainty from a real alert, or claim optimal metric inference. The two-context editions intentionally differ in complete waveform, while the target alone does not. The moral analogy is a proposal, and the theological source cannot be reduced to a DSP method.
