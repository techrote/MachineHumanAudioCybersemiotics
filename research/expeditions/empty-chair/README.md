# The Choir That Sings to an Empty Chair

**2026-11-25 · Refs #602 · Exploratory humanities and artistic research · Draft/HOLD**

## Proposition: address is a musical dimension

A choir faces an empty chair. There is a perfectly good audience behind the choir. The conductor does not ask the chair to confirm receipt. Is this a failed concert?

If its score is an invocation rather than an instruction, it need not be. The chair is not the destination of a packet. It is the object around which the relationship between performers and witnesses is arranged. We might write an entire musical score in which who is spoken to, who is able to hear, and who is invited to respond are independently composed.

This is an interpretation and an artistic proposal. It does not establish that an acoustic listener can infer any of these roles.

## Essay: the third chair

The grammatical second person appears to point in a direction, yet a poet may speak to an urn while intending readers to receive the poem. The extraordinary thing about apostrophe is not that communication reaches an impossible recipient. It is that the named recipient organizes a real experience elsewhere. The poem need not be treated as a poorly functioning telephone call.

The same device could shape instrumental music. A call played toward a sealed cabinet may be addressed to the cabinet within the work, while the real acoustic audience is seated in another part of the gallery. Neither the cabinet nor the audience need be requested to answer. Move the cabinet, replace it with an unoccupied chair, or give it a name from a fictional language: the audience's account of the gesture may change. Whether it actually changes is an empirical question, not a property of the waveform.

A lyric reader may become the person speaking the address, as Culler's account suggests. A performer should therefore be allowed to migrate from witness to utterer. This is not equivalent to a network peer assuming a permission. The score's participant roles are part of its fiction and performance practice, not a security credential.

The Voyager greetings introduce a further wrinkle. The intended distant listener may be real but unknown, while the earthly audience exists in the circumstances of production. Reading Sagan's explanation, one sees that communicative accessibility to the imagined finder was not the only consideration: human plurality mattered to the design. This is an example of several purposes for one artefact, not proof that a difficult code is better. If the aim were uniquely successful decoding by an unknown recipient, a different record might have been produced.

Paterson offers a useful reversal. The Moon participates materially in the transformation of a message, but not necessarily as its intentional recipient. A return is produced by a physical arrangement. The musical performance incorporates absences in that return. To name the Moon as a collaborator is an artistic attribution; to infer from the returned radio signal that it understood Beethoven would be a category error. The artist's account is enough to situate the work; the exact loss mechanism is not assessed here.

A strange instrument would make both distinctions playable. Some addresses should reach a listener without obliging that listener to answer. Some should solicit a response that might not arrive. Some should be reflected without any respondent. A fourth should invite a witness to turn the address elsewhere rather than impersonate the object to which it was addressed.

This is a different use of silence from a rest between notes. A rest can be acoustically identical to the waiting interval of an unanswered question. The difference is not hidden energy or an inaudible frequency; it resides in the composition's relationship to a possible reply.

### The counterargument

Why not call this theatre with ordinary audio? That may be the correct classification. Spatial audio, cue sheets, stage directions, a delay line and a second performer already suffice. The proposed instrument is not a novel synthesis primitive. The artistic contribution would be a convenient way to edit the structure of address across many small sound processes.

Also, address without reply can be oppressive rather than liberating: a listener might be coerced into witnessing a discourse whose ostensible addressee has no opportunity to object. An aesthetic of unilateral speech should not become a justification for excluding actual users from repair or control. The score must permit refusal and withdrawal; it must not redefine silence as agreement.

## Constructed miniature: address and reply are separate axes

The executable [laboratory.py](laboratory.py) renders an original five-note motif at 0.5 s and, in three cases, a second phrase at 3 s. All files are mono PCM, 16 kHz, six seconds, independently readable WAVs. The following are stipulated fictional scenes, not observations:

| Scene | Invoked object | Reply contract | Return provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| invocation | empty chair | none | none |
| unanswered | fictional correspondent | required | none |
| reflection | stone | none | local delay copy (precomputed duplicate) |
| staged-reply | fictional correspondent | required | scripted reply |
| witness-variation | future archive | optional | scripted witness, changed motif |

All scenes are rendered by the same local script. Neither 'stone' nor 'correspondent' is a physical endpoint, and the two return provenances are score labels, not independent network processes or physical experiments. No replies are real. The staged reply does not establish verified uptake. The scripted witness is not a proxy authorized to speak for the future archive.

Two exact collisions are intentional. The invocation and unanswered request produce the same bytes; the reflection and staged reply also produce the same bytes. Consequently a deterministic classifier using just the recording cannot correctly distinguish both members of either pair under the stipulated labels. With equally weighted members, at most one of the two labels can be correct for that pair. This elementary identifiability observation is about deliberately constructed fixtures, not typical speech or human listeners. Context or distinct acoustic encodings can disambiguate. The fifth scene has a contrasting audible return; musical novelty does not establish a new respondent.

A synthetic status helper uses the separately supplied reply contract and return provenance to distinguish no request, optional reply absent, required reply outstanding/absent, and a staged response. These labels are intentionally not a production dialogue state machine. In particular, no caller should infer delivery, comprehension, consent or authority from them.

Run with Python 3.11+ standard library:

    python research/expeditions/empty-chair/laboratory.py

The output directory is generated locally; do not add generated WAVs to canonical records. See [receipt.txt](receipt.txt) for observed native execution and [SCORE.md](SCORE.md) for the performance work.

## Instrument proposal: the Apostrophe Organ

A three-layer score:

- **Invocation layer:** assign a target to a motif (object, abstraction, fictional character, unknown future listener, present performer, none). Allow mutable fictional names independent of actual routes.
- **Witness layer:** author bus routing, physical loudspeaker selection, optional private audition and opportunities to become the next utterer. Make these real routing controls distinct from the target's name.
- **Response layer:** configure no request, invitation or stipulated fictional request; compose return gestures as delay, resonant transformation, independent live performance, or silence. Record provenance separately from sound similarity. Do not invent machine or human understanding.

A practical version could use a small bank of resonators, spatial pan, delayed copies, event buffers, and a score editor with a persistent role graph. The musician could paint an evolving population of invocation targets. A group of oscillators might address a slow-moving imagined constellation; a witness bus receives their melody without becoming that constellation. Another group may re-utter selected phrases in its own timbre. The invocation graph can be changed without changing synthesis or routing. Later experiments can make target attributes modulate resonance, but distinguish symbolic assignment from physical simulation.

A VSTi could implement the original motifs and editable local response rules. A VSTfx could make a physically ordinary delay return available as an independently auditionable *reflection*, without classifying a return as a response. A DAW automation lane could separate the three score dimensions. No actual communications or authentication machinery is needed for this instrument.

Possible artwork: an installation with four named chairs, four transducers and one witness position. Each movement replaces one relation rather than one oscillator. The last movement removes all named objects while retaining their timbral traces. An audience may refuse the role of witness. No sensing, recording or participant research is implied.
