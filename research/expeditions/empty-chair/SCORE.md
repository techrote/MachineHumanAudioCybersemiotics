# Five Invitations to a Chair

Original performance score for a sound performer, a witness, a silent object and an optional second performer. The witness may decline. No live electronic transmission is necessary. The companion recordings are synthetic miniatures; they are not documentation of a performed installation.

## I. Invocation

Place a chair where a second performer would normally sit. Keep the witness in another part of the room. Play five notes toward the chair. No answer is requested. Do not lengthen the silence as punishment for the chair's lack of a response. End the movement by the clock.

## II. Unanswered

Repeat the identical five notes and silence, at identical times. This time the score stipulates a fictional request whose answer is due before the ending. Leave the reply absent. Do not claim that the real witness is the missing correspondent. The piece ends; the fictional request remains unanswered. The piece is not a failed performance.

## III. Mirror

Replace the chair with a stone. Play the five notes. Three seconds into the movement, present the original motif again as a declared local copy (the original begins half a second into the movement). Do not call it an answer from the stone. In a future physical version the stone may participate in an actual resonance; keep the present version's purely synthetic provenance explicit.

## IV. The scripted correspondent

Repeat movement III's exact acoustic sequence. Instead of naming the return as a copy, designate it as the utterance of a fictional correspondent. Its provenance is scripted, not a real reply. In a live realization a second musician may play it; the exact acoustic equality then need not hold. Put the two scores beside the same loudspeaker.

## V. A witness turns the phrase

Name a hypothetical future archive as the addressee. Invite the witness to re-perform the phrase in altered order, or to decline. The synthetic recording supplies one possible altered five-note phrase, not a prediction of a person's decision. Do not treat the witness as the archive. The invitation can change speakers without changing the named object.

## Coda (unrendered)

The conductor removes the chair, leaving its name written in the score. The witness becomes the speaker. The next performance is addressed to the space left by the chair, or to nobody. Stop whenever the performance should stop, without requiring the space to sign a receipt.

## Speculative miniature: the registrar

The registrar visited a concert and asked for a list of all failed calls. The musicians showed him a chair, a stone and an unopened letter addressed to the next century.

He filed the chair under Unresponsive, the stone under Answered, and the letter under Overdue.

The composer asked him to retain the records but change their column headings.

For the following concert he was asked to witness rather than adjudicate. He declined, and the score was performed without him.
