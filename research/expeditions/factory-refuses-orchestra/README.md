# The Factory That Refuses Its Orchestra

**Expedition #790 — exploratory humanities, aesthetic construction and synthetic audio.**
27 November 2026. AI-assisted source reading, interpretation, implementation and self-check by ChatGPT; no independent reviewer, participant, field measurement or empirical evidence promotion.

## The factory and the concert (original fiction)

A composer visits a factory and discovers a beautiful irregularity in the rhythm of a motor. The manager commissions a work based on it. The maintenance worker asks the composer not to obscure it with bells. A visitor asks for the bells alone. The composer produces a compromise, exactly halfway between both requests, and the manager calls it a universal soundtrack.

The maintenance worker replies that the music has acquired a new instrument: the person who decides whose listening counts. The visitor asks for a private audition. The motor continues without consenting to any of these descriptions.

This is a fictional scene, not a report about workers or industrial acoustics.

Russolo proposes expanding instrumental resources through the organization of noises, including mechanical and everyday sounds, and explicitly rejects mere imitation as the ultimate artistic end. His historical claim that preindustrial life was silent is manifesto rhetoric, not a verified history. The text also aestheticizes war; its celebration of mastery is not adopted here. ## Interpretation: the conductor as an administrator of audibility

The recurring movement in Russolo's manifesto is from an overlooked sound to a material the artist can manipulate. Yet the act of musical emancipation can coincide with the act of taking possession. The sound of a machine may be a useful clue to someone responsible for it, an unwanted interruption to somebody else, and a beautiful texture to a third person. None of those descriptions entails the others. The choice of which of them governs a shared sound system is not an acoustic property.

Neuhaus reverses the relocation: leave the sound where it is and relocate attention. But moving a listener is not always possible, and the listener who cannot leave a workplace is not in the same position as a visitor to a concert. The opposition between noise art and quietness is thus inadequate. Thompson's work makes an institutional question visible: who gets to select the listening conditions, who can decline, and who bears their consequences?

An interface might give each sound an audition identity, rather than one global signal/noise label. It could mark a contribution as target material for one explicitly configured receiver and extraneous to another. This would not be a diagnosis of either listener's mental state. The designation would describe a task and an authored mixing contract. A visually noisy display can be useful; an elegant harmonic chord can be irrelevant. In particular a machine that avoids performed human emotion has not thereby abolished affective consequences.

The proposition is not that every person needs their own loudspeaker. It is that the decision to collapse several requirements into one public signal should remain inspectable. In an artwork the incompatibility can be staged; in an operational system it must not be hidden by a claim of universal adequacy.

## A miniature formal counter-concert (synthetic construction)

Let a and b be different known, equal-duration real sample vectors: two desired auditions. One identical waveform y must be supplied to both fictional recipients. Define E_a = ||y-a||² and E_b = ||y-b||², with D = ||a-b||². This is a stipulated sample-domain objective, not a psychoacoustic distance or a model of human welfare.

Writing m=(a+b)/2, expanding squares gives E_a+E_b=2||y-m||²+D/2. Thus max(E_a,E_b) >= D/4, with equality uniquely at m. This holds even for waveforms not made by mixing the two stems. If the acceptable squared error for each target is less than D/4, no common waveform meets both. Separate exact auditions remove this particular common-output restriction; they do not guarantee safe levels, intelligibility, access or consent.

For y=(1-t)a+t b, the normalized errors are t² and (1-t)². Every point on this segment is Pareto efficient: any waveform closer to both endpoints would violate the triangle inequality. Any point outside the segment is dominated by its Euclidean projection onto the segment, which decreases at least one distance and cannot increase the other. A musician can perform the tradeoff; equal error is merely one chosen policy.

The impossibility disappears if a=b. It also does not apply to independent spatial channels, unequal receiver transfer functions or a task that permits a different target. These are model changes, not refutations. No novelty is claimed for elementary Euclidean geometry.

## Six editions (original artistic score)

The sound materials are invented: a continuous mechanical bed and recurrent resonant bell-like gestures, not recorded machinery or alarms. The two fictional recipients request one isolated stem each.

| Edition | Motor gain | Bell gain | Instruction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Motor | 1 | 0 | Attend to continuity. |
| Bell | 0 | 1 | Attend to recurrence. |
| Motor priority | 0.75 | 0.25 | Hear recurrence as an interruption, then as accompaniment. |
| Shared midpoint | 0.5 | 0.5 | Assign the same file two contradictory programme notes. |
| Bell priority | 0.25 | 0.75 | Attend to the interval between gestures. |
| Full mixture | 1 | 1 | Refuse the presumption that adding everything is neutral. |

The midpoint is one file, not two independent performances. Play it twice if desired; do not assume the two listening instructions cause perceptually different experiences. A seventh edition may consist of a listener's decision not to audition anything. Do not turn the absence of a listener into approval.

The score is intended for voluntary, private, comfortable-level audition. It is not an instruction to reproduce industrial noise, alter warning systems, or impose sounds on people.

## Instrument proposals and experiments (unvalidated)

**Counter-Concert mixer (VSTfx):** accept two explicit input stems, editable target gain vectors, independent preview buses and a common output bus. Show actual gains and normalized sample error separately from the user's labels. A comparison switch should preserve source material; no inference of target identity from arbitrary mixtures. Use smoothing, bounded gain and host-qualified bypass in any future real-time implementation.

**Audition Atlas (CYD):** X selects the relative weights assigned to two explicit auditions; Y moves between the independently requested preview and the shared mix. A split display marks two desired gain vectors and the actual vector. The display should retain the disagreement rather than manufacture a composite approval. No embedded implementation is claimed.

**Fictional orchestra (FM-1 / VSTi):** define groups of oscillators whose desired audiences are part of the patch. A performer can compose the routing itself. The musical material is the relationship among simultaneously proposed mixes, not a simulated measurement of preference.

A prospective MHAC experiment could vary target compatibility, shared versus selectable delivery, and clearly typed scope labels. Compare against ordinary text and independently selectable audio buses, not an artificially weak baseline. Measure target-specific identification and task performance separately from annoyance, workload, and preference. Do not equate sample error with any of those outcomes. Preserve negative controls: an unrelated but pleasant melody, two compatible targets, a different receiver transfer function, a missing target, and a listener opting out. No experiment involving people is authorized here.

## Counterarguments and next rabbit hole

The mathematics is deliberately impoverished. A compromise may be an excellent new artwork even when it is a poor reproduction of both requested targets. An exact replica may be inaccessible to a listener, and a small waveform error may destroy a relevant semantic distinction. A private channel is not automatically a remedy: it may isolate people, mask external events or be unavailable. Neither artists nor engineers should infer the right to occupy a space from the aesthetic quality of their output.

The next investigation could treat a room as a collection of incompatible listening practices rather than a single receiver: separately configurable tasks, alternative modalities, changing demands and listener-controlled refusal. A conventional typed scope and routing interface may solve the operational problem; no acoustic advantage has been demonstrated.
