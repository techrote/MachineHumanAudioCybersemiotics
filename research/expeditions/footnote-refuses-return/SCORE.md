# Three readers, one closing bell

Original unperformed score for a principal player, three excursion players and optional witness. A metronome or conductor may mark equal beats. Use comfortable sound levels; choose any instruments. For electronic realization, use quiet sine tones with short ramps. Every opening sounds the same brief low tone (L), every close the same brief high tone (H). No pre-existing music required.

## I. The footnote as a room

The principal player repeats four notes of their own choosing. At beats 1,3,5 play L and at 2,4,6 play H. Each open starts a new numbered excursion, each close retires it. There is never more than one active excursion. A witness may observe that the label is unnecessary for this restricted rule. Repeat with audible principal voice, then with it muted; the anchors still exist in the written score.

## II. The footnote as a corridor

Repeat the six-beat skeleton L L L H H H. Open A, B, C in order. In version N close C,B,A. In version X close A,C,B. The principal and bell tracks must remain identical. In the first rendition excursion players enact their assignments silently, e.g. by turning numbered cards; the witness receives only the bells. No claim about the witness's perception is made.

In the second rendition, while each excursion is active sustain a quiet distinct pitch (choose 220, 275 and 330 Hz, or three comfortable notes). Fade each addressed voice at its own close; other voices continue. Listen to different lifetimes, not different bell inventories. Do not make one ordering correct by default. Pause between versions.

## III. The reader who continues

Repeat the principal phrase continuously. Give A an attachment to the second note of phrase one; B an attachment to A. Close A while B remains audible, explicitly authorizing B to remain. In a second version B must retire with A, and in a third B is promoted to a principal voice. These are separate composed outcomes, not a model of automatic inference. The performer of B may decline promotion. No concealed closure should be considered acknowledged by the witness.

## IV. A footnote to the final rest

Open three excursions, sound only two closures, then stop the bell sequence. In this artistic movement the remaining excursion may decay, continue, or be silently abandoned by its player. This deliberately violates the balanced-word model; it must not be fed to the oracle as a valid complete trace. The principal player may end before all subsidiary voices do. No mandatory explanatory finale. End when all players choose to stop, not when a machine asserts that the questions are answered.

Optional rehearsal experiment: arrange numbered cards beside the bell score, compare stack, queue and freely addressed closures. Use the same principal phrase and bell sequence. Keep the projection and explicit labels separately inspectable. No human data collection or empirical validation is part of this performance instruction.
