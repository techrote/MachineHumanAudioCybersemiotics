# The Choir That Hides Its Fourth Singer

Expedition #522, 2026-11-25. Synthetic mathematics and digital audio, not a canonical evidence record. Same-agent proof, implementation and source inspection; no participant study or independent critique.

## Anomaly and model

The earlier duet expedition (#514) recognizes individual binary fields but loses their sender binding. Here k anonymous, stable senders each carry an m-bit word. Repeated cooperative queries return exact joint counts for any subset of at most r fields, without persistent sender IDs. The population is a multiset; duplicate words are allowed. Counts are reliable, and all senders contribute equally unless stated otherwise. The target is the unordered multiset, not identity attribution.

For k=4, m=3, the even-parity choir {000,011,101,110} and the odd-parity choir {001,010,100,111} have identical one- and two-field histograms. Each single field is balanced and each pair shows all four possible states exactly once. Yet no full message is shared. With three senders, such an obstruction is impossible at order two.

Let N(x) be the integer multiplicity of x, and D=N-N' for two candidates. Agreement on all low-order joint histograms is equivalent by inclusion-exclusion to agreement on all all-one subset counts, or by the invertible Walsh transform to vanishing Fourier coefficients of D through degree r (including degree zero, the total). A nonzero D is precisely a combinatorial [r]-trade. The minimum and equality structure below are established mathematics; the adaptive fibre and audit interpretation is the local MHAC derivation, not a claim of literature priority.

## Sharp threshold and proof

For a nonzero signed difference D, choose a set S of minimum degree d with nonzero Walsh coefficient. All proper-subset coefficients vanish. Its marginal on S is therefore a nonzero multiple of the parity character: each of its 2^d cells equals +a or -a, according to alternating parity. In the integer setting a is a nonzero integer in magnitude. At least 2^(d-1) positive and 2^(d-1) negative units of mass are necessary, since these many marginal cells are positive and negative respectively. Since d>=r+1, each leg contains at least 2^r units. In fact each sign must have at least that many distinct support points, even for real weights.

For r<m the bound is attained by opposite parity classes on r+1 coordinates, with all other coordinates fixed. Identical padding extends a counterexample to every k>=2^r. Therefore, for k>=1, the exact minimum uniform observation order is min(m, 1+floor(log2 k)). Order zero is insufficient when m>=1. When r=m the full histogram trivially identifies the population.

The support-count proof has a stronger audit interpretation: if a positive weighted measure has fewer than 2^r distinct words, no other nonnegative weighted measure, however dense, has the same low-order counts. Total mass must be included. The number of distinct points in the positive leg of any signed difference cannot exceed the actual support. Thus real weights do NOT destroy this exact threshold (a useful correction to the initial question). They do destroy the integer count margin: an arbitrarily small parity trade can be added to a common background, making all differences arbitrarily small. Even without a background, small total mass makes unnormalized differences small; with normalized total and k>=2^r+1 a fixed common background suffices. No practical stability threshold follows from formal uniqueness.

## Equality: the only minimal camouflage

At k=2^r, any distinct integer candidates sharing r-order tables must be disjoint, with no repeated word. In the minimal projection above, d=r+1 and |a|=1. There is exactly one full word per projected assignment, and the legs are the two parity classes. Every additional coordinate is a Boolean function f(y) of the d projected bits. For each subset T of at most r-1 base coordinates, the signed moment involving this extra coordinate and the Walsh character of T vanishes. Writing signs as +/-1, this says the Fourier coefficient of f at the complement of T vanishes. Hence f has no coefficients of degree >=2. A Boolean function of real Fourier degree <=1 is constant or a single input bit up to complement: squaring f and comparing the degree-two coefficients shows at most one linear coefficient is nonzero; the constant-times-linear terms then rule out a simultaneous nonzero constant. This proves the complete equality structure, including multiplicities. It is the block form of Ghorbani et al., Lemma 4.

Equivalently partition the varying coordinates into d nonempty blocks. Each block copies or complements a single latent bit. All other coordinates are fixed. The two legs correspond to opposite parity of the d latent bits. Conversely every such block construction gives a minimum trade: any r fields touch at most r blocks, so at least one latent bit remains unobserved and parity is balanced.

## The two-candidate theorem and adaptive repair

For r>=2, k=2^r, every observational fibre contains either one or two multisets, regardless of m. Proof: if two exist, the equality structure applies. Each nonconstant coordinate is balanced. Two nonconstant columns are either identical/complementary on the entire population, or jointly uniform, since r>=2 and distinct latent bits have uniform pairwise tables. Thus the one- and two-field tables recover the fixed coordinates, the partition into blocks, and each complement orientation relative to its block representative. Any candidate pair in the same fibre must use exactly this same structure. It follows that only the two parity halves can occur; both are already present. This argument is for exact tables, not noisy estimation.

Algorithm: recover constants and groups from the pair tables, reject the minimum-trade template if any singleton is not constant/balanced, or any candidate cross-pair is not uniform/identical/complementary as appropriate. Require exactly r+1 groups; construct both parity halves and compare every supplied moment. If both match, the fibre has exactly these two candidates. If not, the fibre is a singleton assuming the input came from a valid k-word population. The algorithm is an ambiguity detector and an ambiguous-case decoder, not an efficient general unique-case solver. Choose one representative from each block and ask every sender for its XOR over these representatives. The count is either zero or k: one noiseless binary distinction separates the two full populations. This new query is a cooperative statement, not recoverable from the original aggregate recording.

For r=1 the theorem fails: k=2, s varying columns give 2^(s-1) complementary pairs, as #514 showed. The third-field joint observation is the first case of the two-candidate effect.

For r>=2 and exactly 2^r positive support points per candidate with real weights, equality in the support bound forces one point per cell, common magnitude a, disjoint supports and therefore uniform weights whenever there are two distinct candidates. Unequal weights can consequently rule out a second equally sparse candidate, but not an unrestricted dense one. At the threshold a uniform parity leg is mimicked by the half-and-half mixture of both parity legs, with twice as many distinct words. Even a nonuniform positive weighting on that parity leg can be changed by subtracting a sufficiently small uniform trade and adding its opposite leg. This is not a guarantee of auditability at threshold.

## Counting hidden fibres and the price of precommitment

With r>=2, d=r+1, m>=d, the exact count of ambiguous fibres at k=2^r is

    A(m,r) = 2^(m-d) * S(m+1,d+1),

where S(n,j) is a Stirling number of the second kind. Choose v varying coordinates, partition them into d nonempty unlabelled blocks, select each block's relative complement pattern (2^(v-d) choices) and the constant values (2^(m-v) choices). Distinct choices give distinct pair tables and hence distinct fibres. The sum over v of C(m,v)*S(v,d) equals S(m+1,d+1), by adjoining a dummy element to the block of constant coordinates. This is an exact count of fibres, not an estimate of how often real devices encounter one.

A local probe of exactly d coordinates distinguishes the two legs iff it takes one coordinate from every block. This applies to the full joint table, the all-one count, or the XOR count. If a block is missed, both projected distributions are identical. If every block is hit, their projections are opposite parity classes. Therefore there are exactly the product of the d block sizes informative d-local probes for that fibre.

An adaptive strategy needs only one such probe in the worst case, following the reliable r-order tables. A predetermined dictionary of d-local probes that works for every possible population must include all C(m,d) subsets: for each subset create singleton latent blocks on exactly that subset and fix every other field. No different d-local probe separates that collision. The full dictionary suffices. If t supplementary scalar symbol substitutions and e flagged erasures are allowed, repeating the informative adaptive probe q=2t+e+1 times is necessary and sufficient in the fixed-after-baseline query model (the two possible receipt strings must differ in at least q positions). The universal d-local dictionary needs q copies of every subset. This is not a bound on unrestricted higher-weight queries, or on faulted baseline tables.

There is an unusual conditioning benefit at the exact integer boundary: a representative XOR produces all k votes on one side or all on the other, while any selected full d-table differs by only one count per cell. Normalized parity-count separation is one. A scalar bounded additive error strictly below k/2 preserves distinction for that selected parity probe. At equality the midpoint is ambiguous. This is a count-level guarantee only, not an acoustic SNR or psychoacoustic result.

## Executable checks and the Four-Singer Organ

The JavaScript laboratory enumerates all multisets for three to five fields with two or three/four senders, plus all simple eight-word populations on four fields. Repeated words at the eight-word threshold are not enumerated in that last profile; the equality theorem rules out their ambiguity. It checks the detector against actual fibres, the Stirling formula, and every possible d-local probe in ambiguous fibres. A further 63,010 exact comparisons establish singleton-block omitted-probe witnesses for fields up to nine. The separately executed native Python implementation uses literal complete histogram cells instead of all-one moments. Neither computation constitutes independent peer review.

Run:
    
    node laboratory.cjs
    python witness.py

The second generates four mono PCM16 8 kHz files in results/. Each sender emits the same integer tapered pulse at 250 or 500 Hz for its XOR response. For the two three-field parity choirs, first request the three singletons and three pair XORs. Each returns exactly two high and two low votes; raw WAVs are byte-identical. Append the three-way XOR and the final slot is unanimous low versus unanimous high. Files are original and generated, not checked-in third-party recordings. This score is a narrower receipt system than full joint histograms in general; with known total and singleton counts, pair XOR counts recover pair tables. For higher orders the laboratory uses full joint tables; do not equate a single XOR of a subset with its entire table without the lower-order information.

Potential instrument: one performer selects hidden choir assignments, one chooses the maximum queried order, and a third can insert the representative parity question. A separate source-resolved audition could expose the discarded binding. The original PCM rendering and census are implemented; live performance interface and listening study are not.
