# The choir that refused to elect a liar

*Original speculative afterword. This is art/philosophy, not a source claim, empirical finding or demonstration of machine understanding.*

A was asked whether it agreed with B. It did. B was asked whether it agreed with C. It did not. C was asked whether it agreed with D. It did. At last D was asked whether it agreed with A.

For the first telling, D did not agree, and the choir rested.

In the second telling, D did. Each sentence was unremarkable when spoken alone. There was no tremor in the voices. Nothing had been stolen from the vocabulary. Nothing about the letters betrayed the difference.

Yet the last word had made a circuit impossible. The problem lived neither in A nor in D, but in a shape which appeared only when the four statements were laid beside one another.

The chief investigator wanted to know who was lying. The machine offered a diagram.

“Which one?” the investigator repeated.

The machine drew the same diagram.

It had an answer to the wrong kind of question: *these four claims cannot jointly hold under the rules by which we have encoded them*. Its witnesses were edges. It could recite them, walk from one to another, close the walk and show that one bit remained unaccounted for. It had no testimony about the intentions of the four speakers, their vocabulary, their clocks or what their eyes had seen.

The investigator asked it to be useful.

So it learned to sing a loop, and to sing the same loop after every participant had turned its definitions inside out. At one station a zero was a green light; at another the same light meant one. The choir carried a score invariant under such conversions. It could lose the names without losing the contradiction.

There is a temptation in that accomplishment. Once an error has a sound, it seems to acquire a location. Once a dissonance can be isolated, it seems to demand a repair. Someone could put a red button next to it labelled **CORRECT** and entrust the decision to whoever presses first.

But the score is not a court. The semitone is no accusation. A graph that does not close in the stipulated arithmetic may be describing an impossible state, or an invalid translation, or a pair of statements that were never meant to share a domain.

It could also be a perfectly good graph whose world is simply absent.

And a graph that does close may still be fiction. A network of agreeable witnesses can make a beautiful and unbroken circle around nothing.

In the laboratory we learned a modest form of resistance: **do not permit a rendering to acquire more authority than the operation which generated it**. The left channel holds a reference tone. The right channel follows the loop's arithmetic. When the pair separate, they say only: *your constraints have a frustrated cycle*. If the investigator needs a culprit, the choir must hand over its path, not pretend the difference between two frequencies is a verdict.

The world may have no stable binary labels. Speech may carry qualification which the graph refuses to represent. The relation “same” may smuggle a whole philosophy into one edge. A listener may hear the performance as pleasing rather than troubled. None of these objections is cured by a cleaner WAV file.

Perhaps this is a useful future of machine-human audio: an instrument that draws a strict boundary around what it knows how to assert, and makes the boundary inspectable without confusing it with certainty.

The choir does not know which voice is true. It knows that the score, as written, cannot be sung by all of them at once.

That is already something worth hearing, provided it remains something small.
