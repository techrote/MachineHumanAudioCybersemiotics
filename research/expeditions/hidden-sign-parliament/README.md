## 1. Anomaly

The internal predecessor #412, The Chord That Outruns Its Correlator, proved a useful saturation result: in dimension two, two linearly independent cyclic phase relations of l1 norm at most r imply that the entire relation lattice is generated by vectors of that norm. Its rounding proof does not extend unchanged to higher dimensions. We now build a family where even **every** third-order relation is insufficient despite full real rank. There are exponentially many isolated phase configurations, not a continuous null direction.

In this report the domain is the finite group Z/NZ, not a continuous time circle. A fixed positive DC and conjugate paired nonzero Fourier coefficients define a real signal. Choose DC greater than twice the sum of the positive frequency magnitudes if positivity is required. The DFT uses inverse expansion exp(2 pi i a t/N), with normalized forward transform. Complete cyclic third-order correlations are equivalent to the bispectrum, whose coordinates are F_a F_b conjugate(F_(a+b)); all indices are modulo N. Positive DC supplies all magnitudes through coordinates with a zero argument. No measurement of a zero coefficient is informative.

For two matching spectra, phase ratios u_a have modulus one, u_0=1 and u_(-a)=conjugate(u_a). The bispectral equations are u_a u_b=u_(a+b) wherever all three indices are supported. This is the finite analogue of Jaming–Kolountzakis equation (2.3). The real-valued phase relation matrix has integer rows, but equations hold **modulo 2 pi**, not over R. Full rank is only a local isolation condition. A finite index of the generated integer lattice within the translation-relation lattice still gives global ambiguity. See #412 section 2 for the full relation-lattice criterion.

## 2. Construction

**Theorem A.** For integer r>=3 set N=3*2^r and let J be any subset of {0,...,r-3} with no consecutive indices. Write k=|J|. Use the positive-frequency support

    B = {1,2,4,...,2^r}
    a_j = N/2 - 2^j   (j in J)
    A = B union {a_j : j in J}.

Include negative frequencies and DC, with no Nyquist coefficient. All elements of A are distinct and below N/2. For every choice of positive magnitudes and fixed positive DC, a complete bispectrum has exactly 2^k distinct phase configurations **modulo integer cyclic translations**. The relation matrix has full column rank r+1+k. The phase fiber before quotienting has N*2^k members.

Proof: the base relations u_(2^(i+1))=u_(2^i)^2 and u_(2^r)^3=1 (the latter since 2^r+2^r=-2^r modulo N) force u_1^N=1. After dividing by the corresponding physical cyclic translation, all base ratios equal one. The relation 2a_j=-2^(j+1) modulo N forces u_(a_j)^2=1. Thus there are at most 2^k residual sign assignments.

To show there are no further constraints, classify zero-sum triples of signed supported indices, allowing repetition and zeros. Signed extra indices are congruent to N/2 +/- 2^j. One extra cannot equal a sum of two signed base powers: its circular distance exceeds 2^r. The only way for two base powers to produce a positive representative above 2^r is for both to be positive with at least one equal to 2^r, or both negative followed by wrap. Equivalently one must express either N/2 - 2^j or N/2 + 2^j as a positive sum of two powers. After subtracting 2^r the required second power is 2^(r-1) +/- 2^j, neither a power for j<=r-3. Two copies of 2^r do not fit either. With two extras from distinct indices i,j the remaining signed sum modulo N has magnitude |2^i +/- 2^j|; neither is a power of two because their exponent difference is at least two (and both are small relative to N). Two extras from the same index always have even sign multiplicity. Three extras have residue near N/2 and cannot sum to zero since 3*2^(r-3)<N/2. Hence every triple contains each extra an even number of times. Arbitrary sign flips preserve all bispectral coordinates, including zero ones.

Full rank follows independently: in the lifted real equations the r doubling rows, the top row with coefficient 3, and the k rows with coefficient 2 on each extra give an independent triangular system after ordering the base ascending. Its determinant has magnitude N*2^k. These constraints already give the exact finite solution count, so adding the other triple rows cannot change the count. The nonzero fundamental makes the physical translation action free. QED.

Maximal packing J={0,2,4,...} yields k=floor((r-1)/2). This is exponential ambiguity in the number of independently hidden signs, despite full real rank and a nonzero fundamental. Examples:

The exact Smith invariants from exploratory SymPy calculation are 1,1,1,2,24. We do not assert that 24 is the smallest period or 5 the smallest dimension admitting any such obstruction.

## 3. Fourth-order receipts

**Theorem B.** On the bispectrum fiber of Theorem A, every nonzero fourth-order Fourier product has normalized relative sign either 1, s_j or s_i*s_j, with s_j in {+1,-1}. Each singleton and each distinct pair is realized. For k>0 the exact full cyclic correlation order needed for reconstruction up to translation is four. Exactly k adaptively or nonadaptively chosen single complex product coordinates suffice and are necessary in the worst case, assuming the complete bispectrum is already known.

Proof: the invariant products contain four signed indices summing to zero mod N. They cannot contain three extras and a base (including zero), since their circular sum before adding the base has magnitude at least N/2 - 3*2^(r-3)>2^r. With four extras the sum reduces to four signed powers 2^j. It must be zero as an integer, since the magnitude is bounded by 4*2^(r-3)<N. Distinct selected powers differ by a factor of at least four. The largest distinct power cannot be canceled by three smaller powers unless another copy of the largest occurs with opposing sign; removing such a pair leaves a canceling pair. Thus all extra multiplicities are even. At most two extras contribute nontrivially to a fourth-order product.

All the allowed masks occur: the singleton relation is

    a_j + 2^j - 2^r - 2^(r-1) = 0.

The pair relation is

    a_i - a_j + 2^i - 2^j = 0.

Each gives one exact sign bit relative to a fixed representative of the bispectrum fiber. A product containing any unsupported frequency is zero and reveals no sign. DC and conjugate cancellation add no information. Hence k singleton products recover all signs; even an adaptive decision tree of fewer than k binary observations has fewer than 2^k leaves. This also proves exact order four for k>0 (k=0 needs no repair). QED.

The distinction is between a whole fourth-order correlation tensor and a selected Fourier product: the lower bound is for individual invariant coordinates, not arbitrary analog combinations of multiple coordinates.

## 4. Fault tolerance

The available nontrivial quartic coordinates form the complete set of edges on k+1 vertices: vertex zero is an anchored sign +1, and vertices 1..k are the hidden signs. An edge reports the product of its endpoint signs. This is an application of the signed graph idea from #372, now derived from the spectral support rather than chosen freely.

**Theorem C.** Given a fixed selection of quartic receipts (parallel edges allowed for repeated observations), its exact bit-code minimum distance is the graph's minimum nontrivial cut size. It uniquely corrects e arbitrary wrong receipt bits and f flagged erasures iff every cut contains at least D=2e+f+1 edges.

Proof: two sign assignments differ on a subset of the unanchored vertices. Their edge product sequences differ precisely on the boundary cut. All nonempty proper cuts arise, up to complement. The usual Hamming-ball separation condition proves sufficiency and necessity: with distance <=2e+f, erase up to f differing coordinates and divide the rest between the two corruption balls. A disconnected graph has distance zero. The anchor removes the otherwise invisible global sign. QED.

For k>=1 and v=k+1, the sharp minimum scheduled receipt count with repetitions allowed is

    v-1                         if D=1,
    ceil(v*D/2)                 if D>=2.

Lower bounds: a connected graph needs v-1 edges; for D>=2 every vertex degree is at least D. Attainment: take a spanning tree when D=1; for v=2 use D parallel edges. For v>=3 and even D, use D/2 copies of a cycle. For odd D>=3, use floor(D/2) copies of the cycle and a long-chord edge cover of size ceil(v/2): for m=floor(v/2), add edges i to i+m for 0<=i<m if v is even, or 0<=i<=m if v is odd. Every vertex is covered and each chord's shorter cyclic distance is m. If a cut has at least four edges of the base cycle then its multiplicity is already >=D. If it has exactly two cycle edges, the smaller side is a contiguous arc of at most m vertices. Every vertex there has a chord leaving the arc. Thus the added cover supplies at least one crossing, giving D. Edge count is the lower bound. QED.

Example with eight hidden signs: ordinary recovery uses 8 coordinates. One erasure requires 9 scheduled receipts (cycle), one error requires 14 (three-edge-connected construction), one error plus one erasure requires 18. Repetitions count as separate received coordinates; correlated failures of repeated measurements do not obey the stipulated e/f model. If every coordinate is allowed only once, not all D are feasible: the complete simple graph has distance k. The sharp repeated-receipt formula is not claimed as a bound on microphone samples, physical acquisition time, or general noisy analog estimation. Nearest codeword decoding can be exponential; no polynomial-time arbitrary error decoder is claimed.

## 6. Interpretation and negative sediment

- **Proof, not perception:** the chosen invariant cannot identify the phase family. A listener, raw waveform comparator, nonlinear transducer, or different descriptor might distinguish them. No auditory indistinguishability or human comprehension claim follows.
- **Full rank is not identifiability:** the ordinary real linearized rank test ignores the index of an integer subgroup. This failure is global and finite, not a tangent-space null direction. A Smith-normal-form audit is more appropriate for phase constraints modulo 2 pi. The #412 two-tone theorem remains correct; no claim of a smallest counterexample is made.
- **Parity has a physical price:** these hidden bins sit near Nyquist. For the continuous circle, wraparound constraints are absent, so the exact discrete count need not apply to a continuous-time observation model. Zero-sum nonwrapped products are a subset of the cyclic products, so the sign collisions themselves persist for the ideal trigonometric polynomials. One must not generalize the cyclic completeness theorem to the continuous model.
- **A bad support selection:** adjacent j indices produce a two-extra difference equal to a base frequency, tying their signs. For example choosing j=0 and 1 in r=4 creates a relation a_0-a_1=1. This is why the no-consecutive condition matters. Allowing all near-Nyquist bins does not automatically maximize independent ambiguity.
- **Analog versus hard bits:** exact products supply parity bits. The graph code model begins after sign extraction; low magnitudes, uncertain reference phases or correlated estimation errors may invalidate its error budget. The observed PCM discrepancy is not a calibrated noise model.
- **Design hypothesis:** an MHAC descriptor inspector could first build integer phase relations, report rank and finite orbit count separately, then choose a sparse fourth-order receipt graph. The practical baseline remains retaining the full sampled waveform or a phase-registered complex spectrum when permissible. No cybersemiotic explanatory increment beyond harmonic analysis and coding theory is established.

**Speculative instrument — the Parliament Organ.** Let a performer turn hidden upper-partial signs while an observer hears the raw chord and sees a frozen third-order readout. A separate control requests a fourth-order receipt: an anchored sign or pair vote. In a noisy setting, additional votes can be commissioned according to the graph code. This is a proposed live interface; only the offline specimens and compiler are implemented.

**Original fiction.** The clerk insists that every member has spoken because his ledger has full rank. The apprentice asks each member to vote with one neighbour. The votes change, although the ledger does not.
