# Two doors to the same room

Original text score, 2026-11-24. Two performers (keyboard and room), one optional observer. Use a spreadsheet, counters, or a script; no microphone, amplified feedback, recording or physical force required. All numbers are abstract state values. A silent visual realization is equally valid. This is not a reconstruction of Lucier, Tudor or Di Scipio.

## I — Sale by demonstration
Set A: (a,b,c,d)=(2,1,4,2)/8. Set B: (2,4,1,2)/8. Reset both states to zero. Play one keyboard impulse, then six ticks of rest. At each tick let x govern the amplitude of a gentle fixed-pitch tone, or show x as a bar. Use a fixed gain shared by both realizations. Repeat with B. Both x sequences agree. Do not normalize the individual phrases.

## II — The unadvertised door
Repeat with the same initial state and a single unit impulse at the environmental port instead of the keyboard. A and B now sound different. Keep all mapping and timing identical. The room performer may subsequently issue an agreed finite sequence of signed inputs; use a visual sign indicator if negative amplitudes are otherwise obscured. The keyboard performer is not entitled to substitute a different input for that performer without agreement.

## III — Transfer of ownership
The observer receives only the first movement's x sequence and chooses a preferred instrument, or refuses to choose. Now disclose the coefficients, both inputs and both internal states. This is a demonstration of information availability, not a test of the observer's competence. The players exchange environmental roles without changing the calibration. Repeat the second movement. The observer may listen, leave, or decline the disclosure.

## IV — The detachable room
Introduce a switch which disconnects both off-diagonal couplings. Let the players decide whether to preserve the old one-port response using a prerecorded or calculated substitute, preserve the two-port contract with a virtual environment, or abandon the old repertoire. Do not call these options equivalent. Finish when either performer requests it. A quiet ending is permitted.

## Fiction — Receipt for an incomplete shipment

The museum purchased an instrument from a town which no longer existed.

The delivery included a narrow cabinet and a roll of music. The cabinet played everything on the roll. At the unveiling the curator invited a visitor to knock on its side, as visitors had done in the town. The cabinet did not answer.

A technician offered to add a sample of the old knock. The visitor asked whether the cabinet would answer a knock she had not made yet. The technician withdrew the offer.

The museum's inventory eventually contained a fourth item: a description of the exchange that had once occurred between the cabinet and its surroundings.

It was not an inventory of the surroundings. It was an account of two ways of disturbing the same silence.

At the next unveiling there was a second door. It could be opened, left shut, or disconnected. On its inside the visitor found a label: DO NOT CONFUSE A ROOM WITH ITS IMPULSE RESPONSE.

The cabinet had not been made complete. The terms of its incompleteness had become part of its score.
