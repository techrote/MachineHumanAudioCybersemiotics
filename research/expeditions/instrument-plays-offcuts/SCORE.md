# The second workshop — a score for an accepted sound and its offcut

Original text, 25 November 2026. Unperformed. Two audio performers and an optional collector. The native renderer supplies a short demonstration, not a recording of this score. Use software only; moderate monitoring gain, no uncontrolled feedback. Do not modify instruments or electrical equipment.

## I. The commission

Performer P selects a short melody and clips it digitally at a moderate threshold. Performer R monitors the exact difference between the input and clipped output. Route the principal sound to the audience; keep the residual private. Allow the collector to request silence in either monitor. Repeat without increasing the level.

## II. A second object from the same cut

Fix the principal melody. R may change the magnitude of excess only at samples on its clipping boundaries and only in the sign of the boundary. The software verifies that the principal signal is unchanged. Perform two versions, with slow and quick articulation of the excess. Audition principal and residual separately at a fixed monitor gain; do not call a difference in apparent loudness a preference result.

## III. The scrap dealer

R selects three short fragments of the residual, rearranges them and routes them to a separate musical voice. P continues unchanged. The collector marks the new voice as transformed material rather than an exact complement. At least one fragment must be discarded rather than reused. Do not require all sound to justify its survival.

## IV. The second cut

Process one offcut again. Audition the nested remainder and the accepted fragment. If desired, reconstruct the original from the untouched buses using sufficient headroom. Then deliberately abandon reconstruction and perform the transformed residues alone. End by fading both buses, or by leaving the final phrase uncollected.

## Fiction: The municipal workshop

The town ordered a machine to cut perfectly square holes into its music.

A second workshop opened next door. It sold the pieces removed by the first. The inspector asked which workshop had produced the defects.

The first showed its square holes. The second showed its little squares. Both were within specification.

The next morning a third workshop collected only the shadows of the squares. It was obliged to explain that the shadows were not parts of the original sheet.

The town's last commission requested a hole without a souvenir. The second workshop took the afternoon off.
