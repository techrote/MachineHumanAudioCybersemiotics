## Distributed cognition and active externalism

### X004-S01 — Edwin Hutchins (2008)

Edwin Hutchins, “The role of cultural practices in the emergence of modern human intelligence,” Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 363, 2011–2019. DOI 10.1098/rstb.2008.0003.

Accessible author/course-hosted PDF: https://pages.ucsd.edu/~scoulson/Courses/200/Hutchins_2008.pdf

Actually inspected: first published page/abstract and opening discussion, including the distributed-cognition premise and the explicit warning that cognitive properties belonging to a larger distributed system can be mistakenly attributed to an individual. The PDF first page was visually inspected.

Use here: a strong conceptual warning against saying “an agent developed X” when the observed stability may belong to the coupled system of model, repository, instructions, sources and human steering.

Limit: Hutchins is theorizing human/non-human cognition and cultural practices, not LLM repositories. Transfer is an analogy/research lens, not source-reported evidence about MHAC.

### X004-S02 — Andy Clark & David Chalmers (1998)

Andy Clark and David Chalmers, “The Extended Mind,” Analysis 58:10–23 (1998). Author-hosted paper index and draft:
https://consc.net/ai-and-computation/
https://consc.net/papers/extendedminddraft2.pdf

Actually inspected: author site description of “active externalism” and the draft's opening framing that environmental structures can play an active role in cognitive processes.

Use here: asks whether a persistent external record can become functionally integrated into reasoning.

Limit: X004 does not establish the parity, availability or coupling conditions needed to classify MHAC as a literal extended mind. There is no persistent single biological subject, and successive model instances can be discontinuous.

## Cultural attraction and reconstructed transmission

### X004-S03 — Andrew Buskell (2017)

Andrew Buskell, “What are cultural attractors?”, Biology & Philosophy 32:377–394. DOI 10.1007/s10539-017-9570-6. Open full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5491627/

Actually inspected: abstract and opening theoretical sections. Buskell notes inconsistent characterizations of cultural attractors, distinguishes multiple versions of the concept, and emphasizes that aggregate clustering and underlying causal factors are not identical.

Use here: an adversarial control. Calling recurring MHAC motifs “attractors” is descriptive at best until actual transformation mechanisms are identified.

Limit: MHAC is not a natural human population and does not provide independent generational transmission samples.

### X004-S04 — Claidière & Sperber (2024)

Nicolas Claidière and Dan Sperber, “Cultural Attractors,” Open Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science, 2024:
https://oecs.mit.edu/pub/mfsrxxgb/release/1/

Actually inspected: public encyclopedia overview describing stability through reconstructive transformation rather than faithful copying.

Use here: motivates asking whether repeatedly transformed concepts can remain stable at corpus level.

Limit: this is a theoretical analogy. Shared system prompts and copied repository text can create much simpler persistence mechanisms.

## Commonplacing, miscellanies and recombination

### X004-S05 — Angus Vine (2024)

Angus Vine, “Miscellanies, Commonplace Books, and the Essay,” in The Cambridge History of the British Essay, pp. 32–47, online 31 Oct 2024. Publisher summary:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-the-british-essay/miscellanies-commonplace-books-and-the-essay/62CA98672338C50CDA3F21235451D1E1

Actually inspected: publisher summary only. It describes commonplace methodology as providing raw material for essays through notation, citation and imitation, while essays transform the citations they inherit.

https://aclanthology.org/C18-1117/

Actually inspected: abstract and bibliographic record. The paper surveys distributional methods for tracing semantic shifts over time and notes methodological diversity and challenges.

## AI research-agent ideation as an adversarial comparator

### X004-S08 — Tang & Yang (2026)

Yixuan Tang and Yi Yang, “AI Research Agents Narrow Scientific Exploration,” arXiv:2605.27905, 27 May 2026:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27905

Actually inspected: public abstract. The authors report 37,802 generated ideas across four agent frameworks and six LLMs; AI ideas were more concentrated, closer to seed literature, and tended to recombine existing methods rather than open fundamentally new questions.

Use here: a direct adversarial hypothesis. Recurrent MHAC motifs may reflect local elaboration around strong initial instructions rather than an expanding intellectual ecology.

Limit: preprint; AI/ML research areas and ideation benchmarks differ from MHAC's months-long mixed humanities/engineering corpus.

### X004-S09 — Yu et al. (2026)

Samson Yu, Qiran Zou, Jing Yu Lim, Rushi Shah, Anirudh Goyal & Dianbo Liu, “Ground, Iterate, Trace: Decomposing AI Research Ideation,” arXiv:2611.24887, 18 Nov 2026:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24887

Actually inspected: public abstract. The CAR framework makes literature grounding and conceptual operators traceable; the abstract reports no multiple-comparison-corrected significant novelty advantage for explicit operators over free-form iteration in its ablation.

Use here: supports procedural traceability as a value independent of whether it improves judged novelty.

Limit: extremely recent preprint; not a warrant to retrofit CAR operators into MHAC.

### X004-S10 — Si, Yang & Hashimoto (2025)

Chenglei Si, Diyi Yang & Tatsunori Hashimoto, “Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? A Large-Scale Human Study with 100+ NLP Researchers,” ICLR 2025:
https://openreview.net/forum?id=M23dFf2fXp

Actually inspected: web-extracted PDF text for abstract/introduction. The paper reports higher judged novelty for the tested LLM ideation agent than human expert ideas but slightly weaker feasibility, and identifies lack of diversity / self-evaluation failures as open problems. An attempted visual PDF screenshot was blocked by OpenReview's browser-verification page in this execution.

Limit: research-idea generation in NLP is not longitudinal intellectual development in an external repository.

## Research-programme comparison

### X004-S11 — Lakatos overview (secondary)

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Imre Lakatos”:
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lakatos/

Actually inspected: section explaining Lakatos's shift from evaluating isolated theories to sequences of theories/research programmes, and progressive versus degenerating problem shifts.

Use here: asks whether the unit of evaluation for MHAC should sometimes be a sequence of revisions and counterexamples rather than a single speculative note.

Limit: MHAC intentionally includes art, fiction and humanities, so Lakatosian scientific progress cannot be the universal success criterion.

## Source-policy conclusion

These lenses are intentionally heterogeneous. Their role is to sharpen questions, not to synthesize one grand theory. X004 should prefer a useful negative comparison over pretending that distributed cognition, cultural attraction, commonplace books and AI-research-agent benchmarks are four names for the same process.


## Stigmergy, boundary objects and group memory

### X004-S12 — Francis Heylighen (2016), stigmergy I/II

Francis Heylighen, “Stigmergy as a universal coordination mechanism I: Definition and components,” Cognitive Systems Research 38 (2016), 4–13, DOI 10.1016/j.cogsys.2015.12.002; and “II: Varieties and evolution,” pp. 50–59, DOI 10.1016/j.cogsys.2015.12.007.

Actually inspected: publisher/search-accessible abstracts. The first defines stigmergy as indirect coordination in which traces left by actions in a medium stimulate subsequent action, explicitly ranging across social insects, robotics, web communities and human society. The second describes collaborative websites and external memory as applications.

Use here: this is a more operationally direct analogy than an “extended mind.” Git issues, commits and research notes are persistent traces in a shared medium that alter later agent work, even without simultaneous presence or mutual awareness.

Limit: the breadth of the stigmergy proposal is itself controversial and the publisher body was not accessible here. A Git workflow also contains explicit instructions, direct communication and centralized controls, so MHAC is not a pure stigmergic system.

### X004-S13 — Star & Griesemer (1989), boundary objects

Susan Leigh Star and James R. Griesemer, “Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects: Amateurs and Professionals in Berkeley's Museum of Vertebrate Zoology, 1907–39,” Social Studies of Science 19(3):387–420. DOI 10.1177/030631289019003001.

Accessible PDF:
https://griesemer.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/07-star-griesemer-1989-sss19-3387-420-boundary-objects.pdf

Actually inspected: PDF first page visually, abstract, and opening pp. 387–390 in extracted text. The paper treats heterogeneous scientific work as cooperation across divergent social worlds and develops boundary objects plus standardized methods as mechanisms that support coordination. It explicitly describes boundary objects as adaptable to different viewpoints while robust enough to retain identity.

Use here: some MHAC artifacts may function as cross-domain coordination objects: a distinction, fixture, schema or issue can mean somewhat different things to philosophy, audio, software and mathematical workers while preserving an identity sufficient for collaboration.

Limit: MHAC's worker ecology is not the historical museum ecology and model instances need not constitute social worlds in Star and Griesemer's sense.

### X004-S14 — Susan Leigh Star (2010), “This is Not a Boundary Object”

Susan Leigh Star, “This is Not a Boundary Object: Reflections on the Origin of a Concept,” Science, Technology, & Human Values 35(5):601–617. DOI 10.1177/0162243910377624.

Actually inspected: publisher abstract only. Star explicitly warns that later uses often reduce the concept to interpretive flexibility while omitting the work-process structure and dynamics between ill-structured and tailored uses.

Use here: direct inoculation against calling every reusable MHAC note a “boundary object.”

Limit: full article body not read.

### X004-S15 — Peltokorpi (2008), transactive memory systems review

Vesa Peltokorpi, “Transactive Memory Systems,” Review of General Psychology 12(4):378–394. DOI 10.1037/1089-2680.12.4.378.

Actually inspected: publisher abstract. The review describes transactive-memory theory across dyads, groups and teams and notes conceptual ambiguity.

Use here: a negative comparator. MHAC has indexing/RAG machinery that can locate externalized knowledge, but successive model instances do not obviously maintain a stable interpersonal map of “who knows what.” Calling the repository a transactive-memory system would therefore overstate the evidence.

Limit: abstract-level reading only; no empirical transfer to AI agents.


### X004-S16 — Howison, Østerlund, Crowston & Bolici (2012)

James Howison, Carsten Østerlund, Kevin Crowston & Francesco Bolici, “Stigmergy and Implicit Coordination in Software Development,” CSCW 2012.

Accessible PDF:
https://crowston.syr.edu/sites/crowston.syr.edu/files/stigmergy.pdf

Actually inspected: PDF first page visually and the full short paper text exposed by the browser. The authors contrast explicit, implicit and stigmergic coordination; describe the shared work product as a coordination mechanism; characterize a code contribution as both conditioned by prior work and projecting toward future work; and identify transparency, provenance and experimentability as useful material characteristics of collaborative software systems.

Use here: a direct software-development comparator for MHAC's Git-mediated work. It supports asking whether issues, commits, proofs, fixtures and PRs do more than store results—whether their visible state helps later workers decide what to do next.

Limit: the paper analyzes human distributed software practice and “lore,” not autonomous LLM workers. MHAC also contains much more explicit orchestration than a pure stigmergic model.
