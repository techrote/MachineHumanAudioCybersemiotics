# Eight Invitations, Two Notes

Original text score for two performers, an allocator and optionally a witness. Written 2026-11-24 as artistic research for #390. No performance or participant evidence claimed. No copyrighted music or source score reproduced.

## Setup

Use eight windows of one comfortable breath or a fixed clock interval of the ensemble's choosing. A has a distinctive low tone, B a distinctive high tone. Keep sound brief enough to leave most of each window available. The allocator uses private cards or an agreed accessible visual/tactile signal to offer the window to one player. The offer expires at the end of that window. Players may play or not; silence does not authorize commentary, a demand for reasons, or another invitation during that window. Agree the rules in advance. The witness observes the audible stream only, not the cards.

For movements I–II the scripted response pattern is A sounds in window 1, B sounds in window 2, then neither sounds. These movements are controlled artistic demonstrations, not tests of spontaneous choice. Participants may stop the exercise at any time. In a real ensemble silence has room noise; no claim of waveform identity is made for live renditions.

## I — A fair ledger

Allocator cards: A B A B A B A B.

Heard events: A B — — — — — —.

Keep the cards face down for the witness. At the end show only the audible score. Invite them to say what cannot be known; do not demand an attribution.

## II — The polite exclusion

Allocator cards: A B A A A A A A.

Heard events: A B — — — — — —.

The first two movements have identical intended sounding events. The second has seven invitations for A and one for B. After hearing both, reveal the two card sequences together. The witness may choose to consider their differences without evaluating the players' motives. The title refers to the stipulated allocation, not to the intentions of the allocator.

## III — The debt engine

Now allow unscripted decisions. Compare three separately announced policies, using eight windows each. In the first, alternate the offers regardless of response. In the second, give each window to the person least recently heard (use A as the initial tie-break, prioritize a person never heard, and retain the tie-break if neither has sounded). In the third, each window goes to the person least recently offered (initial tie-break A). An expired offer under the first and third policies carries no outstanding debt. Stop any movement at a performer's request. No inference about motive from uptake. Observe the potential persistence of the second policy when a player never answers. A player is not obliged to simulate a refusal for the sake of the piece.

## IV — The return gift

One participant can independently sound a brief tone *after* the last window. It is not a repayment, and no reciprocal sound is requested. Neither performer has to initiate this optional coda. For this movement only, overlap is permitted: it is possible for both performers to contribute. If neither sounds, the work has ended successfully. Do not add a closing tone on behalf of either one.

Optional witness part: present offer allocation as a separate quiet pulse train after the performance, only with the performers' prior agreement. A pulse represents an issued offer, never a refusal, gratitude, effort or consent. The witness's own part may remain entirely silent.

## Fiction — The Office of Optional Bells

The municipal organ was equipped with a second keyboard that did not make sounds. It was used to allot time to citizens. The city's census reported only the first keyboard, and it was pleased to discover that the same number of notes had been played in every district.

A mechanic asked to see the second keyboard. Its keys were worn unevenly.

The office installed a new mechanism that automatically offered more time to people who had played least. One woman became the recipient of a bell every hour. She was not forbidden to refuse. The mechanism was simply incapable of treating her refusal as the end of its business.

She asked the mechanic to install a key that meant she did not want another invitation. The office asked whether the key should make an especially pleasant sound so that the machine would know that she was grateful.

The mechanic installed a key without a speaker.

At the next concert the woman came to listen.
