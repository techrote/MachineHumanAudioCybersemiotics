# Four exercises in becoming ordinary
Original text score and fiction — expedition #430. Unperformed; no perceptual findings.

For two players and an optional witness. Choose two state pitches (or symbols 0 and 1) and two realizations (e.g. plain and softly fluttering), with no intrinsic meaning assigned. Use comfortable levels or silent cards. Every slot lasts the same length; begin at (0,0). The state player flips the state bit at transitions 2 and 6 of eight. The surface player flips the ornament bit as indicated. State and ornament changes are independent. The witness may observe, decline or stop; no deceptive alarm or operational context.

## I. The bell learns the room
Repeat the initial token nine times. The performers may privately attend to different details each time, but make no requested change to the token. The witness need not describe anything. End with an optional rest. The movement does not require habituation to occur.

## II. The decorator and the messenger
Play the two state transitions with ornament flips at 2,4,6,8. Reset explicitly and repeat with ornament flips at 1,3,5,7. Both versions contain two state flips and four ornament flips. Read the two change ledgers after each version: first four surface-change flags (two extra), then six (four extra). Do not ask which is the better artwork.

## III. The clerk hears only the doors
Fix the change mask {1,2,4,6,8}. Behind a screen, choose any two of these positions as state flips; choose one of those two to coincide with an ornament flip. At every remaining position in the mask flip ornament. The witness receives only a card when either bit changes, not the full token. If requested, show the full ledger afterward. There are ten possible state trajectories and twenty joint histories, not twenty measured experiences.

## IV. The right to stop being a spectacle
The state player may keep a constant note while the surface player varies it, or keep both bits constant while exploring its physical sound. If either wants to end, stop. The witness may leave without announcement. No final surprise is owed.

## Speculative fiction: The Museum of the Ordinary Bell
The city's museum had a bell whose makers were paid whenever a visitor turned toward it. They installed a different clapper every morning. By winter the bell could reproduce the arrival of the postman, the announcement of a meal, the hinge of a door and the sound of a falling leaf. It rang for nothing in particular.

A second bell stood in the workshop. Its maker was paid for the work that went on while it was not being attended to. When it rang, it was still possible to ask what had changed. One afternoon a visitor asked to hear it without needing to do anything.

The maker turned off the workshop connection and lent the visitor the bell for the concert. The visitor rang it only once. The following morning she asked for another concert, in which the bell would not ring.

The museum took this as a successful exhibition. The workshop recorded no fault.
