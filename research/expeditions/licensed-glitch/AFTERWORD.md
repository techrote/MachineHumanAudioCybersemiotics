# The Minister of Redundancy

*Original speculative fiction; not a historical account or model result.*

In the city of Drest, the municipal organs announced only two things. A majority of low pipes meant that the northern sluice was closed; a majority of high pipes meant it was open. Five pipes spoke at a time. None of the pipes knew which city it lived in.

At first the organists were forbidden to vary the signals. The Minister of Redundancy argued that a single foreign note might cause a disaster. Her successor brought a book of binary words and demonstrated that one foreign note could be permitted without sacrificing the distinction the sluice required. He called this the Restoration of Music.

The organists did not agree. A low pipe among high pipes could be a decoration, a protest, a failed relay, or a piece of dust on a contact. A machine that could tell the sluice's state could not tell which of those events had occurred.

The city's archives were nevertheless very clean. Every recorded signal was reduced to the same five notes. Officials could reconstruct the history of the sluice, but the century in which the organists composed for it had disappeared.

In the final year of the Ministry, an archivist requested the original recordings. There were none. She requested the original scores. Those had been archived by the same machine.

So she wrote two new scores, the first called Accident and the second Ornament, and made them produce exactly the same recording. She entered both into the catalogue, under the heading Unresolved.

In the margin she wrote a third word: Freedom.

It was the only word the catalogue refused to correct.
