## Interpretation — the map that charges admission in seconds

A composer draws a line between two points and assigns two seconds to traversing it. A second composer inserts a small excursion in the middle. They disagree about whether the score has become longer. One is reading the concert programme and the other is measuring the walk.

The disagreement is not solved by a more precise stopwatch. The two composers have imposed different invariants. The first regards time as a container into which arbitrary detail must fit. The second regards speed as a law and lets the temporal container grow. A third might count turns or charge a different price for ascent and descent.

This is the fruitful tension between cartographic exactitude and musical finitude. Borges' useless map is not useless as material: in its ruins it becomes a different object. In an instrument, the fine details that cease to be individually playable could similarly migrate into the generation of a timbre, instead of disappearing or accelerating without limit. But that migration changes the contract. It should be authored, not presented as a faithful traversal.

Cardew supplies a counterweight to the demand for a definitive interpretation. A geometric mark can be a prompt for the performer to invent a reading, rather than an instruction for a computer to recover a hidden correct mapping. The clock is thus part of the interpretation, and its budget is part of the score. A finite instrument can embody these competing accounts without adjudicating them.

## Exact construction — the Surveyor's Loom

Start with an oriented unit segment from (0,0) to (1,0). Replace each vector v by v/3, Rv/3, v/3, -Rv/3, v/3, where R is a 90-degree counterclockwise rotation. This is a five-edge square detour. Children sum to v. Old vertices persist every fifth index at the next depth (in normalized coordinates). At depth d the path has 5^d edges, each length 3^-d. Therefore total traversed length L_d=(5/3)^d, although displacement remains (1,0). We count traversed edges with multiplicity, not the measure of a geometric union. No assertion of a simple curve, no infinite-dimensional limit used.

For horizontal and vertical edge counts H,V, H'=3H+2V and V'=2H+3V. The sum multiplies by five while their difference remains one, giving H=(5^d+1)/2 and V=(5^d-1)/2. A weighted Manhattan traversal bill with horizontal weight a and vertical weight b is (aH+bV)/3^d. For positive weights this is a path length in a norm; zero weights are degenerate directional accounting controls, not norms. Signed displacement is a different statistic. A rotation of a physical coordinate frame changes the anisotropic bill unless the weights rotate with it; no unique natural metric is implied.

Three scheduling contracts:
- Fixed duration T=2 s: average geometric speed L_d/2, uniform segment duration 2/5^d.
- Fixed geometric speed 1/2 unit/s: duration 2L_d, segment duration 2/3^d.
- Fixed segment-event rate r: duration 5^d/r, a distinct proposed contract, not rendered here.

No pair of depth-changing contracts can preserve both geometric speed and total duration for these paths. This is a statement about stipulated paths and clocks, not a psychological law. At depth 3, 125 events fit into two seconds (16 ms each) or roughly 9.259 seconds (74.07 ms each). At a 20 ms authored minimum event duration, the two-second fixed-time contract can accommodate at most 100 events, so the deepest full uniform level is 2. A selective refinement scheme could use part of the remaining budget instead. This is an engineering constraint, not a human audibility threshold.

A more general score can make each edge independently eligible for refinement. Every substitution adds four edges and increases traversed distance by two thirds of the parent's length. A fixed event budget therefore prices each refinement equally, but a distance budget prices coarse refinements more dearly. Selective details need not have a self-similar appearance. This may be the more interesting instrument: composing which little streets deserve to be heard.

## Score — Five Maps of the Same Departure

Map a step east to 220 Hz, north to 330 Hz, west to 165 Hz and south to 275 Hz (arbitrary original mapping). Each event is a sine with a squared-sine envelope; reset phase per edge, common gain 0.30; 16 kHz mono PCM. Partition frame counts by nearest-integer boundaries; nominal constant speed is subject to sample rounding. Four two-second movements survey depths 0,1,2,3. Fifth: survey exactly the same depth-3 route using the original geometric speed, permitting the concert to expand. Play these in order or replay the last two as a pair. Do not claim that their common destination is audible. The audio is an authored mapping, not a recording of geography. No third-party music.

Listen again to the fourth recording and imagine that each note is a whole sentence. The final recording gives those sentences more space, but still not the duration they had before refinement. The score does not promise that a listener will hear the geometry. An alternative realization could use direction to select noise colours, or preserve turn impulses while continuously interpolating a filter. The map can outlive any of its realizations.

## Instrument and research exaptation

Surveyor's Loom VSTi: editable polyline and replacement grammar, depth mask, independent traversal clock, a four-direction timbre table and a visibly finite event budget. Recompile on the control thread into an immutable bounded event schedule, swap at phrase boundaries, and show actual segment duration. Reject nonfinite parameters and over-budget plans rather than silently dropping events. A CYD 320x240 version could put a zoomable map above a three-position clock selector, with a running cursor and real elapsed/remaining time. VSTfx could reinterpret an explicitly bounded set of audio grains as edge categories; dry audition remains available. These are design proposals, not implementations.

The second rabbit hole is a variable-resolution trajectory for an oscillator population. A performer can spend additional resolution locally, while another controller spends temporal resources globally. One player's refinement may consume the other player's allotted clock budget. Introduce a budget ledger and explicit priorities before making that collaborative. For very fine structures, a scale-crossfade into spectral material might be musically richer than a forced sequence of tiny events; label it as a new rendering policy, not a preserved event history.

MHAC connection: distinguish net progress, actual path traveled, and elapsed time when sonifying a synthetic actuator. Compare endpoint-only summaries, path-integral summaries, resolution-tagged representations and explicit text in a future authorized synthetic evaluation. An adversarial pair should share endpoints but differ in path length; a second pair should share path length and differ in order. Measure reconstruction of each variable separately; do not equate success at one with semantic understanding. A plain display of traveled distance and timestamps may outperform a sound interface. Do not modify real device controls, consequential status, or the frozen review from this analogy. No communication benefit or human interpretation has been demonstrated.

## Speculative afterword — the toll collector

The surveyor was paid to walk from the gate to the tower. On his second journey he found a street hidden inside a street. On the third he found a smaller street inside that one.

The town clerk insisted that the destination had not changed and refused to change the fee. The musician offered him two seconds of the concert.

He completed the journey.

At the fourth concert the musician gave him the whole evening. The clerk asked why the same distance now occupied so much time.

The surveyor showed him the receipt. The receipt was a map.
