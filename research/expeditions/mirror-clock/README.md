## The reference can be rotated out of existence

The predecessor's centered simplex pilot is optimal for an independently observed and calibrated Euclidean channel. But a cyclic shift of a regular simplex is itself an orthogonal transformation. If calibration is unknown, the hypothesis sets for distinct phases coincide exactly. This is not a small perturbation of its distance guarantee; it is zero identifiability even with infinitely many clean cycles.

Let x(t) in R^d have Euclidean norm at most A>0. Offsets s=0,...,K-1 are known candidates, K>=2. On N indexed ticks, the noiseless observation for phase s belongs to the orbit C_s={ (U x(t+s))_t : U in O(d)}. One unknown but fixed orthogonal channel U applies to the entire window; two alternative explanations may use different U. O(1) is the unknown global polarity model. We seek phase, not necessarily U. The pilot is separately observable from payload; gain is known, no arbitrary additive bias unless expressly considered below. No within-window clock slip or channel drift.

Use the predecessor's simultaneous at-most-e arbitrary whole-symbol replacements, f flagged erasures with indices retained, and dense Euclidean error norm <=epsilon on surviving uncorrupted symbols. For r=min(N,2e+f), let T_r be the sum of the N-r smallest squared symbol differences. Define D_r=min over s!=u and V in O(d) of T_r(x(t+s), V x(t+u)). The orbit reduction follows by applying the inverse of the first hypothesis's U to both. The minimum exists (finite masks, compact group). The midpoint/union-of-supports proof in #780 applies to the minimizing pair of orbit members: universal phase identification is possible exactly when 4 epsilon^2 < D_r; if D_r=0 even zero noise fails. The optimal radius is a strict boundary, not an achieved closed-ball guarantee. For a specified erasure mask, restrict to survivors and trim 2e instead. This is a theorem about separate phase sets; two calibrations within the same phase need not be distinguished.

For scalar signed pilots, enumerate both polarities per phase, trim e largest squared residuals on surviving observations, and retain a phase iff either polarity's residual sum <=epsilon^2. This is an exact candidate-list algorithm; no tie breaking. For continuous O(d), the corresponding exact mathematical score minimizes over masks and orthogonal transformations. It is not claimed to be a cheap implemented general vector decoder.

## A sharp uncalibrated optimum using only one dimension

For no sparse faults, assemble phase matrices X_s with time as columns. Orthogonal alignment gives

D_0(s,u)=||X_s||_F^2+||X_u||_F^2-2||X_u X_s^T||_*,

where the nuclear norm is the sum of singular values. Proof: expand squared Frobenius distance and maximize the cross trace over O(d) by SVD, choosing the aligning orthogonal factors; zero singular values impose no constraint. In particular D_0<=2NA^2 in *any* dimension. The same bound follows just by comparing relative transformations +I and -I. Equality for every phase pair requires each sampled phase to have full energy NA^2 and every cross matrix to vanish. Unlike the calibrated simplex, zero cross-covariance, not a large instantaneous angular separation, is the equality mechanism.

Construction: take a binary de Bruijn cycle of order K, replace 0 by -A and 1 by +A, and put this scalar waveform on a fixed unit vector. Each K-tuple occurs exactly once over N=2^K ticks; every pair of different offsets in the range 0,...,K-1 is independent and balanced over that cycle. All shift cross-covariances vanish. Hence D_0=2NA^2, attaining the universal upper bound, even with arbitrary fixed O(d). Repeat cycles for multiples of that N. This is an existence/construction bound, not the shortest possible sequence length for all K. The same construction works with unknown constant translation in addition to the orthogonal transform, for these full untrimmed cycles: optimizing translation centers each phase, and both have mean zero already. If independent gains are also unknown but lower bounded by g>0, the untrimmed distance between these orthogonal phase orbits is at least 2g^2 NA^2, attained at both gains g. Without a positive lower bound the uniform radius collapses.

For K<=4 a shorter pilot is (+A,+A,+A,-A) repeated over N=4k: all three nonzero cyclic lag autocorrelations vanish. It is not DC-free. At K=3, four ticks are the minimum possible at the untrimmed upper bound among full-amplitude scalar binary pilots: three mutually orthogonal sign vectors require N divisible by four. Multiply the first vector coordinatewise into the other two. Both become balanced; their pair correlation zero forces each joint sign combination to occur N/4 times. This argument concerns attaining the upper bound, not the minimum possible clean identification length. At N=4 with unknown constant translation the short pilot has centered energy 3A^2 and cross product -A^2, giving D_0=4A^2 instead of 8A^2. The centered de Bruijn construction avoids this loss. The comparison with #780 changes the nuisance model, not just the pilot.

## Why the three-tick clock is genuinely limited

For a period-three scalar pilot (x,y,z) repeated k times, all phase pairs under unknown fixed sign have squared orbit distance 2k(E-|C|), where E=x^2+y^2+z^2 and C=xy+yz+zx. The sharp maximum is 9kA^2/2, attained by (A,A,-A/2). Normalize A=1. If all signs agree, E-C is convex (half the sum of three squared gaps), hence maximized on the unit cube at a vertex, with value at most 1. Otherwise permute and globally negate to write x,y>=0 and z=-c, all in magnitude <=1. For fixed x,y, E-|C| is piecewise convex in c, so its maximum is attained at 0,1, or the crossing c=xy/(x+y) if x+y>0. At c=0 the function x^2+y^2-xy has maximum 1 on the square by convexity. At c=1 C<=0 and E+C=x^2+y^2+1+xy-x-y has maximum 2 on the square by convexity. At the crossing c<=1/2 and E<=9/4. x=y=1,c=1/2 gives equality. At x=y=0 the bound is immediate. The three-tick optimum is therefore 1.5 A^2 per tick, compared with the four-tick orthogonal pilot's 2 A^2 per tick. This is proved for scalar period three, not all possible three-state vector pilots or all nonperiodic length-three windows.

## Sparse faults: the price of amplitude symmetry

For any full-amplitude binary scalar sequence, two phase words have h mismatches. Under unknown global sign, the alternative sign has N-h mismatches. Their trimmed squared distance is 4A^2 max(0,min(h,N-h)-r). Thus universal binary scalar bound is 4A^2 max(0,floor(N/2)-r). Both the short four-tick and the full de Bruijn pilots attain this for their stated K,N. For a rank-one vector pilot under unknown O(d), a relative orientation contributes cross term c in [-1,1]; for every fixed surviving mask the squared difference is affine in c, minimized at an endpoint. Minimizing over masks yields precisely the same formula. This is not asserted to bound all vector pilots with constant norm and varying directions.

There is a contrasting optimum when almost all evidence can be removed. Use the nonnegative radial cycle x_j=jA/(K-1), j=0,...,K-1, on a unit vector. The reverse triangle inequality gives a per-tick difference of at least g=A/(K-1) under every orthogonal alignment, hence D_r >= (N-r)g^2. For r=N-1 this is globally sharp even in arbitrary dimension: at any one tick the K norms in [0,A] have some pair separated by at most g; an orthogonal transformation can align the two vectors (including the zero case), and the adversary retains just that tick. This is an instantaneous norm-labeling result, not the calibrated vector simplex result.

For scalar unknown polarity, a stronger high-fault statement follows. At each tick some pair of K magnitudes has separation <=g. At least one relative sign aligns that pair. Assign each tick to one such pair/sign class; there are 2*binom(K,2)=K(K-1) classes, so one contains at least ceil(N/[K(K-1)]) ticks. If N-r is at most this number, this pair/sign has trimmed distance <=(N-r)g^2. The radial cycle attains the bound for every N in this regime. This proof does not extend to arbitrary vector orientations across multiple surviving ticks, because the aligning rotation could differ by tick. In the intermediate regime no global continuous scalar optimum is claimed.

For an illustrative three-offset contract at N=12,A=1, the binary four-tick reference has squared radius numerator 4 max(0,6-r); the radial period-three reference (0,1/2,1) has inventory eight differences of 1/4 and four of 1 for each positive-alignment phase pair, and the opposite sign can only increase individual distances. Its trimmed D is 6-r for r<=4, and (12-r)/4 for r>=4. Binary wins at r<=5, radial wins at r>=6. The radial result is globally proved for scalar at r>=10 here, not for every intermediate r. Dense-noise boundary is sqrt(D_r)/2, in raw total L2 units.

At N=9,r=4, the nonperiodic eleven-tick string (-1,-1,-1,0,-1,1,0,-1,1,0,-1) attains D=3. The period-three signed ternary cycle (-1,0,1) yields D=2 (opposite sign leaves one zero and two unit differences per cycle). The radial half-step cycle yields D=5/4, and no full-amplitude binary scalar can survive r=4 at N=9. This is a concrete finite witness that the two easy endpoint constructions do not exhaust intermediate-budget design. It does not certify the optimum on the continuum. At N=4,r=3 the finite five-level optimum agrees with the universal one-survivor theorem.
