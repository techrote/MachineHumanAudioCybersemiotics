# Four hearings of an absent law
Original performance instruction; not performed by human musicians.

Equipment: four arbitrary pitches (suggested 196, 247, 294, 370 Hz), paper or screen with four labelled state cards, one musician, one score editor, optional auditor. Each pitch occupies a fixed half-second slot, with a short attack and release. The silence between slots is not evidence of a rule change. The editor shows the active prescription before each note and the proposed next prescription after it. The host's stop control remains external to all musical rules.

## I — The lawful revolution
Set f=(1,2,3,0). Begin at 0. Sound 16 slots, updating the active state only after each emission. Do not try to resolve the repeating sequence. End without a cadence. Show the actual event-time state beside each recorded note.

## II — A court with no witnesses
Keep the same table. Ask which notes could satisfy x=f(x) at the instant of their own amendment. There are none. Place an empty card on the music stand labelled “no self-consistent audition”. Keep the earlier recording available. Do not claim that the empty result invalidates movement I.

## III — Two hidden constitutions
Perform eight slots from 0 with f=(1,0,2,3), then eight from 0 with f=(1,0,3,2). Use the same pitch, timing, gain and renderer for both. The observer hears identical symbolic sequences. Now audition the fixed points of each table: first notes 2 and 3 together, then an explicitly labelled empty audition. These are newly authored diagnostic performances, not recovered hidden audio. Repeat both from state 2.

## IV — The editor's refusal
Invite the score editor to change an unvisited rule. The musician may request a new start, decline the amendment, or stop. Make no effort to preserve the formal one-table guarantee once an amendment is declined or two versions are played. Write the old and new table and their effective times separately. Finish with a note that does not propose a new rule. The absence of an amendment is an artistic ending, not a broken instrument.

Optional visual staging: a conductor card faces the stage, then turns to face the score. A card does not acquire real agency through rotation. No unauthorized device control, hidden commands or human data.
