# Four Ways to Leave a Pipe

Original performance score, 25 November 2026. Three seats, three sustaining voices, one operator and optional willing guest performers. The synthetic recording uses scripted roles only.

- **I. Invitation without obligation.** Sound three pipes. Offer the first and withdraw the offer on refusal; repeat with the second. Neither refusal changes the chord. The invitation is a visible gesture, not a demand for an audible answer.
- **II. Handoff.** Begin with the same chord. Offer the first pipe. On explicit acceptance, the machine relinquishes it and the guest may sound a different tone. Repeat with the second. Do not interpret silence as acceptance.
- **III. Two vacant seats.** The machine retires the first two pipes without replacements. Offer the third; on refusal leave its machine tone intact. Resist the temptation to fill the gaps.
- **IV. A guest leaves.** Offer the first pipe, perform a scripted acceptance, then allow the guest to release it. Retire the second machine pipe. End in the same occupancy state as III, but with a different history.
- **V. Optional coda (unrendered).** A willing performer may excite a previously retired resonant chamber through an independent instrument. This deliberately departs from the terminal-vacancy model. Do not reinstate the machine voice. The audience may remain an audience. End without requiring all seats to be occupied or retired.

In the rendered miniatures, each state lasts half a second and has a short edge envelope. Machine frequencies are 165, 220 and 330 Hz; guest frequencies are 1.5 times the corresponding machine frequency with a gentle 4 Hz amplitude modulation. These are arbitrary artistic choices, not meanings intrinsic to those frequencies. No public performance, human evaluation, participant recording, or biometric measurement occurred.

For a human performance, establish accessible instructions, agreed participation and stop rules in advance; no response need be offered or explained. This is a proposed artistic score, not a recruitment protocol.
