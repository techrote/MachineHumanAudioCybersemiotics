# Three Ends That Do Not End
Original performance score · 25 November 2026 · exploratory, unperformed by humans.

For one pitched voice, two contrasting boundary voices and an optional fourth performer. Pulse = 250 ms in the electronic demonstration; human performers may use a comfortable pace. One eight-pulse cycle repeats. Use modest levels; there is no requirement to sustain sound after the performance ends.

Carrier: A3, B3, D4, E4, C-sharp4, F-sharp4, D4, B3. (The electronic realization instead uses offsets 0,2,5,7,4,9,5,2 from A3: A3,B3,D4,E4,C-sharp4,F-sharp4,D4,B3.) All notes of equal duration. Two boundary voices mark starts, not claims of semantic closure. A marks slots 0 and 4, B marks the indicated offsets. The carrier is unaltered across movements.

I. **The two pencils.** Both boundary voices mark 0 and 4. Play three cycles; then repeat with A only, B only, carrier only. Do not claim that the removal changes grammatical meaning.

II. **The line crosses the sentence.** B moves to slots 1 and 5, then 2 and 6, then 3 and 7. A remains fixed. Each setting lasts three cycles. Same block lengths, four overlap regions with changing durations. Two percussion pitches, or low and high electronic resonances, are sufficient.

III. **A third voice that belongs to neither pencil.** The optional performer plays a distinct timbre for each of the four A/B intersections. Hold it for the interval length. Vary B's phase, and allow the four durations to contract and expand in alternating directions. A and B keep their total block lengths. The fourth performer may refuse this arrangement and remain silent.

IV. **The end that is not a last line.** At a chosen noncoincident boundary, fade the carrier and one boundary voice. The other may stop slightly before or after the fade. Do not continue indefinitely. Finish at the agreed performance limit, leaving the theoretical loop unresolved. In a second reading insert a simultaneous cut before the fade. Neither reading is the correct ending.

V. **Paper interlude.** Print the original miniature in the essay, then recast it as two sentences on separate lines. Have a reader perform both with identical timing, then with distinct line cues. Readers need not agree on the significance of those cues. Never manufacture a claim of changed perception from the mere existence of alternative notation.

Speculative coda:

The town had two clocks. One ended its hours where the other ended its sentences. The clockmaker was ordered to repair the disagreement. She added a third dial, which measured only the rooms that belonged to both hours. Each room could lengthen without changing the duration of either hour.

At the public demonstration the mayor asked which clock would signal the end. The clockmaker switched both off. On the paper score the two rows of little rooms continued beyond the margin.

The mayor asked if this was a defect. The clockmaker said it was the difference between stopping a machine and completing a sentence.
