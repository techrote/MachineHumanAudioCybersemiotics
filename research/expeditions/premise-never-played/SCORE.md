# Warrant counterpoint — Two reasons for one note

Original text score, 25 November 2026. For three performers and optional observer. Duration freely chosen; no strenuous sounds, amplification, microphones, or real instructions outside the performance. May be played with cards in silence. No human performance has been conducted.

## I. Separate rooms
Prepare four fact cards, each bearing a distinct identifier and a value of either 0 or 1. Let A see only the first pair, B only the second. The fixed rule: a rising fifth may be played if either musician's two facts are both 1. Set all four to 1. Each performer plays a rising fifth once, together if convenient. Do not make the private cards public. A witness may report whether the fifths agreed, but not which cards anyone saw.

## II. The public invoice
Repeat the same note after showing one complete pair of cards to everyone. The third musician sounds a short, quiet wooden click for each fact made public. No change to the principal phrase is required. Reverse which pair is public in a third rendition. The observer may describe the reasons that are now available, but must distinguish them from a claim about the reasons actually used.

## III. The counter-warrant
Change the rule to require all four facts. Repeat the partial-card setup; do not cue an inference from silence. Each performer may wait until the missing facts arrive, ask to inspect them, or decline the invitation. If neither plays, the score continues without requiring an explanation. Repeat with the OR rule restored. Do not equate the compositional rule with a human obligation.

## IV. The unplayed premise
A third musician chooses a quiet sound for the first sufficient path and another for the second. The first two performers may continue to play identical fifths while the third alternates their ground voices. The third may also play neither. In the final minute invite each performer to propose a different rule. A rejected rule remains written, not executed. The witness may leave.

### Afterword (fiction): The municipal clerk

The city kept two books of reasons. One was bound in green, the other in grey. Both authorized the ringing of a small bell in a rehearsal room. The bell rang, and the clerk recorded unanimous agreement. She had never seen the books.

When the archivist asked why the bell had rung, the keeper produced the green book and another keeper produced the grey one. The clerk offered to staple them together. The keepers declined. They had agreed on the bell, not on the binding.

A composer borrowed the books and asked that their pages be performed by separate instruments. The bell was to remain unchanged. In the last movement the composer left its line blank. The two books continued to rustle.

The clerk added a column to the inventory: REASONS NOT REQUIRED TO AGREE.
