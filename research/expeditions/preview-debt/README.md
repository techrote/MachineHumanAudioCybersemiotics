# The Filter That Borrows Tomorrow

## Scope and motivation
A channel can preserve the magnitude of every Fourier component while destroying the possibility of exact bounded-latency recovery by a stable causal filter. This is a familiar nonminimum-phase phenomenon; the expedition develops a sharp, constructive latency/error instrument, including a repeated-zero failure of the single-stage rule. It is not an empirical auditory result or mathematical priority claim. It modifies no canonical records, frozen protocol, or core programme claims. ## Single stage: exact minimax recovery
Let w represent one sample delay, 0<a<1, and H(w)=(w-a)/(1-aw). H is causal, stable, and has |H|=1 on the unit circle. For a causal BIBO-stable LTI equalizer G with integer delay D>=0, define E=w^D-GH. The maximum modulus principle gives ||E||_infty >= |E(a)|=a^D, where the norm is the supremum on the unit circle, equivalently the induced l2 error gain. At D=0 take G=0. For D>=1 the finite polynomial
`G=(1-aw) sum_{i=0}^{D-1} a^(D-1-i) w^i`
makes E=a^D exactly. This meets the lower bound, and the residual is an undelayed scaled copy of the input (not delayed noise). The same design achieves l-infinity input error gain a^D. Its worst-case l2 measurement-noise gain is 1+a^D for D>=1, since |G|=|w^D-a^D| on the circle; D=0 has zero noise gain. No noisy minimax optimality is claimed.

**Stronger bounded-input pointwise result.** Allow any deterministic estimator at time D, using all y[t] for t<=D. Inputs are bi-infinite real sequences with |x[t]|<=1, channel output defined by bounded causal convolution, no known initial state. Choose x_+[t]=a^(D-t) for t<=D, zero thereafter, and x_-=-x_+. Both satisfy the bound. Their outputs vanish through time D: recurrence y[t]-a y[t-1]=x[t-1]-a x[t]=0 and the bounded solution on that past half-line is zero. The inputs at time 0 differ by 2a^D. Every deterministic estimate has worst-case absolute error at least a^D, and the FIR above achieves it for all bounded inputs. This is not a claim about arbitrary channels, unknown a, or a known zero-state finite start. Under that finite-start assumption exact noiseless recursive recovery exists, but errors can grow by factor 1/a per step; the unstable inverse is not BIBO stable. This negative control is important.

## Two stages: repeated-zero penalty
For H^2, E(a)=a^D=c and E'(a)=D a^(D-1). Put b=(1-a^2) D a^(D-1), treating b=0 at D=0. Schwarz–Pick applied to E/M for M=||E||_infty yields M^2-bM-c^2>=0. Hence M >= (b+sqrt(b^2+4c^2))/2. If D=0,1, this lower bound is 1 and G=0 attains it. For D>=2, put M equal to the bound, r=c/M, and
`E(w)=M (r+(w-a)/(1-aw))/(1+r (w-a)/(1-aw))`.
The expression is a scaled disk automorphism with norm M; at a its value is c and its derivative is M(1-r^2)/(1-a^2)=D a^(D-1). Thus G=(w^D-E)/H^2 has removable singularities at a, a stable denominator (the automorphism pole is outside the disk), and real causal rational coefficients. This attains the bound for stable causal LTI filters under the l2 criterion. Unlike the one-stage result, no optimality for arbitrary nonlinear estimators or bounded-input l-infinity error is asserted. The one-stage power-law estimate is not sufficient for the cascade. For D=1 the two-stage minimax filter does nothing although the channel is all-pass. The limit a=0 (excluded from solver) is pure delay, not an invertibility miracle: H^n=w^n can be recovered exactly once D>=n, while D<n gives optimal error gain 1.
