## 2. Exact two-state stability (mathematics)

Take a finite connected simple bipartite graph G with N>=2 vertices, uniformly distributed original X, and the same pair-collapse intervention model as #387: one edge action merges one endpoint into the other, leaving all other vertices unchanged. The authorized decoder knows the action and resulting state and sees the receipt; the observer sees the receipt alone. The encoder knows X but not the selected action. Arbitrary finite stochastic channel rows p_x(t) sum to one. Zero-error recovery for every action is equivalent to disjoint row supports across every graph edge. There is no key.

Let m(t)=max_x p_x(t) and define normalized vulnerability C=N V=sum_t m(t), where V is the optimal observer one-guess success. C>=2, and a binary proper colouring attains C=2. Set a budget C<=2+s, s>=0. The slack s is not an absolute probability point gap: relative loss compared with optimal V is at most s/2.

For vertices u,v at graph distance L, write l=floor(L/2), b=min(1,l*s). Then the exact attainable interval for their total variation distance, over all valid channels satisfying the budget, is:

| Distance parity | Minimum TV | Maximum TV |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Even | 0 | b |
| Odd | 1-b | 1 |

The statement is pair-specific: different pairs may need different extremizing channels. In particular, for an odd distance of at least three, the normalized excess necessary and sufficient to make the two opposite-side rows identical is 1/l. For an even positive distance, the same excess is necessary and sufficient to make the two same-side rows disjoint. Adjacent rows remain perfectly distinguishable at every budget.

**Upper/lower bounds.** For an edge ab, define a nonnegative slack vector e_ab(t)=m(t)-p_a(t)-p_b(t). Its sum is C-2<=s since the edge supports are disjoint. For a two-edge walk a-b-c, p_a-p_c=e_bc-e_ab. Taking half of its L1 norm proves TV(p_a,p_c)<=s. Along a shortest even path, use the triangle inequality l times. For an odd path, stop at the penultimate vertex w; TV(p_w,p_v)=1, so the reverse triangle inequality gives TV(p_u,p_v)>=1-l*s. Clip both bounds to [0,1]. The other two endpoints of the intervals follow from the binary proper colouring. At s=0 this rederives the row rigidity of #387.

**Finite attaining construction.** For l>=1 put h=min(s,1/l), t=l*h. Use a palette interval U=[0,2+h) with unit-length measurable supports. For even distance levels 2i, i=0,...,l, define E_i=[i*h,1+i*h). For intermediate odd levels 2i+1, i<l, use O_i=U minus (E_i union E_(i+1)). The two consecutive even intervals overlap in length 1-h, so O_i has length exactly one and is disjoint from each. If the target is at odd level 2l+1, use O_l=[0,t) union [1+t,2), of length one and disjoint from E_l. Its overlap with E_0 has length t. For an even target, choose O_l=O_(l-1) if further levels exist. Beyond the target, repeat E_l and O_l by parity. Assign each vertex the support for its shortest-path distance from u. In a connected bipartite graph every edge joins levels differing by exactly one, so every edge is valid even with cycles and extra branches. E_0 and E_l have overlap 1-t, giving TV=t for an even target; the odd target has TV=1-t. For l=0 the binary colouring suffices.

Partition the palette at all interval endpoints; each positive-length atom is one finite receipt name. A vertex emits that name with probability equal to its length if the atom is in its support, otherwise zero. Rows sum to one, each atom maximum equals its length if used, and TV equals one minus support-overlap length. Hence C<=2+h<=2+s, attaining the bounds even for real s. Interpolating with the binary colouring via a visible independent mixture selector (disjoint tag alphabets) attains every intermediate TV value; the cost of a mixture is the weighted mean of costs.

**Why the palette is not a restriction.** Conversely, for an arbitrary finite stochastic channel partition a palette of total length C into blocks of lengths m(t). Within block t assign vertex x the initial segment of length p_x(t). Adjacent vertices cannot both occupy any positive-length segment of a block. The overlap between any two vertex supports equals sum_t min(p_x(t),p_y(t)), hence reproduces their TV exactly. This is a mathematical equivalence to a fractional set-colouring representation for this objective, not an assumption about physical signal spectra.

## 3. A vanishing privacy concession can destroy an entire semantic bit

Pairwise aliasing need not be an exceptional rare endpoint event. Take the odd path of length 2l+1 with the above extremizing channel at s=1/l. Its endpoints are on opposite bipartition sides and have identical receipt rows. Attach K new degree-one vertices to each endpoint. Assign every new leaf the same unit support disjoint from the common endpoint support (available in U). All new edges remain zero-error. The palette cost is at most 2+1/l. The N=2l+2+2K originals are uniform and the two bipartition classes are equally large.

Pair the endpoints across sides and pair the K leaves on one side with the K leaves on the other. Every such paired contribution is identical across parity. Only the 2l interior path vertices remain unpaired. Thus optimal guessing of the bipartition bit, a different objective from guessing X, satisfies

    1/2 <= P_correct(parity | receipt) <= 1/2 + l/N.

Proof: a pair with identical rows contributes exactly one state's mass to the maximum of the two parity columns in each receipt cell, plus at most the total unpaired mass after summing. The lower bound is the balanced prior. This is an upper bound, not a claim of exact parity optimum for every l,K.

At exact optimality C=2 every connected graph reveals the bit perfectly. For any positive relative tolerance eta and any positive target gap delta, choose l large enough that 1/(2l)<=eta and then K large enough that l/N<=delta. This gives connected graphs and valid receipts whose full-state guessing score is within factor 1+eta of optimal, yet whose balanced bipartition bit is almost unguessable. There is no graph-independent continuity modulus for semantic-bit recovery under near-optimal full-state vulnerability. Each fixed finite graph still has a distance-dependent modulus; the order of limits matters.

Illustrative exact upper bound (not computed empirical performance): l=100, K=10000, N=20202; local full-state vulnerability is at most 0.5% larger in relative terms than optimal, while parity guessing is at most 50.4951%. No authorized reconstruction errors are introduced. A changed metric, target or population could produce a different result.

## 4. Cross-context audit and countercontrols

**A constructive robustness certificate.** For each connected bipartite context i choose an anchor edge a_i b_i. Classify a receipt as the side of a_i if it belongs to the support of p_(a_i), otherwise the side of b_i. For any x, let r_i(x) be the anchor on its true side, and D_i(x)=dist_i(x,r_i(x))/2, an integer. The marginal classification error is at most min(1,D_i(x)*s_i): for x on the a side, the missing support mass is bounded by TV from p_a; on the b side, the erroneous mass on a's support is bounded by TV from p_b, whose support is disjoint. This explicitly uses the anchor edge, not a heuristic that every label has stable semantics.

Under arbitrary correlated joint emissions for the same X, the probability of recovering its whole bipartition signature is at least 1-sum_i min(1,D_i(x)*s_i), clipped at zero, by the union bound. If all signatures are unique, the observer's joint vulnerability is at least the uniform average of these clipped lower bounds over x. If signatures are not unique, select a single representative per signature and sum only their bounds divided by N. This is a sufficient lower certificate, not a sharp general joint frontier. It can be vacuous for long paths. #387's exact double-star frontier is stronger in its restricted geometry, and remains intact; its anchor distances for same-side comparisons are at most two.

**Countercontrols and proposed design test.** A shortcut joining opposite aliased endpoints destroys the alias while preserving bipartiteness. A disconnected graph can independently flip its component encodings at s=0; the connected theorem does not apply. Odd cycles do not admit C=2. Nonuniform original priors change the vulnerability expression and this proof; imperfect authorized recovery also invalidates the edge-disjointness premise. A classifier that is not required by the application should not be forced into the interface. In a privacy application, losing the unintended parity distinction may be desirable; in a semantic agreement test, it may be unacceptable. Do not harmonize those objectives.

For a candidate interface or synthetic historical receipt, retain the actual collision graph, test every required decoder fibre, report the excess relative to its own optimum, and separately audit distant same-side and opposite-side comparisons, the anchor-distance distribution, and the actual target confusion matrix. Do not infer robust semantics from a scalar near-optimum. Do not call a graph-theoretic distance an auditory metric. A human hearing study would need a separate perceptual target, task, exposure and appraisal; none is performed here. If the required semantics are versioned or task-dependent, the graph itself must be justified rather than inferred from the labels.
