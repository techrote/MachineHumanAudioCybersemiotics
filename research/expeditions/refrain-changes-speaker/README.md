# The Refrain That Changes Its Speaker
*An acoustic object does not carry an irrevocable warrant to act.*

Exploratory humanities and synthetic artistic research, 24 November 2026. Refs #278. AI-authored and self-checked, not a contribution to the frozen E/G evidence set or a claim of scholarly priority.

## 1. The voice in the display case

A museum exhibits a bell which once signalled the closing of a factory gate. The bell is not disconnected. It has been carefully connected to a new circuit: an amplifier, an archive and a small speaker. Every hour a visitor can hear exactly the signal that once closed the gate. No gate moves.

There is no acoustic feature that necessarily distinguishes the exhibit from the old instruction. We could record the factory signal and reuse the recording unchanged. What has changed is the relationship of the event to the surrounding practice. The signal now exhibits an instruction instead of issuing one.

It would be a mistake to conclude that the exhibition is consequently inert. It may annoy the neighbours, summon a memory, prompt someone to leave, or constitute an act of commemoration. Its difference from the old instruction is not an absence of consequences; it is a change in the kinds of consequence for which it is offered.

This expedition calls that distinction *operative footing*. The phrase is a project-specific metaphor, not a definition taken from Goffman. A sound may be presented as an authorized input to a stipulated mechanism, or as an object available for inspection, rehearsal, citation and alteration. The sound can remain unchanged. The mechanism should not.

## 2. Reading against the easy analogy

Manning and Gershon's [Animating interaction](https://doi.org/10.14318/hau3.3.006) (2013), section “Goffman's performance, Goffman's animation,” paragraphs beginning “One of the main refinements” and “Goffman's innovation,” describes the decomposition of a speaker into animator, author and principal. They develop the implications for technologically mediated speech, including a heterogeneous assemblage rather than a single human mouth. Their discussion of the figure expands the metaphor further. Their subsequent section on animation should not be mistaken for a formal role-based access control scheme. I consulted these passages and the abstract, not the complete book of Goffman; the original [Footing](https://doi.org/10.1515/semi.1979.25.1-2.1) (1979) was located bibliographically, and Forms of Talk (1981), pp. 144–145, is the locator reported by Manning and Gershon, not independently verified here.

Interpretation: the same TTS renderer may voice a maintenance report, a quotation from an operator, a test fixture, or an organizational instruction. Its voice identity does not specify who selects the words or whose position is represented. A generated “I” does not, by itself, establish a unified accountable speaker. Conversely, dividing the roles does not absolve anyone of responsibility: a real institution's commitments, permissions and accountability need external evidence. A typed packet may record a designated principal without making that designation true.

Clark and Gerrig, [Quotations as Demonstrations](https://doi.org/10.2307/414729), Language 66(4), 764–805 (1990), publisher abstract (read at Cambridge Core; full article not consulted), propose that quotations depict selected features of their referents and are demonstrations rather than straightforward enactments of the depicted actions. This is particularly fertile for sampling: a sampler can reproduce the grain of a voice or a rhythm without preserving the original situation. A quotation can be approximate, theatrical or selectively faithful. A PCM hash authenticates byte identity only relative to a known reference, not the accuracy of an entire reported episode.

The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Quotation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/quotation/), substantive revision 19 April 2023, §§1.2, 2.2 and 4 (passages read), offers a counterweight. Spoken quotation need not have overt delimiter words; accounts of quotation disagree, and mixed quotation complicates the contrast between using and mentioning an expression. A rigid binary distinction would be especially inadequate for scare quotes, irony, quotation incorporated into an assertion, and the reporting of an expression while also endorsing it. The model below therefore separates *an explicitly stipulated mechanical effect* from linguistic meaning. It does not purport to classify utterances or infer intent.

In Luciano Berio's [author's note to Sinfonia](https://www.lucianoberio.org/en/program-note-work/sinfonia-authors-note/) (work 1968–69; English note read, introductory paragraphs, description of IV, and the detailed account of III), differing degrees of textual comprehension are part of the composition, and familiar musical fragments transform as they enter the harmonic continuity of the Mahler scherzo. Berio describes the pre-existing movement as both container and generator. His fourth movement imitates rather than simply states fragments from earlier movements. This is evidence of his stated compositional outlook, not of how any particular listener understands the piece. No score or recording was analyzed in this expedition. Nothing from Sinfonia is sampled in our original score.

These sources do not converge into a theorem of meaning. One describes interactional positions, one proposes a theory of quotation, one surveys disagreements about it, and one discusses a composition. Their disagreement is the useful part. A safety-oriented interface may need to mark a distinction explicitly which a work of art can productively leave open.

## 3. Restricted mathematical construction: punctuation without waveform edits

Imagine a score of n fixed audible events. The sound renderer depends on event position and pitch only. A second interpreter increments a toy visual counter when an event's trusted disposition is LIVE, and does not increment it if the event is QUOTE. REHEARSE is a third named frame with the same counter behavior as QUOTE. These are stipulated effects, not assertions of human intention, and the audio never controls any actual device.

Let a binary mask x record which events increment the counter. Every one of the 2^n masks has the same raw score. If exact recovery of the ordered counter increments is required, any fixed-length binary side channel must distinguish 2^n cases, so it needs n bits. This is a counting statement; it assumes every mask admissible, no pre-shared extra evidence and exact recovery. If only a final tally is needed, n+1 distinct values suffice; recovering the entire history is a different task. If there is a deterministic fixed score policy already known to both sides, no extra bits may be needed.

There is an interesting middle ground: suppose an authenticated external constraint establishes exactly r maximal LIVE runs, possibly at the first or last event. Add virtual QUOTE events before and after the score. The mask changes state at precisely 2r distinct boundaries selected from the n+1 cuts. Conversely, every increasing set of 2r cuts uniquely specifies r nonempty LIVE runs, alternating with QUOTE. Thus the exact number of admissible masks is choose(n+1, 2r). The minimal fixed-length bit budget is ceil(log2 of that count), provided n and r are already known. An enumerative codec that ranks boundary combinations lexicographically attains it; unused indices are rejected. If r is not shared, the run count itself or another complete code must be supplied. No efficiency claim for unrestricted natural discourse follows.

Eight notes give the following census:

| LIVE runs r | Masks | Minimum fixed bits given r |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 1 | 36 | 6 |
| 2 | 126 | 7 |
| 3 | 84 | 7 |
| 4 | 9 | 4 |

The total is 256. If the composition is fixed at eight notes and exactly one run, the difference between hearing the notes and recovering their operation is still 36 possible histories. A count of live notes alone also fails: 11000000 and 00000011 have the same live total and number of live runs, but act at different times. A designer should choose the observation appropriate to the actual decision, not insist on full replay if only the total matters.

Nested scope: in this toy grammar LIVE is transparent, QUOTE and REHEARSE are absorbing barriers for the *counter effect*. A LIVE note inside a historical QUOTE remains inactive. Adding more inner LIVE wrappers cannot reactivate it. To act on a quotation, create a new outer live event referring to the quoted object, with its own authority and freshness checks (not implemented). Do not strip the enclosing frame as if quotation were a removable audio filter. This is a conservative design convention, not a model of all mixed quotation; a quote can certainly participate in a real speech act, and a new command can legitimately cite an old one.

Delayed qualification: suppose an otherwise identical group of n events may have a footer LIVE or QUOTE that determines whether the group is operative. Before the footer, any immediate irreversible increment would be incorrect in the QUOTE history; a policy that never increments before the footer avoids that particular error but cannot achieve the same immediate response in the LIVE history. Waiting, using a prefix declaration, or explicitly allowing reversible speculative effects are different ways of addressing this limited impossibility. The demo's footer interpreter returns no increments while disposition is missing. This is not a general lower bound for all protocols or a security proof. A prefix can be absent, false or stale; real authentication and replay protection require their own mechanisms.

## 4. A composer for grammatical mood

A conventional sampler has a transport, clip, pitch, envelope, filter and output. A Footing Sampler adds an editable scope lane. The artist can change whether a clip is a quotation, an executable instruction in a toy environment, or a rehearsal event. The audio need not change; the downstream composition does. The counterpart of a filter sweep is a sweep through responsibility and quotation depth.

Imagine a piece for two players. Player A chooses acoustic objects; player B decides whether those objects belong to the concert's operative or exhibition layer. Player B cannot alter the actual sample. Player A cannot choose whether the sound advances a counter that controls the following harmony. Player C might add or remove a visual caption, exposing a distinction without changing the music itself. The performance is less a dialogue of two melodic lines than a dialogue between a sounded event and its framing.

There is a counterargument. The sampler's apparent precision may falsely suggest that an utterance has exactly one interpretable status. An institution could use a meticulously populated ledger to avoid confronting who actually made a decision. In art, the inability to decide whether a sound is being cited or asserted may be the intended experience. In an operational setting, on the other hand, a missing frame must not masquerade as permission. Keep artistic audition and operational effects independently configurable and distinguish the ledger's asserted roles from their verified authority. The proposed interface is neither proof of human comprehension nor a production control architecture.

MHAC design question: when playing back an alarm history, can an operator distinguish a currently issued request from an exact recording of a former request? Does an explicit introductory scope label help, or can its absence and ambiguity become a source of new error? Compare audible prefixes, persistent visual labels, and a distinct archival channel in a later authorized study. Measure task-appropriate identification, response time, erroneous response to replay, missed current request, and accessibility; include a no-replay baseline and cases in which the quote is itself the content of a legitimate new request. Do not infer that listeners naturally understand an arbitrary sonified bracket.
