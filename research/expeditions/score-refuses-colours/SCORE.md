# Six events, two absences
Original text score and fictional afterword · 24 November 2026

## Material

Choose six equal-duration slots A B C D E F, separated by silence. Before playing, agree on a set of distinct sound-object labels. They may be pitches, sounds made on found objects, fixed samples, or lights if a silent performance is preferred. Label identity, not perceived similarity, defines the formal score. Do not use someone else's recording without permission. Keep sound at a comfortable level. This score needs neither a computer nor a human-subject experiment.

An edge means the two events must use different labels. Draw edges AB, AC, AD, AE, AF, BC, BD, BE, BF. No other instructions regarding material. Repeat a slot only when asked in a later movement.

## I — The withheld refrain

Choose exactly three labels. Play the six slots, assigning different labels to connected events. Do not announce the graph before playing.

Now reveal the graph. Discuss whether the recurrence was written, selected or compelled. Do not attempt to agree on the answer.

## II — The fourth object

Keep the graph. Add a fourth sound. Play the same six slots three times: first preserving the old refrain, then dividing the final four events into two groups, then choosing freely among feasible assignments. An observer may announce the moment at which the new sound became necessary to the performance; the performer may disagree.

Example pitches for a sine oscillator, arbitrary and not a claim of equal perceptual spacing: labels 1–4 = 220, 330, 440, 550 Hz. Scores:
1 2 3 3 3 3
1 2 3 4 3 4
1 2 3 3 4 4

The first version is identical to a possible Movement I; the new possibility need not manifest itself.

## III — A line across the blank

Add CD, DE, EF. Use four labels. Play the slots. Do not write equality signs. Ask a second player to derive the unwritten relations before playing.

Add a fifth label (for example 660 Hz). Keep the graph. Find two realizations in which C and E respectively match and differ. Find another in which C and F match. Do not assert that all these relations can be combined simultaneously without checking.

Example valid words: 1 2 3 4 3 4; 1 2 3 4 5 4; 1 2 3 4 5 3.

## IV — The missing instrument

Return to three labels without removing the new edges. The graph is impossible. Choose one of three endings: remain silent; remove a constraint publicly and play; or violate a constraint and identify it afterwards. No ending is designated superior. The ledger distinguishes a refusal from an improvisation.

## Fiction — The conservatory of absent notes

The city of Lorn had no surviving recording of its first opera. It had, instead, a manuscript containing three hundred and seventeen instructions about which sounds must not resemble which other sounds.

The students were taught the opera on three metal plates. They were told that its recurrences were sacred. At graduation they learned the names of the bells and were examined on the length of each refrain.

In the western district a fourth plate was discovered in a kiln. The academy refused to admit it into the orchestra, because the manuscript did not require it.

A student played the opera with four plates. None of the instructions in the manuscript had been violated. Nevertheless the academy recognized nothing in the second act. It summoned the composer, who had been dead for six centuries, and received no answer.

For years the two interpretations were listed separately. The first was called the traditional opera; the second, an arrangement. Finally a conservator noticed a small detail: the manuscript had never contained any name for a plate.

The academy did not abolish the traditional performance. It gave the fourth plate a chair. The chair could remain empty.

At the next examination, a student played the old refrain. The examiner asked why she had repeated it.

For the first time, she was allowed to say that she liked it.
