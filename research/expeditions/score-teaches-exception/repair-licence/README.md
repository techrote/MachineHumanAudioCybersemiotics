## A conservatory with an appeals desk (fiction)

The pupil returns to the courtyard with the same exception each day. The teacher no longer knows whether the instrument is broken, whether the rule contains a clause for the courtyard, or whether a clerk has miscopied the lesson at the same place every morning. A second teacher wants to average away the exception. A third teacher wants to call every deviation an innovation. Both propose to let the sound decide who may amend the score.

The instrument has no such power. It can answer the question it was built to answer, or malfunction; neither output is a deed of amendment. The conservatory needs an appeals desk rather than a more confident ear.

## The trouble source is not the verdict

The parent expedition taught a diagnostic context to a pair of incompatible rules. It assumed that the candidate class was fixed and the correct label already defined. This supplement asks what happens when a persistent exception is part of the candidate class itself, while transient transmission faults remain possible.

No full appraisal or complete paper reading.

Interpretation: a diagnostic note can be an invitation to revisit the account rather than a command to normalize the note. An interface that simply plays its preferred correction collapses the initiation and outcome of repair. In a musical workshop this can erase the performer's intended deviation. In a safety-critical interface, however, waiting for a reply can cost time and a machine is not a human conversational partner. A bounded clarification window and independently authorized fail-safe are preferable to simulating a person's deference. No empirical transfer has been established.

This distinction is useful because a contrary reply is not necessarily evidence that the rule itself was illegitimate, and loss of a warrant need not supply the opposite answer. Our finite exception model is not a formalization of Hart, Pollock or the full logic of defeasibility.

A score can be changed by a person entitled to change it; a reception log can be changed by a faulty channel. They may induce the same distribution of notes. Neither coding distance nor conversational fluency can settle the entitlement. An institution that mistakes its observer for its legislator is making a category error, not merely an inaccurate inference.

## A finite model of the licensed exception

Retain the parent's eight exact binary contexts x and sixteen affine response functions h. Enlarge the class to pairs (h,j) where j is either absent or one of the eight contexts. The effective response is h(x) XOR the indicator of x=j. The word 'licensed' is an authored name for this model, not an inference of authorization. Repeated queries to j always elicit its reversed label. Separate transient faults may flip individual report coordinates; flagged erasures omit them. The context and its identity remain correct.

There are 144 distinct response tables: 16 affine bases times 9 exception choices. Distinct bases disagree on four or eight cube points. One exception in each table removes at most two of those disagreements, leaving at least two. For the same base, different exception choices differ at one or two points. Thus noiseless complete response tables recover both base and exception uniquely within the stipulated family. Without an authorized record this does not establish why the exception exists.

Let c_x be the number of times context x is rehearsed, and write c_(1) <= c_(2) for the two smallest counts.

**Theorem.** The minimum weighted Hamming distance between tables with different base rules is exactly c_(1)+c_(2). The minimum distance between distinct full states (base and exception) is exactly c_(1).

Proof: different bases differ on at least four contexts, and their two exceptions can cancel at most two; any two distinct surviving contexts have combined weight at least the two smallest counts. Conversely every distinct pair p,q lies in some four-point affine hyperplane: choose a nonzero linear functional annihilating p XOR q and its corresponding affine level set. This hyperplane is exactly the support of a difference of two affine bases. Use one exception in each state to cancel the other two points, leaving precisely p,q. Choose p,q with the two smallest counts. For full states the lower bound follows from distinct response tables and the upper bound from comparing a no-exception table with one that reverses a minimum-count context. QED.

For N=8k+r, 0<=r<8, the exact optimum for base recovery is 2k+1 if r=7, and 2k otherwise; for full-state recovery it is k. Balanced counts achieve both. To prove the first upper bound, if the two smallest sum to at least 2k+1, then either the smallest is k and the other seven are at least k+1 (requires N>=8k+7), or the smallest is k-d with d>=1 and the other seven are at least k+d+1 (requires N>=8k+7+6d). If the smallest is >=k+1 then N>=8k+8. For a sum >=2k+2, when the smallest is k-d with d>=0, the total is at least 8k+14+6d; otherwise it is at least 8k+8. For full-state recovery the minimum count cannot exceed floor(N/8). This proves both statements for all N, including incomplete schedules.

Consequently for e arbitrary reply substitutions and f erasures, universal base identification is possible precisely when 2e+f is strictly smaller than the base distance. Full-state identification uses the full-state distance instead. Sufficiency follows from disjoint fault balls between distinct targets; necessity erases differing coordinates and splits the remaining differences. At zero distance the target is not identifiable even without faults. The theorem concerns distinct latent base labels, not just observed truth tables, and explicitly relies on at most one persistent exception per state.

A balanced cube (8 queries) therefore protects the base against one erased reply, but does not protect the full state against even one erasure. Two cubes (16 queries) protect the base against one substitution and one erasure, while full-state recovery fails at that budget. Three cubes (24 queries) protect the entire state against one substitution. In contrast the original no-exception class could already tolerate one substitution and one erasure on a single cube. Allowing the exception is not free.

## Negative result: the exception cannot certify its cause

For base x0 and context 2, an authorized exception produces exactly the same eight replies as an unamended base with a systematic inversion in its upstream channel at context 2. Repeat any context any number of times, even adaptively: induction on the queries and identical responses establishes identical histories. Randomized query choice cannot help when coupled with the same random seed. Neither observer count nor a stronger finite-distance theorem repairs this missing distinction.

The same response table can be accounted for by two incompatible authority ledgers. In one the exception is an authorized clause; in the other it is a defect. This is an impossibility of output-only inference within these explicit hypotheses, not a claim about all physical fault mechanisms. A trusted independent read of the effective configuration or controlled intervention before the fault domain can distinguish the accounts under additional assumptions. A signed assertion authenticates its issuer and payload under its security contract, not necessarily the physical state or legitimacy of that issuer.

The limit of one exception also matters. With two allowed permanent exceptions per state, distinct affine bases can produce the same complete cube. The oracle constructs an explicit table and four distance-two base candidates. It also enumerates all 256 binary truth tables: 16 at distance zero, 128 at distance one, 112 at distance two from the original bases. Every distance-two table has tied nearest bases. This is a model adequacy check: when no allowed state explains the evidence, the receiver should say so, not silently increase its exception budget.

## Score for the appeals desk (original symbolic sound art)

Perform a complete eight-context inventory in integer order. Use the predecessor's convention: three short high/low triangle cues followed by one answer note. Rule A is x0, except that context 2 is inverted. The executable receipt contains the actual eight context/answer pairs. Two players read the same score, but the first is given a card saying the exception is authorized, while the second is told that the channel systematically reverses the reply at context 2. Both produce precisely the same note sequence. This is a symbolic score; no audience was recruited, no WAV rendered in this run.

Movement I — The duplicate: play both editions without their cards. Do not request a listener to infer which card was issued. Movement II — The appeal: after context 2, a third player marks the location of disagreement using a new, explicitly authored cue, without proposing a replacement reply. Movement III — The counter-signature: the original player may confirm, correct or suspend the reply; the institution then announces whether it has authorized a rule change. Confirmation is not the same event as authorization. Movement IV — The closed desk: suppress the institutional response. Leave both accounts written on the score; no cadence can settle their status. The silence is a compositional choice, not a universal abstention signal.

This is not a test of human hearing. It is a construction in which knowledge of the performance's institutional history is deliberately external to the sound.

## Research implications and checkpoint

An instrument could expose separate objects for a trouble source, candidate correction, rule version, exception scope, channel diagnosis, authorization and release to act. An unconfirmed correction must not rewrite the immutable observation. A rule amendment must not be represented as a transient error solely to preserve a preferred hypothesis. A useful experiment would compare an automatic overwrite against an explicit appeal interface, with measured recovery, delays, false certainty and consent to changes; the experiment remains unperformed and would need independent human authorization.

Conventional coding theory, typed provenance and conversation analysis suffice to account for the demonstrated distinction. No incremental cybersemiotic mechanism, normative authority theorem or human validation is claimed. Scientific priority unassessed.
