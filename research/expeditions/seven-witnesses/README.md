## A score, a history, and a report of a history

The previous technical expedition #805 / #806 studied which phase survives a distorted amplitude ruler. Here the ruler is discarded: each witness knows an exact local event order, but has no shared clock or absolute time. The related humanities expedition #471 / #472 asks whether a cue-response binding can be counterfeit while preserving its output repertoire. This is a different problem: the event inventory is fixed and the unknown variable is the relative order of the actually occurring events.

Consider seven single-use named sound gestures A–G. A score can leave their ordering free, a performance can instantiate a particular ordering, and a recording apparatus can expose only fragments of that ordering. The freedom of a performance and the ambiguity of its record are different properties. A performance can be highly constrained yet poorly witnessed; a freely ordered work can be fully witnessed. Neither witness count nor recoverability measures artistic quality.

## Exact projection-distance theorem

Let V be a known finite set of n>=2 distinct event identifiers, each occurring exactly once. A history p is a permutation of V. A monitor is specified by a subset S of V and returns p restricted to S, in relative order, with event identities retained. It need not report absolute timestamps. A family M of monitors is a multiset: identical subsets may appear at distinct output coordinates. For a pair a,b define c(a,b) as the number of monitors whose subsets contain both.

A target dependency graph G on V specifies which unordered event pairs have order relevant to the application. Define a target trace as the orientation of every edge of G induced by p. This is an abstract declared relevance graph, not empirical causality. In particular, the trace can be fully determined even if p is not. The graph must have at least one edge. Monitor subsets may include nonedges; the resulting extra order information is permitted, but not required as target output.

**Theorem A.** The minimum Hamming distance between monitor-output vectors of two histories having different target traces is exactly min_{ab in G} c(a,b).

Proof: histories with different traces differ on at least one required edge. Every monitor containing both endpoints necessarily returns a different projected word. This gives the lower bound. For an edge attaining the smallest coverage, choose a permutation placing its endpoints adjacently and exchange those two. Only their relative order changes. Exactly the monitors containing both endpoints change their output. This attains the bound, including zero. No statistical independence or numerical geometry is needed.

With up to e arbitrary whole-monitor replacements and f flagged whole-monitor erasures, universal exact target-trace recovery is possible iff 2e+f<d, where d is this minimum. If 2e+f<d, two distinct trace candidates cannot fit the received vector: outside at most f erasures and the union of their e+e discrepancy locations they agree. Conversely, take the minimum-distance pair, erase up to f differing coordinates and split the remaining differing coordinates between the two hypotheses with at most e substitutions each. A common received vector results. When d=0 even uncorrupted observations can collide. A candidate-list implementation can simply enumerate permutations for small n and collect distinct target traces satisfying the fault budget; its factorial complexity is retained, not presented as scalable.

**Theorem B.** For a fixed full history p with all pairs relevant, its minimum distance to any other permutation under the same fixed monitor family is exactly min_i c(p_i,p_{i+1}). Indeed any distinct permutation must invert at least one originally adjacent pair, since preserving the entire adjacent chain forces p. An adjacent swap attaining the minimum supplies the reverse inequality.

Theorem B allows a history-specific distance certificate, but a certifier must already have a proposed history and justified monitor outputs. It is not a discovery guarantee for an unknown history. If monitor selection is itself allowed to communicate the history, it is an additional channel; all the following retrospective comparisons hold the selected family fixed as side information and compare every permutation against it. Do not conflate local distance with prior-free global distance.

## Seven events: a sharp witness budget

Take n=7 and monitors observing at most three distinct events. Let lambda be the required minimum distance (a positive integer). Universal full-order protection requires every one of the 21 event pairs to occur in at least lambda monitors. Each triple covers at most 3 pairs, so at least 7 lambda monitors are necessary.

Use the seven triples {i,i+1,i+3} modulo 7 for i=0,...,6. The three positive differences of the base triple are 1,2,3, and their negatives are 6,5,4; hence every ordered nonzero difference occurs once. Translating the triple covers each unordered pair exactly once. This is the standard Fano incidence design, here applied as an exact projection code. Repeating the seven monitor coordinates lambda times attains the lower bound. Thus 7 lambda is the sharp universal triple budget. For one arbitrary replacement, lambda=3 gives 21 triple monitors; for two flagged erasures it gives the same budget. This is redundancy of abstract reports, not assurance that physical witnesses fail independently.

Retrospectively, for a specific history A B C D E F G, the triples ABC, CDE, EFG cover every adjacent pair. Any triple covers at most two adjacent pairs. Consequently 6 lambda adjacency incidences require at least 3 lambda monitors, and these three triples repeated lambda times attain it. The factor 7/3 difference between the budgets is the exact cost of preparing for an unknown ordering in this finite model versus protecting a selected history against competing histories. This does not include the cost of obtaining the proposed history or adaptive selection. For general n and monitor size b, the universal counting lower bound is ceil(lambda*n*(n-1)/(b*(b-1))); the retrospective bound is ceil(lambda*(n-1)/(b-1)), attained by contiguous blocks with one-event overlaps when b-1 divides n-1. No universal attainability claimed for arbitrary parameters.

Negative witness: ABC,CDE,EFG certifies the alphabetical order but is not a universal scheme. A and D are never jointly observed. If A,D are adjacent in another performance, swapping them is invisible to all three monitors. The census finds only eight of 5040 histories uniquely determined by this fixed three-triple family, with as many as 71 candidates for a single output. Equal report quantity is not equal information.

## Exact erasure geometry

For the Fano family with one erased line T, every pair comparison outside T (i.e., having at least one endpoint outside T) survives exactly once, whereas the three pairs internal to T are lost. Fix a true history. Every outside event remains in its original order and has the same relative relationship to each event of T. Therefore the only freedoms are permutations within maximal consecutive runs of events belonging to T. If their lengths are r_1,...,r_m, the exact candidate count is the product of their factorials. This proves the formula independently of enumeration.

With T placed uniformly among the three positions of a seven-position history, 35 position subsets are possible: 10 have no adjacent pair (choose 3 positions from 5 after removing mandatory gaps); 5 consist of a consecutive triple; the remaining 20 have exactly one adjacent pair. Thus the possible candidate counts are 1, 6, 2 respectively. Under the finite uniform census of all histories and erased-line choices, a single flagged erasure leaves unique identification in 2/7 of cases, two candidates in 4/7 and six in 1/7. These proportions are not measured failure probabilities.

Full enumeration groups all 5040 permutations by surviving monitor signatures for each of the 128 erasure masks. A histogram counts history-mask cases, not distinct equivalence classes. Worst candidate counts for zero through seven erased monitors: 1, 6, 20, 56, 90, 252, 840, 5040. See execution.json for the actual counts. A single erased monitor does not always destroy unique identification, but does destroy the universal guarantee. The candidate set should be returned, not replaced by a guessed history.

## Original performance score: the witness ensemble

Seven labeled gestures, each about 300 ms, use original pitch choices 196, 233, 277, 330, 392, 466, 554 Hz. Perform each exactly once in any order, without simultaneous onsets. Each gesture may be played on any instrument with an identifiable label; pitch is merely an optional label carrier. Seven auditors use the Fano triples below and record their three assigned events in order, ignoring the others and discarding timing. Do not provide the auditors with other information for the symbolic experiment.

Movement I: A B C D E F G. Display all seven local words; reconstruct the complete ordering. Movement II: exchange A and B, keeping the rest unchanged. Only witness ABD changes its report, from ABD to BAD. Silence that witness in both performances; the remaining six report lists are identical. These two performances are different despite their identical partial records. For a six-candidate version, perform A B D consecutively before C E F G and suppress ABD. All six permutations of A B D then share the six surviving reports.

Movement III: issue only ABC, CDE, EFG after selecting the alphabetical history; contrast its complete retrospective certificate with its inability to protect an unknown performance. Movement IV: choose a dependency graph with only edges AB and FG, and allow the other comparisons to remain unresolved. Show that different full histories may satisfy the same target trace; the freedom is deliberate, not a failure of fidelity.

This is an original symbolic score, not a reproduction or recording of Brown, and not a human listening trial. The observer contract assumes perfect event identities and exact local order; physically hearing all the gestures, recognizing the pitches or selectively ignoring others may violate that contract. The score can be played using synthetic tone bursts later; no audio recording is claimed here.

## Interpretation, criticism, and scope

The model suggests an interface that can distinguish: (i) the history claimed by a system, (ii) the partial order actually recoverable from reports, and (iii) the order distinctions a task explicitly requires. A UI could show compatible alternatives and the particular missing comparisons that separate them. A protocol designer should define necessary ordering before buying more redundancy; more timestamps may not be the right resource. This is a design hypothesis only. The model does not establish that human listeners benefit from projected auditory receipts.

The score's fictional premise is that seven witnesses are each honest about a smaller world. Their disagreement with the complete chronicle need not be dishonesty. A missing relationship can be a more useful diagnostic than a missing event. Conversely, a partial-order log cannot certify that all actions occurred, that identities are authentic, that the monitors were independent, or that anyone understood or consented. A correlated upstream event-label failure can invalidate all reports at once. A causal receipt needs separate evidence of action, binding, provenance, and timing where required.

No novel theory of human meaning is inferred from this finite coding result. Standard permutation ordering, finite incidence designs, and Hamming geometry account for all proved results; scientific priority for the particular formulation is unassessed. Human research, physical channels, exact timing, repeatable event identities, multiple occurrences and efficient large-scale decoding are outside this proof.
