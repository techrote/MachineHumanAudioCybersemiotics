# The Song That Borrows Its Meter

Exploratory expedition, 24 November 2026 — original interpretation, source-located historical notes, formal toy model and artistic score. Refs #358. Not part of the selected empirical corpus, a reconstruction of Brier, or a production communication design.

## 1. The archive with two doors

A conservator encounters a mechanical singer whose eight gestures always occur in the same order. She makes a perfect recording. The machine's apprentice, however, learns not the recording but a set of alternatives: for each gesture, a short form and a long form. The apprentice receives a different allocation of time and reconstructs the song accordingly. The conservator announces corruption because the waveform has changed. The apprentice announces fidelity because the order of gestures has survived. Neither has yet asked what the original singer regarded as its song.

An archive can contain an object, a competence, or evidence of an encounter. These are not substitutes. A tape may preserve the object extremely well while telling us very little about the range of other performances its maker could have given. A rulebook may permit thousands of realizations while preserving none of their occasions. Neither reconstructs the audience that gave the performance its consequence.

The interpretive conjecture here is that a machine instrument could be designed to preserve an ability to recompose under constraint, not merely a particular sound. That is a choice of artistic medium, not a claim that variability is always better than fidelity.

## 3. Synthetic meter laboratory: what has to move?

Declare n ordered themes. Each has a one-tick short form and two-tick long form. A binary mask x chooses the forms. Exactly k entries must be long, so all versions last n+k ticks. The eight theme frequencies are fixed as a toy symbolic skeleton. The actual renderer uses an extra upper-neighbour tone for long forms; the semantics are stipulated, not acoustically inferred.

A local edit swaps adjacent unequal bits, transferring one extra tick across their common boundary. No theme is removed or exchanged. The change is not audio time-stretching: it recomposes two formula slots. For masks x and y define the boundary displacement after theme i as D_i = sum_{j<=i}(x_j-y_j). The minimum number of local transfers is

    d(x,y) = sum_{i=1}^{n-1} |D_i|.

Proof: each adjacent swap changes exactly one prefix by one, so the sum of absolute required boundary changes is a lower bound. Match the j-th long slot in x with the j-th in y in increasing order. Transport on a line with these ordered matches is monotone; across any boundary the required number of matched particles crossing is |D_i|, all in one direction. Adjacent swaps with vacancies realize that transport: for example correct the first unequal bit by bringing the nearest desired bit from the right across a contiguous run of its opposite, then continue on the suffix. Each such swap reduces the absolute prefix discrepancy by one because all crossed prefixes have the appropriate sign. The procedure terminates at y with exactly the lower-bound cost. Equivalently, d is the sum of absolute differences between corresponding sorted long-slot positions.

The greatest possible distance for fixed n,k is k(n-k): each ordered long-slot position can vary by at most n-k, and the masks with all long slots at opposite ends attain that bound. This is a standard one-dimensional transport/adjacent-swap construction, not a novelty claim.

For n=8,k=4 there are 70 masks and a maximum of 16 transfers. Our two endpoint versions have equal duration, equal number of embellishments, equal theme order, but internal boundary offsets in ticks of 1,2,3,4,3,2,1. None of their seven internal boundaries coincide *for the same theme index*. The last boundary does. Some noncorresponding boundaries can coincide. The 16-transfer path has seventeen performances, each one tick exchange away from its predecessor.

The laboratory uses breadth-first shortest paths for every mask pair within each fixed-weight graph for n=1..8, independently checks the closed expression and graph diameter, and constructs the extreme route. It also renders and reopens four original stereo PCM WAVs twice. See lab.py and RESULTS.txt. No waveform equivalence, aesthetic cost, cognitive effort, or natural-language meaning is inferred from the graph.

### A second rabbit hole: the misleading completeness of an archive

Consider two archives of playable capabilities. One instrument can produce only the two complementary extreme masks. The other can produce any of the 70 masks. Record both extreme masks from each instrument. Every local theme/form combination appears in both archives, every recording is byte-identical given the deterministic renderer, and both archives attain the same extreme distance. Nevertheless the second can perform 68 additional versions and the first cannot perform even one adjacent transfer while remaining within its two allowed states.

This is not an empirical claim about oral archives. It is a finite witness that complete local marginal coverage and endpoint coverage do not determine combinatorial repertoire or connectedness. A catalogue of components, a catalogue of permitted combinations and an affordance for moving between combinations are three different things.

The interesting question is not whether a sufficiently enormous archive could list every legal output of this finite toy. It could. Rather, what should a curator ask when the object being preserved is a practice? Can the instrument respond to a request the recorder never made? Can a musician introduce a new formula? Who is allowed to revise the repertoire? The finite example cannot answer those questions, but it makes the difference between them legible.

## 4. Exaptation: The Recomposition Loom

Prototype proposal, not implemented as a live instrument. A sequence of theme IDs feeds a dictionary of interchangeable formula objects, each with duration, pitch, articulation and a declared scope of equivalence. A duration allocator chooses one form per theme, constrained by total meter and optional local boundary windows. A transfer lane lets the performer move one extra tick left/right. A separate monitor plays a reference take and computes the declared boundary displacement. A history ledger preserves actual performances separately from allowable grammar and edit permissions.

A useful artistic extension is to let an accompanist play the discrepancy D_i, not the melody: the larger the difference between two singers' local time allocations, the more the accompaniment diverges, even though both finish together. A second performer might change the local dictionary while the first plays. No automatic claim of semantic sameness is made when the dictionary changes.

For an instrument designer, archive at least three separate layers: actual rendered takes; the versioned generative dictionary and constraints; and the actions by which a performer may alter the dictionary. A future hardware device might use a footswitch to choose an expanded formula rather than an absolute pitch, making time itself a negotiable resource.

A direct MHAC hypothesis is that a message template could be re-expressed under a variable time budget while preserving a declared action-bearing semantic skeleton. Compare fixed playback, uniform time compression, and explicit selection among short/long forms with independent ground truth; measure action recognition, urgency/uncertainty confusion, time-to-correct-action, perceived burden and recognition of previously unheard versions. Preserve explicit negation, lifecycle, authority and uncertainty; do not compress them away to meet meter. If equivalent forms systematically confuse listeners, reject the equivalence. Do not deploy in safety-critical settings on the strength of this toy. Ordinary language already uses reformulation; the proposed instrument makes that choice visible and playable, rather than claiming to invent paraphrase.

## 5. Counterargument and unresolved questions

Sometimes exact wording is the point: legal testimony, poetry with a particular lineation, a signature, a recording of a unique event. Recomposition may remove style that matters rather than preserve meaning. A taxonomy declaring a short and long phrase equivalent is itself an act of authority; the artist or community may reject it. A recording can support learning and community transmission; the distinction between archive and competence is not a war on recording. Nor does every variation constitute creativity.

This expedition deliberately declines to simulate any living oral tradition, derive psychological laws from the graph or equate a compositional policy with cultural preservation. Its instrumental promise is limited to giving performers more control over what is fixed and what can be recomposed. The original score in SCORE.md stages a disagreement rather than resolving it.
