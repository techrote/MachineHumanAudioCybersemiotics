# Score for two singers and a clock that does not sing

Original text score. No ethnographic performance, borrowed melody or actual cultural tradition is represented. All sound forms are invented.

## I — The inventory

Eight gestures, in this order: door, river, hinge, lamp, stair, window, stone, return. Give each a sound of one tick and an expanded version of two ticks. The expansion may add an ornament; it must retain the stipulated anchor. Four gestures must be expanded. All performances end after twelve ticks.

Singer A expands the first four. Singer B expands the last four. Each performs alone. The clock records only the end. It awards both the same result.

## II — The borrowed measure

Perform both versions simultaneously, in different stereo channels or on distinguishable instruments. The first singer repeats the first version; the second repeats the second. An observer writes down the temporal discrepancy at each corresponding theme boundary. Do not correct a discrepancy merely because it exists.

For the next seventeen cycles the second singer begins at the first version and makes one neighbouring expansion/shortening exchange per cycle, until the second version is reached. The left singer remains unchanged. The discrepancy itself is the counterpoint. The renderer implements one shortest route, not a privileged interpretation.

## III — The complete museum

The museum acquires precisely the two endpoint recordings. The curator displays a green light beside every gesture: both versions have been heard. The visitor may request a new combination. One singer must refuse; the other may comply. Neither may modify the two original recordings.

Now ask whether the catalogue has preserved the work, the ability to perform the work, or the authority to vary it. No vote is required.

## IV — The apprentice

The apprentice may invent a third form of any gesture. The original twelve-tick constraint may be renegotiated by the performers. The recording of the negotiation is not the score and may not substitute for the right to negotiate again.

Finish with a performance that the museum cannot play back because it has not yet happened.

## Afterword — speculative fiction

The conservators of Neral kept a machine that sang the instructions for opening the harbour. Its recordings were immaculate. When the tide changed the duration of the crossing, the machine would no longer fit its instructions between the gates.

An apprentice discovered that each old phrase belonged to a family. She could shorten the invitation to enter, but not the words identifying which channel was closed. She could lengthen a pause, but not exchange the warning for its opposite.

The conservators accused her of borrowing time from the original. She admitted it. She then asked them to indicate where the original had kept its unused time.

At the next exhibition there were two drawers. One contained the voice of the old singer. One contained the instrument on which the old singer had learned to vary its voice. The drawers were locked separately.
