## 1. A second first hearing is not a button

A conservator has two recordings of an utterance. One sounds like machinery and the other sounds like words. On inspection she discovers that the two files are the same. The differences were in the activities of the visitor before hearing each one. Perhaps the second encounter was preceded by a sentence. Perhaps the listener has acquired a new capacity to attend. The conservation problem is suddenly not a matter of maintaining a medium but of preserving an occasion.

Consider the familiar interface operation RESET. A synthesizer can restore an oscillator phase, a program can clear a buffer, and a curator can remove a caption. None of these actions logically entails that a listener has forgotten the caption. There need not be permanent or universal learning for this distinction to hold. Resetting an artifact is an operation on an artifact; resetting a person's encounter would require a separate and as yet unspecified operation. The first encounter is not a preset.

I propose **aspect composition** as a provisional name for works whose principal score includes the acquisition, competition and possible loss of ways of hearing. In a conventional arrangement, one may alter the oscillator to make an event more legible. In aspect composition one can hold a target constant and alter the circumstances in which it becomes available for attention. The listener is not a passive output terminal, but nor is their inner life a synthesizer module that the composer can simply control.

An aspect is not just a label. A critic who writes "this is a march" has not necessarily made a march audible to someone. Conversely a listener may apprehend a rhythmic grouping without being able to state a genre. Nor is a new aspect necessarily a truer or better one. There are distinct achievements: recognition, noticing, making a comparison, reporting a category, understanding a proposition, and acting correctly on it. These may coincide but their coincidence must not be legislated.

## 3. From a verb to a lens

The artwork's premise is not that human listeners are faulty decoding devices. A verb such as "hear" is doing too much work in engineering discussions. There is hearing a pressure pattern, distinguishing a signal, hearing a sentence in a noise, attending to an upper strand in a texture, and hearing a warning as a request that concerns me. They share words; they do not share a single failure mechanism.

A museum can make an object newly visible through comparison. A sentence can make a degraded sound newly intelligible. A rhythmic sequence can reorganize an auditory stream. These processes have different timescales, sources of evidence and criteria of success. The productive collision is to let an artist move between them while resisting the temptation to identify them mathematically.

I call a second track in a score a **lens track**: a sequence of prior examples, hints, misleading comparisons, withdrawals and opportunities to attend. It is independent of the target waveform. A lens track can disclose an affordance without adding an audible feature to the target itself. The effect may depend on a person, language, equipment or occasion; the track is not an instruction that guarantees an experience. An incorrect lens might encourage a false transcription, or simply do nothing. The latter is an interesting artistic outcome too.

One important distinction is between adding evidence and prescribing an answer. A matching cue might disambiguate a degraded sentence; an erroneous cue might be rejected by a sufficiently informative signal. If the sound carries no evidence independently of the cue, "decoding" may reduce to repeating the cue. This yields a principled objection to measuring only agreement with an announced transcript. In operational settings, compare independently logged ground truth, uncued decoding, cue-only performance and conflicting-cue performance. A convincing subjective clarity report is not by itself a correctness certificate.

A further objection to the lens metaphor is that it suggests a view of an object already fully constituted independently of the observer. In music it may be the relationship between comparisons, rather than a latent hidden object, that constitutes what the artist is exploring. No preferred transcription is necessary for an ABA tone train. The two synthetic studies deliberately preserve this difference.

## 4. Executed artistic laboratory and design hypotheses

Run `python render.py audio` with eSpeak, NumPy and SciPy. Eight original mono 24 kHz PCM WAVs are produced. The phrase and foil are disclosed in the source; a first-time listener should not read the code or subsequent score before hearing the isolated target if they want an unprimed personal encounter. Four and twelve logarithmic noise-vocoder bands provide different spectral resolutions. Envelope extraction, seeded noise excitation, per-band filtering and summation are conventional DSP elements; no model of perception is implemented. Forward-backward filtering is offline and noncausal. It is not proposed for live communication.

Speech frames: same four-band target after silence, matching clear speech or a near-neighbour foil, with equal padded lead-in lengths and a 500 ms quiet gap. The clear/foil cues are not energy-matched, and the neutral condition contains silence rather than matched sound. These are exploratory artwork variants, not controlled trials. For the tone study: target is eight repetitions of 440-Hz A, B eight semitones higher, A again; 50 ms raised-sine-squared tone envelopes and 75 ms intervening gaps, 150 ms after the last A. Two 16-triplet precursors differ only in B frequency (2 vs 14 semitones above A); no gap at the transition beyond the ordinary triplet spacing. The frequency transition itself may influence grouping. No specific perceptual outcome is asserted.

Mechanical invariant: the last 49,445 samples of each speech frame are byte-identical to the isolated 4-band target. The last 86,400 samples of each tone frame equal the isolated ABA target. This establishes sample equality within a fixed digital representation, not equality of whole stimuli, acoustic playback at the ear, or listener experience. Two renders in the same environment matched all eight WAV file hashes. PCM sample counts, sample rates, channel counts, bit depth, bounds and write/read equality were asserted. See RESULTS.txt. No human listening or device playback took place.

Design concept: an Aspect Console would expose an immutable target lane, independently schedulable cue lanes, a cue-provenance monitor, and explicit response/abstention channels. The performance state includes a history of what has been offered, not a guessed record of what a person has experienced. A session can rewind the file but must not falsely describe the listener as unexposed. This is an interface history property, not a cognitive-state detector.

Proposed operational hypothesis H1: an independently offered correct preview may improve decoding of synthetic messages in a degraded channel; a false or stale preview may instead induce error. Compare correct, neutral, wrong and cue-only conditions, with held-out messages and genuine uncued baseline. Separate exact transcription, action selection, confidence and latency. Reject the useful-cue hypothesis for a target task if benefit is absent or wrong-cue costs erase it under a predeclared loss budget. Compare conventional speech, visual redundant labels and repeat/detail rather than privileging the exotic sound. No real alarms, device actuation or participants authorized.

Proposed artistic hypothesis H2: an invariant target and competing lens tracks can sustain an interesting composition even when the listener does not perceive a switch. Aesthetic success is not defined as response agreement. The score gives the listener explicit permission to report no change, multiple aspects, or uncertainty.

Study design caution: to investigate naive first hearings, use independently assigned initial exposure groups with multiple novel sentences, avoid repeating the same item across purportedly naive conditions, log language/hearing/accessibility and playback context only under approved protocols. Counterbalance order for subsequent exposure questions, not as a magic eraser of prior learning. Test retention and transfer separately from immediate cueing. No sample size or effect claimed. Real participant work remains gated.
