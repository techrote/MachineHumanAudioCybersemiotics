# The Prism That Passes Every Spoke

MHAC expedition #690, 26 November 2026. Additive mathematics and synthetic acoustics, not accepted Track E/G evidence. AI-agent derivation, source inspection and same-agent computational cross-checks. Independent critique and repository qualification outstanding.

## Abstract

A finite acoustic confusion channel is represented by its uniform-prior posterior measure. An entropy-curvature sweep along a line in the input simplex reveals only a one-dimensional projection of this measure. We give a fixed-direction guarantee, a quantitative projection inequality, an error/erasure budget, sharp planar cases with two or three posterior atoms, and a constructive adaptive reconstruction. A strictly positive three-state/three-output example agrees on the complete raw Shannon information curve along every vertex spoke, yet admits opposing decision preferences. Crucially, equality of projected curvature is not generally equality of the raw information curve: an affine term survives. This is a bridge from discrete tomography to audit design, not a claim of priority over tomography or perceptual evidence.

## Observation contract

Let n>=2 be the number of labelled input states, d=n-1, and K a fixed finite row-stochastic channel. At the uniform prior p0 define w_y=(sum_i K(y|i))/n for each nonzero column, b_y=(K(y|i)/w_y-1)_i. Then b_y has coordinate sum zero and entries >=-1, and the probability measure mu=sum_y w_y delta_(b_y) has vector mean zero. Conversely any such finite probability law yields K(y|i)=w_y(1+b_y[i]); coincident b columns can be merged and split independently of input. Thus equality of these measures is equality up to mutual stochastic garbling (Blackwell equivalence), as established in #686. Output labels are not recovered.

For a nonzero zero-sum direction v and p=p0+uv strictly in the simplex, put q=v dot b and g(z)=(1+z)log(1+z), with g(-1)=0. Define h_i=sum_y w_y g(b_y[i]). Direct expansion gives

    I(p0+uv) = (p0+uv) dot h - integral g(uq) dmu_v(q)
    -d²I/du² = integral q²/(1+uq) dmu_v(q).

The pushforward mu_v under b -> v dot b is the directional receipt. Curvature is determined by that receipt; raw I has an additional affine term. A line with the same projection can disagree in I(0) or its slope. Conversely agreement of full I curves forces projection agreement by the rational argument below. This is a fixed K contract; if the instrument changes its conditional behavior with the prior, the formula no longer applies.

**Finite scalar budget per line.** Suppose each channel has at most m distinct posterior atoms. Subtract projected measures, cancel common masses, and write the residual as sum c_j delta_(q_j) on r<=2m distinct nodes. It has zero total mass and first moment. The rational transform H(u)=sum c_j/(1+u q_j) has numerator degree <=r-1 over product(1+u q_j), with its numerator divisible by u². Consequently curvature difference H(u)/u² has numerator degree <=r-3. It is not identically zero for a nonzero residual (unique nonzero poles; a residual at zero alone cannot have zero mass). All denominators are positive in the strict interior. Hence 2m-2 distinct exact curvature evaluations on a line suffice to determine the projected law. For raw I, 2m distinct interior evaluations suffice: a difference with 2m distinct zeros would have >=2m-2 distinct second-derivative zeros by Rolle, impossible for a nonzero projection residual. If the residual is zero, the difference is affine and two zeros force it to vanish. Unlike binary full-simplex sweeps, arbitrary multistate lines need not have two zero-information endpoints, so do not import the binary raw-value count unmodified. These are sufficient per-line budgets, not a sharp lower bound for raw line values. m=1 is trivial (only the zero posterior contrast).

## Fixed directions and error correction

Work in the d-dimensional zero-sum contrast space. Choose L directions in linear general position (every d independent, when L>=d); more precisely require that no nonzero displacement is orthogonal to more than d-1 of them. For example, in coordinates dual to a chosen basis use moment-curve covectors (1,t,...,t^(d-1)) for distinct t; rescale freely to keep the prior within the simplex.

**Theorem (weighted projection budget).** Two different measures with at most m support points each agree on at most B=m(d-1) of these projected laws. Therefore B+1 fixed directional receipts suffice. Proof: after cancellation, fix a positive residual point x. A direction on which the projected measures agree must have some negative residual point y with the same projection. Each y can account for at most d-1 directions. There are at most m negative points, proving the bound. The same argument is valid for unequal weights and support sizes.

**Quantitative form.** For L>B, write TV for half the L1 distance of discrete measures. Then

    sum_j TV(mu_vj,lambda_vj) >= (L-B) TV(mu,lambda).

At every positive residual x, at least L-B directions have no negative residual in its projection fiber. For each fixed direction, group the qualifying x by common fiber; their total positive mass is bounded by the projected positive residual mass on that fiber, with no double counting. Summing over directions and x proves the claim. The planar B+1 case and its TV inequality have direct Heppes-theorem ancestry; the finite signed-residual proof here also supplies the surplus-direction multiplicity factor and a one-dimensional general-position formulation for d>2. No priority asserted.

**Corollary (whole-line substitutions and erasures).** Among channels of the stated support bound, the code of L projected receipts has Hamming distance at least L-B. Thus e arbitrary wrong line receipts and f flagged missing line receipts can be uniquely corrected if

    L >= m(d-1)+2e+f+1.

Indeed two compatible candidates could disagree on at most 2e of the received lines and at most f missing lines. If each of the nonmissing good lines is only approximately correct in projected TV with error <=delta, any two compatible candidates share at least h=L-2e-f good lines; their TV is at most min(1, 2 delta h/(h-B)) when h>B. This follows by the quantitative inequality on the shared honest subset (if there are more than h lines, the bound only improves). This is a model-level uniqueness result, not an efficient universal decoder. A finite dictionary nearest-signature decoder is implemented in the laboratory. Errors within individual scalar measurements are NOT covered unless they are conservatively treated as whole-line corruption or the resulting projections are certified in the stipulated metric.

## Planar sharpness and switching components

For d=2, any pairwise nonparallel directions meet the general-position condition. For m=2 the bound L>=3 is sharp: choose nonzero independent kernel vectors a,b for the first two directions. The even and odd subset-sum distributions of a,b have equal projections in both directions; subtract their common mean (a+b)/2. For three arbitrary distinct nonparallel planar directions, choose nonzero vectors a,b,c in their respective kernels such that a+b+c=0. This is possible because any two kernel directions are independent and the third is not parallel to either. The signed product

    (delta_0-delta_a)*(delta_0-delta_b)*(delta_0-delta_c)

has its empty and full terms cancel. The three singletons and three complementary pairs lie at a,b,c and -a,-b,-c, with opposite signs. All six points are distinct; both normalized laws have mean zero. Their three projections agree because each difference factor projects to zero on its designated direction. A common small scaling and linear embedding into the zero-sum posterior simplex makes every channel entry strictly positive. Thus m=3 requires four fixed curvature-projection directions and four suffice. For m=2 or 3 the worst-case whole-line error/erasure budget above is also sharp: choose a pair agreeing on any m of the audited directions, erase up to f of the remaining disagreements, and split the rest between two candidates using <=e substitutions each when L<=m+2e+f. For fewer than m lines take a subset of an extended m-line ambiguity. Repeated/parallel directions cannot rescue universal identification: apply the construction to distinct directions and count duplicates as agreeing (when there are >=m distinct choose any m). This sharpness is for projected receipts, not arbitrary raw Shannon data.

**Large alphabets.** For L>=2 arbitrary directions in d>=2, a convolution of L signed differences along respective kernels gives indistinguishable positive/negative laws with 2^(L-1) atoms each; scale factors can be chosen sequentially to avoid subset-sum collisions. The two laws share mean (each increment occurs in half the subsets of each parity). Translate to mean zero and scale inside the posterior domain. In the plane, group triples of nonparallel directions into six-term switching components and convolve these blocks plus any leftover one/two differences, scaling each block to avoid coincident sums. This improves the obstruction to 6^floor(L/3)*2^(L mod 3)/2 atoms per sign for L>=2. The block laws have common means after taking the full product (all factors have total signed mass zero, and the first signed moment of a convolution of >=2 difference factors vanishes). No optimal general m>3 fixed-direction count is claimed. Matoušek et al. give stronger asymptotic results for the related unweighted planar point-set problem; we do not transfer those directly to arbitrary weighted posterior laws.

## The three-state instrument that passes every vertex spoke

Let A and B be the following exact channel count tables, each divided by 12, with rows input states and columns output tones:

    A = [5 4 3; 4 3 5; 3 5 4]
    B = [3 4 5; 4 5 3; 5 3 4].

All entries are positive and each row sums to one. At the uniform prior the three output weights are 1/3. Write b=(x,y,-x-y)/4. A has (x,y)=(1,0),(0,-1),(-1,1); B has their negatives. In every coordinate the same multiset {-1/4,0,1/4} occurs. Thus all h_i match between channels and the projections in directions e_i-p0 match. The entire raw mutual information functions along all three vertex spokes p=p0+u(e_i-p0), -1/2<=u<=1, are equal, including their continuous endpoints. The posterior supports are disjoint; the channels are not Blackwell-equivalent.

Take the off-spoke direction (1,-1,0). A's projected contrasts are (1/4,1/4,-1/2); B's are their negatives. Their third projected moments differ, so their curvature curves differ. At u=1/4 (prior 7/12,1/12,1/3), the native calculation finds a nonzero I(A)-I(B). A receiver choosing between abstention and an action with state payoffs (1,-1,0)-1/12 has optimal expected value E[( (b1-b2)/3 -1/12 )_+] at fair prior. A obtains zero, B obtains 1/36. Reversing the sign of the state payoff vector reverses the preference. These are exact decision reversals; not empirical human rewards. The complete raw spoke audit is blind to them. A fourth independent planar line (nonparallel to the first three) necessarily distinguishes their projected receipts.

**Affine-term countercontrol.** The mirrored pair (x,y)=(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1) instead agrees in x, y and x-y projections but not x+y. Embedded the same way, the third row's contrast marginal is (-1/4,-1/4,1/2) versus its reflection; its h_3 differs. The odd-power expansion of g shows the difference is nonzero: odd moments of order >=3 all have the same nonzero sign and their coefficients are negative. Thus even I(p0) differs despite three equal curvature receipts. The earlier full-information counterexample works precisely because the additional affine data agree. This negative control prevents interpreting every projection collision as a raw Shannon collision.

## Adaptive reconstruction and known grids

Use d independent initial directions. Each exact projected law gives a list of at most m possible coordinates. In their coordinate basis, every genuine support point lies in the Cartesian product G of these lists, with N<=m^d candidate points. Choose one more covector (1,t,...,t^(d-1)) injective on G: each pair of different grid points forbids at most d-1 real t values. Searching integer t=0,1,...,(d-1) choose(N,2) therefore must find an admissible direction. The final projected law reveals the mass of each candidate point by its unique code; transform back and reject any violation of normalization, positivity, support bound or zero mean. The method uses d+1 directional receipts independent of m, or just one when the support grid is known in advance. It is constructive but can be exponentially expensive in d and numerically fragile. The native witness implements exact integer-weighted coordinate-grid recovery with a computed final direction; it starts from projected laws, not an arbitrary noisy entropy-curve fitting procedure. The corresponding successive-determination idea is established in Gardner and Gritzmann's tomography work, and known-support injection is Proposition 2.3 in Fraiman et al.

For a bounded integer lattice with coordinates in [-R,R], the known-grid direction (1,B,...,B^(d-1)), B=2R+1, is injective by radix encoding after a common coordinate shift. This may demand a very narrow physical prior sweep when rescaled to fit the simplex. Finite precision is not an incidental issue: on a compact strict-interior region, nearby posterior point locations produce nearby I curves, while shifted finite supports can be disjoint and have TV=1. For example equal masses at +/-a and +/- (1+epsilon)a (small nonzero a in the zero-sum space) have TV=1 for epsilon>0 but their complete I surfaces converge uniformly as epsilon->0 (continuity on a compact domain with strict positivity). So a bound in projected TV cannot be repackaged as a bound in noisy entropy values without an additional conditioning theorem. The exact finite-sample theorem is not a floating-point tolerance policy.

Native Python witness and receipt supplied separately and, when committed, linked in this directory. It checks exact rational curvature at 447 interior spoke positions, numerical raw I computed independently from the channel tables, exact opposed rewards and 180 weighted adaptive reconstructions in dimensions 2..5. The actual integer samples are read back through matched-template decoding; 144 symbols recovered across two recordings. These are exact shuffled score inventories, not independent random trials or physical acoustic tests. The proposed Prism Organ would let a performer choose a prior direction and reveal an ambiguity-breaking fourth spoke; it remains an instrument hypothesis, not an implemented real-time synthesizer.
