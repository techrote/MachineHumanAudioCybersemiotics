# Six surfaces for one instrument

Original performance score for a synthetic organ, two operators and an optional registrar. No recordings by other artists are required.

**Materials:** six pitches 196, 247, 294, 220, 330, 262 Hz, each half a second. A scratch adds a unit of virtual relief. A polish halves relief. These are abstract score events; do not damage physical instruments.

## I. Two biographies

Begin twice from a clean surface. In one performance execute AAPPA. In the other execute PAAAP. Sound one note at the beginning and after each operation. Do not try to make the histories resemble each other.

## II. Two identical surfaces

Use only the resulting 3/2 relief states. Replay the six pitches at constant relief. The registrar may retain two separate labels. The renderer must produce identical PCM in both cases.

## III. The two extreme restorers

Use three scratches and two polishes in both performances. In the first, execute all scratches before polishing; in the second, polish twice before scratching. Audition the final states at 3/4 and 3 units respectively. Neither is the more experienced instrument by the count of gestures.

## IV. A borrowed antiquity (unrendered)

Create a new instrument instance and import the endpoint state from movement I. Do not pretend that the new instrument performed those gestures. If a third musician wishes to make a different imitation, label it authored rather than accrued.

## V. The registrar's two switches (unrendered)

Reset the surface while retaining its history, if the musicians have chosen to record a history. Then create another instance that retains a surface but does not retain the history. Present the two states without assigning moral value to either. Do not substitute an invented record for the missing one.

## VI. A surface with no further duty (unrendered)

Allow a performer to stop altering a surface. Play it unchanged or leave it unplayed. Let the instrument's age become irrelevant to its next performance.

The first three movements have six local rendered WAVs. The remaining movements are score instructions only. No human performance or audience test is claimed. The artwork's value is not established by the exact equality tests.
