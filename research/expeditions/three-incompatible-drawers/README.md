# The Museum With Three Incompatible Drawers

*An expedition in the afterlife of gestures, faceted listening and the cost of making a collection into a hierarchy.*

26 November 2026 · MHAC #672 · exploratory humanities/art and synthetic software, outside frozen Tracks E/G · Draft/HOLD.

## 1. An imaginary museum (original essay)

The curator has eight small recordings. She possesses only one cabinet, and every drawer can contain smaller drawers or recordings, but never a second copy of a recording. A visitor requests all ascending sounds. Another requests all pulsating sounds. A third asks for all sounds containing a particular spectral structure.

The curator can satisfy any visitor. She cannot give every visitor a single drawer.

This is not a failure of the recordings. It is a consequence of the cabinet's architecture. Some things belong together for one purpose and must separate for another. A useful archive makes this tension visible rather than pretending that it has discovered the natural family of each sound.

Consider a rising gesture which accompanies the opening of a protective enclosure in one fictional scene, and the lifting of a barrier to an invading force in another. It would be a mistake to infer a single affect, moral valence or instruction from the direction of its pitch. Yet the recurrence can be aesthetically valuable. The artist may make the recurrence audible precisely to bring the two scenes into conflict. This is an authored analogy, not an experiment on listeners or a historical claim about a Warburgian gesture.

The proposed instrument is an atlas of gestures. Its primary object is not an oscillator, a preset, or a finished piece but a collection whose relationships can be rearranged without rewriting the collection. Its second object is an editorial decision: the selection of which relationship deserves to govern today's audition.

An atlas is not a dictionary with a picturesque interface. A dictionary entry tends to settle what its headword denotes; the atlas can put several competing readings on the same board. In an instrument the readings may become executable scores. The composer can play the arrangement of the archive.

The opposing aesthetic is equally important. A composer may *want* the cabinet to force one distinction above another, because traversing three drawers to collect an unexpected family can generate a rhythm, a pause or a change in texture. A hierarchy can be an expressive obstruction rather than a defect. We should expose the cost rather than presume that all artistic impediments should be removed.

This is an ethical corrective: our toy cabinet has voluntary aesthetic constraints, not the stakes of medical, racial or administrative classification. **Interpretive synthesis:** Warburg's historical juxtapositions and the selective attention associated with sound-object listening pull in different directions. One asks us to restore histories and relationships; the other can bracket them for an encounter with sound. A musical tool could alternate between these practices. Faceted information retrieval offers the engineering substrate but does not prescribe the artistic relationships. A parameter facet is not the same thing as an iconographic argument; an atlas should permit contested interpretive links as well as exact authored tags. The latter are the only objects of the mathematical model below.

**Counterargument:** A relational archive can manufacture persuasive patterns simply by selecting and placing its specimens. A similarity does not prove a historical lineage, a depicted action, or even a psychologically salient resemblance. The proposed tool must make the construction of a view inspectable: selected items, exclusions, facet definitions, relation author, version, alternative view, and what would count as contradictory evidence. A deliberately fictional panel may be freely invented but must not be labelled historical evidence.

## 3. Finite cabinet: exact synthetic construction

Eight sound tiles are indexed by the full binary product of three independently authored parameters: contour (ascending/descending chirp), amplitude shape (single broad envelope / three-lobed envelope), and texture (one sinusoid / three harmonics). The six one-facet families contain four tiles apiece. No claim of perceptual orthogonality follows from the construction.

Define a rooted full binary tree whose leaves are the eight distinct tiles, each appearing exactly once. Every internal node represents the set of all its descendant leaves. Sibling order is irrelevant. A query for a facet value must be answered by a disjoint cover of complete subtrees containing exactly the requested leaves; no false positives or filtering inside a selected subtree. Count one unit per selected subtree, not per click, leaf, second, or listener effort.

**Why there cannot be one drawer per family.** Subtree leaf sets form a laminar family: two sets are disjoint or one includes the other. But the ascending set and the pulsating set have a two-tile intersection; each also has two tiles outside the other. They cross. Therefore a single-copy hierarchy cannot make both of those queries individual subtrees. This obstruction is independent of binary branching.

For each axis a, combine the costs of querying value 0 and value 1 into a coordinate C_a. A facet-first tree with a fixed axis order has costs (2,4,8) in that order: two root-child subtrees, four grandchildren, and eight leaves. An adaptive tree first splits on contour, then divides the ascending side by pulse and the descending side by texture. Its cost vector is (2,6,6). The total is unchanged, but the worst axis cost drops by two. This is a different classification, not a better sound.

For a node S, if all its leaves share coordinate a, the joint cover cost for the two values is 1. Otherwise it is the sum of both children's costs. Enumerate unordered bipartitions by requiring the smallest indexed leaf to belong to the first child. A singleton has vector (1,1,1). Multiplying child multiplicities and aggregating recursively enumerates every tree exactly once. Independently count maximal monochromatic subtree covers by starting from the eight singleton leaves and subtracting one for each monochromatic internal node. The two computations share the split generator but differ in the cover calculation. The direct enumeration also checks unique cluster sets and the known count 13!! = 135135.

An analytical total-cost lower bound is available. There are seven internal nodes; the root has no constant axis. A size-two cluster can be constant on at most two axes; size three or four at most one; size five or larger none. At most four internal nodes have size two. If c is their number, the total savings from the 24 singleton contributions is at most 2c+(6-c)=6+c <=10. Thus total cost is at least 14, attained by both example trees. The worst-coordinate bound 6 is certified here by complete finite enumeration, not claimed as a general theorem for arbitrary sizes.

Execution result: 135135 unique hierarchies, 183 distinct cost vectors. Minimum total 14 (12 trees); minimum worst-axis cost 6. Six trees attain both minima; none has all three coordinates <=5. The six fixed axis-order trees attain minimum total but have worst coordinate 8. See laboratory.py; the exhaustive histogram is generated into the local receipt, not a canonical registry.

A faceted index can expose each value as one selection, six selections for six queries, because sets can overlap. This is a different selection contract, not a claim of six physical folders or measured UI efficiency. An ordinary tag search might be preferable to a dedicated instrument. Multi-copy directories, aliases, filtering, DAGs and nonbinary trees are outside the enumerated model. The laminar obstruction still applies to any single-copy tree under exact-node representation. The costs ignore click depth, visual search, file access, ordering, storage and learning.

## 4. Original performance score: Three exhibitions, one inventory

Eight 0.75 s mono tiles, 16 kHz signed 16-bit PCM; each identifier is three bits in contour/pulse/texture order. Bit 0 means rising 180–360 Hz / broad envelope / sinusoid, bit 1 means falling 360–180 Hz / three-lobed envelope / harmonic mixture. Chirp phase is integrated analytically. The harmonic mixture is normalized by the sum of its coefficients and all tiles use a common peak bound (12000 integer samples); loudness is not matched.

The three six-second montages concatenate the *same* eight PCM tiles without overlap, once each. Order is authored by first sorting by the indicated facet, with lexicographic tie order:
- I. The Surveyor (contour): 000 001 010 011 | 100 101 110 111.
- II. The Clockmaker (pulse): 000 001 100 101 | 010 011 110 111.
- III. The Metallurgist (texture): 000 010 100 110 | 001 011 101 111.

The middle division is conceptually a doorway, not additional silence. The listener may attend to continuity of one axis and discontinuity of another. The score makes no prediction of what listeners actually hear. As an optional fourth, unrendered, live movement, allow a second curator to reorder the tiles by a newly invented interpretive axis, such as fictive roles of invitation and refusal. Require them to specify which relationships are fictional and which are exact features. Do not make contour a proxy for the role; one can assign either role to any tile.

A more radical next artwork would use independently authored testimonies: three curators classify the same actual field recordings with competing explanations of their origin. Preserve each original clip, evidence link, and disagreement, rather than selecting a majority label. No such recordings or human testimonies are included in this expedition.

## 5. Exaptation, countertests and disposition

**Instrument:** Gesture Atlas VSTi. A persistent sample or oscillator-preset pool, multiple simultaneous facet views, and a panel sequencer treating a change of grouping as a performance gesture. A curator can bind a macro to the currently selected group, swap to a different facet, and hear a different ensemble without moving or duplicating a source. Separate (a) exact parameter tags, (b) measured descriptors with method/version, (c) author annotations and (d) explicitly fictional roles. Keep an immutable source ID and versioned view definitions. Permit overlapping groups, explicit union/intersection, and a preview of the resulting membership. Never silently replace unknown with negative.

**Effect:** Drawer Switch VSTfx. A bounded slice buffer plus manually authored tag lanes; audition or rearrange slice groups by switching the active facet, without altering stored source clips. Real-time implementation would need fixed preallocated voices, bounded slice durations, smooth transitions, deterministic scheduling, and off-audio-thread view compilation. Neither VST is implemented here.

**Embedded interface:** a 320×240 CYD panel, eight tile glyphs in a 4×2 grid, three facet buttons, optional joint filter, and a persistent selected tile marker. Reorder by view rather than physically copying clips. Use a clear indicator of count and selected axis. Retain an ordinary list as a baseline. No hardware qualification.

**A different research question:** Can an interface help a listener distinguish an acoustic family (e.g., a shared amplitude shape) from a task family (e.g., messages requiring a similar response)? Construct a fully crossed synthetic set with independent task labels and authored acoustic attributes, counterbalance assignment, and compare (1) ordinary explicit text/flat filter, (2) single hierarchy, (3) multi-facet visual filter, (4) visual filter plus optional sound examples. Measure wrong inclusion, wrong exclusion, query completion, task correctness and time separately; include noninformative and conflicting tags, an unlabelled control, and accessibility conditions. A visual-only baseline may perform best. No participants, statistical analysis of human data, or communication benefit is asserted. In consequential contexts explicit status/action labels and accessible alternatives are necessary; fictional roles are not operational instructions.

**Original fragment: The fourth cabinet.** The conservator opened the drawer labelled rising. The clockmaker opened the drawer labelled three. Both found the same recording. When the director demanded a single correct drawer, the curator handed him a map of the cabinet. He asked for a drawer for the map. She left it on the wall.
