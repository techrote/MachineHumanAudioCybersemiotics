# The Clock That Draws a Triangle

Refs #780, #775, #776 · 27 November 2026. Exploratory mathematical DSP, separate from frozen Track E/G. One ChatGPT agent performed source reading, proofs, implementation and computational cross-checks; no human review, participant evidence, physical qualification or priority claim.

## An active reference instead of a passive waveform

The [lost-tick expedition](https://github.com/techrote/MachineHumanAudioCybersemiotics/pull/776) proves that a known nonconstant additive waveform can hide a deletion for 47 consecutive differences (and longer under sparse substitutions). Rather than infer timing from arbitrary payload shapes, design an independently observable timing pilot. This is a different contract, not an improvement to the previous receiver on the same input.

Let x(t) be a scalar or Euclidean-vector pilot with norm <= A, A>0. On N coherently indexed ticks, the reference is exact, calibrated and anchored. The settled offset s belongs to a known set of K consecutive integer offsets, normalized to {0,...,K-1}. For the three-offset case this is equivalent to {-1,0,+1} by shifting the reference origin. Hypothesis c_s(t)=x(t+s). There are at most e arbitrary **symbol** replacements, f flagged erased symbols (indices retained), and total Euclidean dense error <= epsilon on the remaining good symbols. For vectors a symbol is an entire vector, not one scalar channel. The pilot is observed independently of unknown payload, with known channel coordinates and gain. No insertion/deletion inside the settled window, unknown drift, stochastic noise model, psychoacoustic claim or physical radio/audio assumption is made.

## Exact mixed-fault distance and abstention

For two codewords a,b, form N squared symbol distances d_t=||a(t)-b(t)||^2. Write T_r(a,b) for the sum of the N-r smallest distances, r=min(N,2e+f). Define D=min_{a!=b} T_r(a,b), where distinct hypotheses can have identical codewords. Then the supremal safe dense-noise radius is sqrt(D)/2 whenever D>0; strict inequality is required. If D=0, even zero-noise universal identification fails. For a specified erasure mask, use surviving coordinates and r=2e.

Proof: if a received record is feasible for both, remove the common erasures and the union of their at most e substitutions each. On the remaining coordinates the triangle inequality gives distance <=2 epsilon. Any surviving sum is >=T_r. Conversely take the r largest differences (or all coordinates if r saturates N); erase up to f, assign up to e removed locations to each hypothesis's arbitrary substitutions, putting the other hypothesis's value there. Put the midpoint on the rest. Both explanations then have squared dense error T_r/4. This attains the ambiguity boundary, not merely a decoder-dependent bound. This argument also handles overlaps and zero differences. A candidate-list decoder scores each reference by summing its smallest N-f-e squared residuals (or zero if fewer than e survive); retain candidates whose score <=epsilon^2. With fewer than f erasures, use the actual surviving count. Each score is the minimum over allowable replacement supports, so this list is exact under the model. Empty list means model inconsistency; multiple candidates means ambiguity; no arbitrary tie breaking.

## Scalar clock: phase energy versus local historical separation

For three consecutive scalar values in [-A,A], their three pairwise squared differences sum to <=8A^2 (a convex quadratic achieves its maximum over the cube at a vertex). Hence the minimum of the three settled-offset distances over N ticks is <=8NA^2/3. For N=3k the period-three pilot (-A,-A,A) attains equality: each pair sees k zeros and 2k squared differences 4A^2. With a binary alphabet each triple has at most two differing pairs, so min Hamming distance <=floor(2N/3); the same pilot attains the bound for any start and N, including nonmultiples of three. But it has adjacent equal samples, so one deletion at either side of that equality gives the same noiseless history. A pure alternating pilot is even worse for three-way phase: offsets differing by two are exactly aliased.

Impose a stronger local requirement: all three values x(t),x(t+1),x(t+2) have pairwise absolute separation >=g, with 0<=g<=A. Then for ordered values u<=v<=w, gaps a=v-u, b=w-v satisfy a,b>=g and a+b<=2A. The sum a^2+b^2+(a+b)^2 is convex on this triangle, maximized at (g,2A-g) or its reversal. Therefore the sharp bound for N=3k is

min_{s!=s'} ||c_s-c_s'||^2 <= N(8A^2-4Ag+2g^2)/3.

Equality is achieved by repeating (-A,-A+g,A), for any start and real g in [0,A]. Thus a scalar pilot with maximum instantaneous three-way gap g=A sacrifices 25% of the optimal squared settled-phase distance (2 versus 8/3 per tick, at A=1). It removes local equalities. This tradeoff is for all three offsets per tick; imposing only adjacent separation is a weaker, different optimization. The peak constraint does not require equal RMS energy or zero DC.

## Sparse corruption changes the scalar optimum

For the same period-three family, let N=3k, r=2e+f <=N. The sorted per-pair distance inventory consists of k copies each of g^2, (2A-g)^2 and 4A^2. Thus the trimmed squared distance is exactly

- r<=k: k*g^2+k*(2A-g)^2+(k-r)*4A^2;
- k<=r<=2k: k*g^2+(2k-r)*(2A-g)^2;
- 2k<=r<=3k: (3k-r)*g^2.

Each formula is convex in g. The maximum over this family is therefore attained by g=0 (binary) or g=A (equispaced ternary), not by an intermediate gap. Comparing endpoints, the binary is strictly preferred for r<5k/3, ternary strictly preferred for 5k/3<r<3k, and they tie at 5k/3 if integral. Both vanish at r=3k. This is a maximin distance result under the specified deterministic error budget, not an empirical choice of modulation.

The scalar ternary solution is globally optimal over *all* bounded real pilots for r>=2N/3 when N=3k: at each tick some pair among the three sorted values differs by <=A. By pigeonhole at least one of the three hypothesis pairs has >=N/3 such small distances, hence its N-r smallest distances are all <=A^2. The ternary pilot attains (N-r)A^2. The untrimmed global optimum was proved above. For intermediate r the period-three endpoint result is proved only within the stated family; exhaustive three-level finite-window checks at N=3,6,9 match it, but neither those checks nor convexity in g prove optimality among arbitrary real longer sequences.

## A triangle removes the compromise; a general simplex theorem

Allow a vector pilot of dimension at least K-1, with the same total Euclidean peak norm A. For any K vectors v_i at a tick,

sum_{i<j} ||v_i-v_j||^2 = K sum_i ||v_i||^2 - ||sum_i v_i||^2 <= K^2 A^2.

Remove any common r ticks from the comparison. Averaging the remaining pairwise distances gives some pair with squared distance <= (N-r)*2K A^2/(K-1). The trimmed distance for that pair can only be smaller. Therefore every pilot, even a nonperiodic one, satisfies

D <= (N-r)*2K A^2/(K-1).

Repeat the K vertices of a centered regular simplex of radius A cyclically. All K candidate offsets select different vertices at every tick and all pair distances equal 2K A^2/(K-1). This attains the upper bound for **every N and r**, without divisibility constraints. In particular K=3 needs only two independent dimensions and gives a rotating equilateral triangle with squared separation 3A^2 at every tick. The exact optimal noise radius is A sqrt(3(N-r))/2. At A=1,N=12,r=0, binary scalar, ternary scalar and triangle squared distances are 32,24,36; at r=8 they are 0,4,12. All comparisons hold the total per-symbol Euclidean peak bound fixed, not the dimension, channel use, bandwidth or implementation cost.

For exact integer checks use v_i=K e_i-1 in K coordinates. Its radius squared is K(K-1), its pairwise squared distance is 2K^2, and the vectors lie in a (K-1)-dimensional zero-sum subspace. Divide by sqrt(K(K-1))/A to normalize. An orthonormal basis of that subspace is equivalent in the real mathematical model; integer PCM and channel calibration can perturb the ideal geometry. No claim of a perfectly equilateral two-dimensional integer lattice representation.

For a single deletion boundary k, the candidate switches from x(t) to x(t+1) at k. Two boundaries k<l have exactly l-k differing symbols for a simplex pilot, so their trimmed squared distance is c*max(0,l-k-r), c=2K A^2/(K-1). Under the mixed-fault model, two histories can coincide iff c*max(0,l-k-r)<=4epsilon^2. Thus the maximal separation between compatible boundaries in an N-tick history is min(N,r+floor(4epsilon^2/c)), with endpoints inclusive and exact mathematical arithmetic. This proves sharp pairwise ambiguity diameter over all admissible received records (not that all intervening candidates necessarily occur). Even an optimal pilot cannot recover exact adjacent event histories under one arbitrary substitution; it also cannot recover unbounded absolute time from a periodic phase marker.

## Executable qualification and retained failures

Run node laboratory.cjs (no dependencies). The actual execution receipt records 35,398 integer triple upper-bound comparisons, 90 attainments, 3,036 exact period-three trim comparisons, and all ternary finite windows of lengths N+2 for N=3,6,9 (243+6,561+177,147). The respective optimal squared distance profiles at A=1 are [8,4,1,0], [16,12,8,4,2,1,0], and [24,20,16,12,8,4,3,2,1,0]. A separately written native Python enumeration reproduced these three arrays during investigation. It was an exploratory same-agent cross-check, not independent review. The retained JS laboratory also verifies 84 integer simplex pair distances, 17,052 strict-radius candidate decodes, 17,052 explicit ambiguity midpoint constructions and 69,965 history-trim comparisons. The strict trials use dense error one quarter of pair separation on common positions; they are structured witnesses, not randomly sampled channel measurements. Numerical floating-point operations in those checks are exactly representable dyadic values; the decisive finite scalar comparisons are integers.

Negative controls: any period-K pilot aliases offsets differing by K. Unknown rotation of the vector channel can also absorb a cyclic simplex shift: a permutation of coordinates maps every vertex to its successor. Calibration is therefore a real assumption, not an optional implementation detail. An unobserved additive payload can cancel a scalar pilot exactly, e.g. (-1,0,1)+(1,0,-1)=0. This is even possible inside a model allowing arbitrary period-three payloads. A reserved independent channel or a separately proved payload/marker separation is necessary; the present proof says nothing about an arbitrary mixture. The scalar binary pilot has nonzero mean, while the equally spaced ternary and regular simplex are centered; DC-free, spectral-mask, loudness and energy constraints are different optimization problems. Vector dimension costs communication resources. No physical advantage is claimed solely from the peak-norm comparison.
