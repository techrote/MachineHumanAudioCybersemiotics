## The object and its testimony — interpretive essay

A curator commissions an electronic urn. It has no microphone, but possesses a loudspeaker. When the museum closes it sings, in the first person, about the bell that rang at noon.

The curator calls this a reconstruction. The poet calls it a monologue. The archivist asks which record supplies the time. The technician says that the instrument has no input capable of hearing a bell.

None of these positions need contradict the physical account of the apparatus. The disagreement concerns what its performance is doing.

Keats's Ode on a Grecian Urn provides a particularly useful prehistory of this problem. In stanza II (lines 11–14) the painted musicians' melodies are valued precisely without ordinary audible realization. The poem supplies a different kind of audition. In stanza IV the speaker imagines an emptied town that lies outside the described procession; the work becomes the occasion for inventing an off-frame world. In stanza V the silent form is represented as addressing humanity. This is my reading of the transitions, not a claim to settle the famous dispute over the final lines' voice. The urn's silence is not a technical defect that a loudspeaker should repair.

The modern museum might nonetheless install a speaker within the urn. A voice that emerged there would not thereby become archaeological evidence of an ancient performance. The most convincing reconstruction might be the most dangerous if its presentation disguises how it was constructed. Conversely, a deliberately impossible monologue can be excellent art precisely because it does not pretend to be a recovered voice.

Quintilian's account of prosopopoeia admits the imagined speeches of absent people, gods, the dead and cities. Its rhetorical efficacy depends in part on the plausibility of the attributed discourse; the invented speaker can acquire persuasive force that the orator's own voice could not. His discussion separates it from apostrophe, the turning of an address. A machine that says a city is speaking is therefore not simply the inverse of a person speaking to a city. The earlier MHAC apostrophe expedition #602 explored address; the present one moves the instability to the originating end.

The word "voice" consequently conceals several questions: Which body produces the sound? Who arranged the material? Which figure is portrayed? On whose behalf is a statement made? What supports its content? These questions can have different answers. Giving the statue a microphone would answer only some of them.

Manning and Gershon's reading of Goffman furnishes a productive vocabulary for pulling these relations apart. A loudspeaker can be an animator, not necessarily an author or principal. The represented figure can itself be an inanimate object. Their discussion further distinguishes the actual technological division of roles from participants' understanding of those roles. I use this as a design inspiration, not as a proof that the categories exhaust nonhuman communication.

My proposed addition is to make the allocation of these relations an independently editable musical score. A choir is not just a population of voices; it can be a population of obligations, quotations and fictional personae. The timbral score and the footing score need not coincide. The same oscillator can play on behalf of an invented character, relay an archival quotation, encode a synthetic observation, or perform a scoped delegated statement. None of these modes entails consciousness or emotional experience.

The austerity of a machine voice does not solve the problem. A perfectly affectless utterance can still present a fictional reconstruction as a verified report. A richly expressive puppet can honestly disclose that it is an invention. Affective design and provenance design are distinct questions.

## The four depositions — original artistic score

A single urn is represented by four notes (220, 330, 275, 440 Hz), each 300 ms long, all with the same squared-sine envelope. This is an arbitrary authored sign for the proposition "a bell sounded at noon", not an imitation of the bell and not a code learned by listeners.

| Movement | Representation | Current assertion principal | Supporting material |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. The poet | Fictional urn monologue | None | No claim record |
| II. The archivist | Quotation presented as quotation | None | Archive record, not proof of the quoted event |
| III. The registrar | Gallery's report | Gallery | Synthetic sensor record |
| IV. The emissary | Gallery's delegated assertion | Curator | Synthetic sensor record plus exact scoped mandate |

The fictional score-writer, oscillator, issuer and represented figure remain constant. The null principal means no current assertion of the event in this toy; it does NOT mean absence of responsibility for publishing fiction or quoting accurately. Likewise the quote record is represented by a matching proposition identifier, not a full quotation-fidelity mechanism.

Perform the unframed motif four times while changing only the printed caption. Then perform the four prefixed variants: 550/660/770/880 Hz respectively for 200 ms, 100 ms silence, the unchanged motif. The prefixes are conventional labels, not inherently meaningful acoustic features. They can be audible without being understood, and can be forged. They are a proposed artistic contrast, not a provenance proof.

For a gallery installation, place the same sounding transducer inside the urn throughout. Keep an independently visible ledger of the role assignments. The imagined work is not a real historical testimony, and no live installation has been built.

A fifth, unperformed movement: a curator invites the audience to change the fictional figure without changing the report's accountable principal. A sixth: the archivist quotes a curator who later retracts the statement; preserve both contexts with their separate dates. Do not retroactively convert the quotation into the gallery's current assent.

## Finite construction and actual execution

The lab is deliberately small. The event identifier is fixed. The admitted records are exactly the four listed cases. Fiction has no evidence/mandate/quote/principal; quotation has a matching archive record without current assertion; report has a matching evidence record and the issuer as principal; delegation has matching evidence and a mandate bound to issuer, principal and event. No grant is real. Wrong event, wrong issuer, missing evidence, mismatched quote, or an invented mode cannot satisfy the relevant contract.

The set of candidates is the Cartesian product of four modes, three principal values, three evidence values, four mandate values and three quote values: 432 assignments. The independent explicitly enumerated expected tuple for each mode is compared with all candidates. Four admitted, 428 rejected. An extra unknown-mode negative control passes.

An unframed renderer ignores the role assignment. Consequently its observation function maps all four modes to the same byte string. Any deterministic classifier of that string alone must return the same label for all four; under a stipulated uniform prior its maximum accuracy is one quarter. This is an elementary non-identifiability witness about this constructed mapping, not a model of human listeners or all acoustic communication. A prefix can make the rendered signals distinct under the stipulated renderer; distinctness is not authentication or demonstrated comprehension.

## Exaptation — the Footing Organ and the counterargument

A VSTi could separate timbre, figure, composer and mandate as independent control lanes. A performer could rotate personae over sustained voices, or pass the role of quoted composer across a fixed patch. A VSTfx could preserve audio unchanged while layering a clearly separate authored margin, or audition alternative provenance labels for artistic material. A CYD touch interface could show a sounding object at the center with source, figure, author and principal nodes around it. Tap a node to change its role; do not implicitly change unrelated nodes. These are design sketches, not implementations.

A more interesting instrument might let the composer refuse to assign a principal at all. For fictional art this is useful; for an operational assertion, an unassigned accountable source may demand a different interface treatment. The instrument should never automatically infer an authorization from an audio prefix. It should be possible to mute the explanatory margin, but the operating interface must still display explicit status where consequences matter.

Counterargument: the apparent unity of a singing object may be the work's entire achievement. Requiring an elaborate provenance diagram can flatten a poem or kill a puppet's theatrical presence. There is no reason every musical sound requires a responsible principal. The proposed apparatus is valuable because it makes the choice available, not because role disaggregation is a universal aesthetic virtue.

For MHAC, a testable proposition is that separating quote, fiction, report and delegated assertion at the interface may reduce incorrect attribution of current commitments. A future synthetic study would cross an explicit role label with an identical carrier, compare label-only, audio-only, both and deliberately mismatched presentations, and score mode identification, evidence attribution, endorsement and authorized action separately. Ordinary plain text is the serious baseline. Neither a successful fixture nor acoustically distinctive prefix establishes a communication improvement. A negative result in which the structured text dominates would be valuable. No human study is authorized or performed here.

## Speculative coda — the second witness

The museum added a microphone to the urn.

For the first time, it could record the bell. It continued to tell the same story as before.

The conservator replaced its loudspeaker. The technician replaced its microphone. The poet removed the bell from the exhibition.

On the last day, the urn sang again.

The curator called this proof of continuity.

The archivist asked, "Continuity of which thing?"
