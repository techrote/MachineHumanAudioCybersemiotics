## 1. A different beginning for machine language

Imagine a machine that has not been told what a mouth is. Its first utterance is a change in the distances between the six resonances of a fictitious material. Its second is a contraction. Its third is a series of contractions in increasingly rapid succession. A technician calls the third a warning; a child calls it an animal; a composer writes it three times and changes its ending.

The machine has not thereby acquired a language. The observers have begun to put its output to use. The interesting artistic possibility is that a dictionary could grow out of performances rather than prescribing them in advance. Instead of selecting a human word and removing its emotional colouring, we might begin with a repertoire of articulations no human mouth can produce. We can subsequently name these, demonstrate their relationships to fictional events, or decline to assign them any meaning.

This is an aesthetic project, not a theory of the emergence of natural language. Indeed, a machine that never resembles a human voice might still be described in animate, affective or intentional terms. Removing familiar phonemes does not remove the listener's interpretative activity. The original MHAC preference for non-human sounding communication should not be converted into the claim that non-human sound is immune to anthropomorphism.

### A note on the two kinds of imitation

The distinction is between imitating an event's acoustic product and constructing an analogue of its temporal shape. The sound of a collapsing tower is one possible object of imitation; an abstract sequence of narrowing, displacement, collision and dispersal is another. A silent or hypothetical event can have a designed sonic depiction without having made an original sound.

The latter approach is seductive for MHAC: one could render growth, compression, diffusion, oscillation, interruption, or hesitation without necessarily using a recording of the physical event. But an authored analogy is not an established interpretation. A narrowing spectral distribution may be a productive image of contraction to its author and an image of distance or occlusion to someone else. It may be nothing of the sort. Even if the intended resemblance is recognized, the listener has not thereby identified the machine state, its cause or the appropriate action.

## 2. Source encounters and disagreements

Reading ledger, dated 2026-11-26. These are selected reading passages, not a completed literature review. Direct web access and selected passages inspected unless stated otherwise. No original recording or complete source corpus retained.

**S1 — Hugo Ball, Eroeffnungs-Manifest, 1. Dada-Abend, Zürich, 14 July 1916.** [Projekt Gutenberg transcription, pendo Verlag 1991](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/hugo-ball/books/hugo-ball-eroeffnungs-manifest-1-dada-abend/). The dated text, especially the final four paragraphs beginning with his account of reading verse, was read in German. Ball moves between a wish to abandon inherited language and the invention of words proportionate to imagined things. He also questions the need to name the tree at all. The manifesto is not a psychological treatise. My reading is that it authorizes two contrary directions: constructing a new articulation and resisting the demand for articulation. It is worth taking both seriously. The political and aesthetic circumstances of wartime Dada are not reducible to an engineering technique.

**S2 — Getty Research Institute, The Scores Project, object 005.** [Object 005](https://www.getty.edu/publications/scores/object-index/005/), published 2025, metadata and descriptive paragraph. Getty identifies the 1916 photograph of Ball at Cabaret Voltaire as a performance in a cubist costume, and records the inscription referring to verse without words. The photograph's catalogue entry is a source for the material staging of the event, not evidence of what the audience heard. My interpretation: removing lexical meaning did not remove theatrical embodiment; the costumed performer remains part of the apparatus through which the poem is encountered.

**S3 — Steve McCaffery, Cacophony, Abstraction, and Potentiality: The Fate of the Dada Sound Poem, 2009, pp. 118–128.** [Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226657448.003.0010). Indexed abstract consulted; direct publisher retrieval failed. McCaffery locates the contradictions of Dada sound poetry in Ball's theory of phonemic rhythm and records interpretive uncertainty around his reported transformation during performance. This is a secondary reading lead; the chapter and diary have not been read. It qualifies any easy story about phonemes liberating poetry from culture: the project itself may be caught up in beliefs about origin, ritual and memory.

**S4 — Mark Dingemanse, ‘Ideophone’ as a comparative concept, 2019, in Kimi Akita and Prashant Pardeshi (eds.), Ideophones, Mimetics and Expressives, pp. 13–34.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1075/ill.16.02din); [accessible chapter PDF](https://www.smg.surrey.ac.uk/media/134335/dingemanse_2019_ideophone-as-a-comparative-concept.pdf). Abstract, §2.1–2.3, particularly printed pp. 15–19 consulted; page images 1 and 6 (one-based PDF indices) inspected. Dingemanse differentiates conventional lexical class, depiction and perceived form–meaning resemblance. He argues in §2.3 that the framing of an utterance as a depiction can invite the recognition of iconic relations, and emphasizes language-specific convention. The explicit distinction rules out treating an arbitrary laboratory sound as a natural-language ideophone. An invented machine gesture can be ideophone-inspired, but this project has neither a speech community nor evidence of lexical conventionalization. My proposal takes the distinction seriously by treating the synthetic trajectory, the designated interpretation and the invitation to construe it as separately authored objects.

**S5 — Alan K. S. Nielsen and Mark Dingemanse, Iconicity in Word Learning and Beyond: A Critical Review, Language and Speech 64(1), 2021 (online 20 April 2020).** [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/0023830920914339), abstract, §3.4 discussion and §4 conclusion consulted. The authors distinguish effects on learning individual iconic items from the much less supported idea that iconic vocabulary facilitates learning unrelated vocabulary. They discuss aesthetic, affective and situated uses as well. This is a review author's assessment, not an independent appraisal of its underlying experiments. For MHAC it suggests an appropriate negative hypothesis: a compelling demonstration of one gesture may have no measurable transfer to a different gesture, body, convention or task. Expressive force and semantic reliability should be measured independently rather than equated.

**S6 — Ingo R. Titze, Nonlinear source-filter coupling in phonation: theory, JASA 123(5), 2008, pp. 2733–2749.** [PubMed abstract, PMID 18529191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18529191/). Indexed abstract only; direct retrieval exposed a site notice. Titze treats interaction between the vocal source and vocal tract rather than assuming every source and filter can be varied without influencing one another. This is a technical corrective, not the basis for the implemented equations. The virtual mouths here are direct additive oscillators with designed spectral weighting, not vocal tract simulation or an acoustic model of reeds and plates. A later implementation that claims physical modelling would need separate qualification.

### Two incompatible ambitions (interpretation)

Ball's manifesto resists the obligation of a sound to be a word; the linguistic literature describes the productive conventionalization of sound into words. These ambitions need not be reconciled. An instrument should permit a performer to produce a gesture that remains unclassified even after a listener supplies a label. The instrument's user might deliberately reject a useful naming convention, just as a poem can resist paraphrase without being incapable of expression.

An operational interface cannot adopt this freedom wholesale. An artist has the liberty to leave a piece unfinished, ambiguous or undecidable. A system asking a human to perform a consequential action needs validated, explicit semantics and a means of detecting noncomprehension. A sound poem and a command language may share a synthesizer but must not share an unexamined authority model.

## 3. Conceptual construction: the articulation atlas

Let a gesture be a pair of continuous trajectories, x(u) and e(u), with normalized time u in [0,1]. x is a dimensionless deformation coordinate; e is excitation. Neither is a statement about a real event. Each virtual body specifies a base frequency b, six positive partial ratios r_j, an octave span s (signed), and a direction for mapping x to spectral weighting. For the first partial:

    f_0(u) = b * 2**(s*(x(u)-0.5))

The other partials follow fixed ratios to this trajectory. The phase accumulator integrates the per-sample frequencies; Gaussian-shaped index weights are normalized to sum to one. A 20 ms linear edge taper and a common 0.65 full-scale gain bound the output; no per-file loudness normalization is used. This is simple additive trajectory synthesis, not a source-filter physical model.

Three invented gestures:

| Gesture | Deformation coordinate | Excitation |
|---|---|---|
| Unfurl | Smoothstep from 0 to 1 | Broad increasing excitation |
| Knuckle | Three periodic oscillations | Three pronounced pulses |
| Coil | Accelerating sinusoid | Gentle cyclic emphasis |

Three fictitious bodies:

| Body | Base | Ratios | Octave span |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Reed | 165 Hz | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 | +1 |
| Plate | 220 Hz | 1, 1.414, 2.16, 2.91, 4.07, 5.43 | +0.75 |
| Inverse | 260 Hz | 1, 1.5, 2.5, 3.7, 5.1, 6.2 | −1 |

Reed and Plate map increasing x to higher partial-index weight. Inverse maps increasing x to lower weight. The names are working metaphors; these are not recordings or calibrated simulations of the named materials.

The key construction is a negative witness against a casual equation between articulatory direction and acoustic direction. Unfurl is increasing in x for every body, yet its first partial rises by an octave in Reed, rises by three quarters of an octave in Plate, and falls by an octave in Inverse. This is not a psychoacoustic claim about perceived pitch. It follows directly from the signed octave span.

If the body parameters are known and the first-partial parameter is available, inversion gives x = log2(f_0/b)/s + 0.5 for nonzero s. This says nothing about blind estimation from the waveform, and the first partial can be weak when weights favour higher partials. If the body is unknown, even the direction of x cannot be inferred in general: the sign of s is needed. The same intended motion could be mapped to opposite spectral motion without a software error.

An interface could expose two modes: a portable deformation score whose gestures are remapped on every body, and a fixed acoustic score whose frequency trajectory is held constant while the body changes. The difference is useful to a musician deciding whether identity resides in the action or in its sounding consequence. Neither mode is the true definition of a gesture.

### Native execution

[Renderer](laboratory.py), Python 3.13.5, standard library only. Run `python laboratory.py` in a writable working directory (or pass an output directory); do not use Python's optimized mode, which removes assertions. It generates nine 1.2-second gesture/body WAVs and four 4-second montages at 24 kHz, mono 16-bit PCM. The source records model-level coordinate inversion, positive/sub-Nyquist instantaneous partial parameters, normalized nonnegative weights, bounded PCM, silent edges, deterministic rerendering, distinct cell bytes and WAV header/payload readback. An invalid normalized time is rejected. Time-varying synthesis can still create modulation sidebands; instantaneous frequency bounds alone do not prove absence of aliasing.

9,009 model parameter comparisons passed (3 gestures × 1,001 time samples × 3 bodies). The nine cells are distinct. Unfurl's first-partial endpoint ratios are 2, approximately 1.6817928305, and 0.5. Largest measured absolute PCM across the 13 files: 20,726 (approximately 0.6325 full scale). [Receipt](RECEIPT.md) retains exact source and sound hashes. This is native software self-qualification, not an acoustic measurement, a speech recognition test or a listening study.

## 4. Original score: The Museum of Unpronounced Things

Three instruments are constructed from the same gesture grammar. A curator initially refuses to identify what the gestures represent. A second curator reads the first set of labels; a third reads an incompatible set. No physical object is required to have made any of the sounds.

**I — Three mouths.** Play Unfurl through Reed, Plate and Inverse, with the supplied short gaps. The performer uses one gesture score; the audience encounters three sounding realizations. Do not announce which one is the original.

**II — The mouths learn their words.** Play each body's lexicon in the order Unfurl, Knuckle, Coil. Leave the printed names on an easel or hide them. Do not ask whether the audience understood an utterance: there is as yet no agreed proposition to understand.

**III — The curator changes the plaque.** Repeat the same Coil-Plate recording three times. Label the repetitions, respectively, 'a flock tightening its orbit', 'a gear seeking a tooth' and 'an unnamed study'. These are fictional artistic descriptions, not machine measurements. The performer changes no audio. Permit listeners to prefer none of the descriptions. Do not collect responses as research without the proper gates.

**IV — An instrument cannot translate itself.** Repeat Unfurl-Inverse immediately after Unfurl-Reed. Display the common deformation curve and the opposing frequency trajectories. One player calls them the same action; another calls them different sounds. Both statements can be true under the score's definitions.

**V — The tree declines a name.** Play a fourth, independently invented deformation; do not assign it an English gloss. Leave time before the next utterance. The lack of a name is the work, not a task left incomplete.

The supplied four montages are realized score fragments, not a completed recording of this whole participatory performance. No participant was recruited or heard. The labels are original speculative prompts, not meanings assigned to actual linguistic ideophones.

## 5. Exaptation: Articulation Atlas and machine depiction research

**Artistic design proposal.** A gesture painter stores two curves rather than a WAV: deformation and excitation. The renderer holds independent body presets, each exposing spectral ratios, excitation mapping and signed response. A performer can choose one gesture, distribute it among several bodies, or have every body interpret it differently. A depiction editor holds optional text, visual motion or contextual captions; the sound need not encode the caption. A revision operation can preserve the gesture and mutate the mouth, or preserve the sounded trajectory and compensate the gesture. A dictionary panel records names only when deliberately added. Unnamed gestures are valid presets.

The most interesting next development is a body that cannot reproduce some trajectories. The current model maps every deformation to legal parameters; a body with bounded acceleration, resonant hysteresis, limited amplitude or inaccessible spectral regions would have its own articulation constraints. It may require approximation, splitting or a new gesture. Instead of treating this as a failure of the renderer, the composer could make the approximation audible and author a response. This is distinct from declaring a machine to be a speaking subject.

**VSTi candidate.** Articulation Atlas: a polyphonic additive instrument with per-note gesture trajectories, named/unnamed glyphs, interchangeable body presets, signed polarity and a preview of first-partial frequency and excitation. Start with a fixed voice count and bounded partials; smooth parameter changes and independently test real-time stability and host state recall. Conventional modulation envelopes and additive synthesis are substantial prior engineering art; the contribution is the body/gesture/frame separation.

**VSTfx candidate.** Depiction Stamp: a user-triggered resonant spectral-envelope modulation effect on an existing signal. Preserve dry audition; frame labels must never be interpreted as machine state. This is a proposed design, not implemented here.

**CYD / embedded candidate.** A 320×240 display could allocate the upper half to an XY gesture trace and the lower half to three body selectors, a scrub control and an explicit audition button. Begin with a few oscillators and a bounded gesture buffer; never assume this offline Python renderer meets embedded timing. FM-1 implementation should remain subject to its actual hardware and separate repo authority; no other repo was modified.

**Hypothesis, not protocol:** a structurally congruent gesture might aid learning its own explicitly taught label but need not transfer across a body change. Compare unlabelled playback, caption-only, playback with a matched visual depiction, playback with an equally plausible incompatible depiction, and a conventional explicit text baseline. Use the same audio across framing conditions; counterbalance captions and order. Separate direct gesture discrimination, cue-to-label learning, cross-body generalization, interpretation confidence, affect attribution and consequential action selection. Ask no participants in this expedition. Control loudness, duration and discriminability before attributing any effect to framing; the present shared gain is not a loudness match. Include free responses and 'none of these' to avoid forcing agreement. A preregistration-ready study would require approved source and dependency gates, ethics and actual human authorization. A negative result (caption effects only, or no transferable advantage over explicit text) would be entirely useful.

**Critical counterargument.** This may simply be programmable envelope modulation with unusually literary preset names. It does not establish that an acoustic shape has an intrinsic semantics, that listeners recognize a common gesture, that a synthetic mouth can become a linguistic community, or that anthropomorphism decreases. It is plausible that the caption supplies most of the perceived relationship. In operational MHAC, explicit speech/text and independently validated state fields may outperform the entire apparatus. For art, an unnamed and misinterpretable sound may be valuable precisely where the operational system cannot afford one.

### Speculative coda

The curator asked the machine to pronounce the name of a tree. It produced the sound of a surface becoming narrower. She built a mouth for it out of six thin plates. The sound became brighter. She built a second mouth, and the same movement darkened it.

The machine received two labels, then a third label meaning the disagreement between the first two. At the opening the artist removed the labels. The three visitors did not agree about the sound, but they all asked to hear it again. In the museum's notebook, no word was entered.

The tree was never required to speak.
