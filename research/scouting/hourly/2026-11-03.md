### Lead P1 — Parallel: concurrent-message failure has at least two different mechanisms

**Sources:** S1, S2 below. Lacherez, Seah & Sanderson (2007) examined nurses identifying sequential, partially overlapping and strongly overlapping medical melodies under a divided-attention condition; their abstract reports that overlaps were particularly hard to identify. Bolton et al. (2020) examined audible alarm presence in concurrent standard tones; the abstract reports higher miss rates and lower sensitivity under predicted masking. These are **source-reported empirical results in restricted domains**, not a general acoustic law demonstrated for all future MHAC signals.

**New connection:** distinguish (a) *perceptual masking / undetected message* from (b) *audible but unsegregable or confused message identity*. A warning system might satisfy an acoustic level or detection check yet still fail to convey the correct source, priority and requested action in a multi-message context. The R004 alarm-framework comparator already mentions masking, so **masking as such is not a novelty claim**. The proposed extension is a structured two-stage failure taxonomy and an explicit concurrency variable within MHAC's message/action outcomes.

**Suggested next action:** In #5/#6, treat these as pre-cutoff Track E screening candidates rather than silently including them. In #9, if eligible, keep detection, identity and correct-action outcomes separate; code concurrent sound count, temporal overlap, secondary task and signal spectrum. In #12/#13, formulate a *provisional* concurrency/queueing contract: priority alone must not be assumed to guarantee audibility or identity; explore sequencing and overload semantics without prescribing an alarm mixer.

**Evidence class / confidence:** primary-study abstract claims; **high** confidence in bibliographic identity, **moderate** in the domain-specific interpretation, **low** in transfer to MHAC without method/full-text and cross-domain evaluation. **Expected value:** high, especially for multi-agent/multi-event systems. **Falsifier:** show that adequately tested MHAC-like mixed streams maintain detection, identity and action accuracy as concurrency rises, or that its scope needs no concurrent signals.

### Lead P2 — Parallel: speech may increase trust without improving decisions under uncertainty

**Source:** S3. Stokes, Sanker, Cogley & Setlur (2024) report two crowdsourced experiments comparing uncertainty communication through speech, text and visualization. Their published abstract states that visual/text forms were most effective for rational decisions in the tested settings, whereas speech was trusted most despite sometimes producing riskier decisions. These comparisons are task- and representation-specific and must not be generalized to every voice interface.

**New connection:** MHAC distinguishes epistemic certainty, urgency and requested action; **trust or perceived confidence must not be equated with calibrated comprehension or correct action**. Even a deliberately non-human machine voice could acquire credibility from being spoken, irrespective of whether it simulates an emotion. This directly tests a possible shortcut from “neutral prosody” to “epistemically honest communication”; neither the paper nor MHAC's existing assumptions establish that shortcut.

**Suggested next action:** Candidate for #5/#6 (eligibility check: manipulated machine speech and eligible outcomes), and for #9/#13 as an uncertainty-calibration contrast. Prepare an #14 *design-only* test matrix crossing spoken hedge/probability information with non-speech certainty cue, holding the underlying stated probability constant; prespecify interpretation error, decision quality, latency, trust and listener confidence independently. No participants until #20 authorization.

**Evidence class / confidence:** published empirical abstract; **high** identity, **moderate** source-reported comparative direction, **low-to-moderate** direct transfer to alerts. **Expected value:** high; potential adversarial case against simplistic audio certainty encoding. **Falsifier:** performance differences disappear once equivalent information, interface training and decision context are controlled.

### Lead O1 — Orthogonal: workload-sensitive interruption timing belongs to the message lifecycle, not just its sound

**Source:** S4. Shaposhnik (2019; online date) describes two air-traffic-control simulator experiments using pupil-based workload prediction and deferral to lower-load moments. Its publisher abstract reports 76% accuracy for identifying workload moments and reports that the system deferred interruptions appropriately in that experiment. This is an **adjacent interruption-management method**, **not** evidence that delaying auditory hazards is safe; the abstract does not establish that it tested MHAC-like audible warnings.

**New connection:** introduce *interruptibility / delivery window* as a lifecycle state distinct from the message's *semantic urgency* and *truth status*. For example, a nonurgent background status report might be safely deferred in a controlled synthetic task, but a safety-critical hazard may have a hard deadline and must not inherit the same deferral policy. A temporal scheduler can cause missed action even when every individual sound is clear.

**Suggested next action:** In #12, consider explicit fields such as `latest_safe_delivery`, `deferral_reason`, `suppression_expires`, `acknowledgement_required`, and an override rule for true hazards, as hypotheses not adopted schemas. In #14, design a **synthetic-only** queue/replay experiment measuring stale messages, deadline misses and interruption cost; only compare human workload effects after human research approval. No biometrics collection or device control is authorized.

**Evidence class / confidence:** **methodological analogy** grounded by a primary-study abstract; **high** identity, **moderate** feasibility of modelling, **low** transfer to audio/hazard handling. **Expected value:** moderate to high; potentially clarifies R012 lifecycle semantics. **Falsifier:** the proposed scheduler creates more missed or stale consequential action than it prevents; no safe deliverability window can be defined for the target use case.

### Lead X1 — Perpendicular: signal reliability and independent checkability may dominate how persuasive an alarm sounds

**Source:** S5. Manzey, Gérard & Wiczorek (2014) report four laboratory experiments (total N=256) on alarm predictive validity, independent validity-check information and workload. Their abstract reports a cry-wolf nonresponse effect when positive predictive validity was low (≤0.5) and cross-checking unavailable; access to validity information reduced that effect, with workload affecting the benefit. This is not an MHAC-specific experiment and not proof of any single best alarm sound.

**Counter-position:** improving acoustic expressiveness, urgency or semantic richness alone cannot guarantee effective communication if the underlying alarm stream frequently produces false positives or people have no way to verify what triggered it. There are **three different concepts** to preserve: reliability of the alerting mechanism, uncertainty conveyed by this message, and perceived urgency/severity. Combining them into one acoustic “confidence” parameter risks an untestable conflation.

**Suggested next action:** #8/#11 should challenge sound-centric interpretations with decision-theoretic and verification-based explanations; #12/#13 should consider source/evidence-check affordances alongside an audio message (without assuming vision is always available). For #14's synthetic protocol plan, cross alert validity, checkability and acoustic form; keep decision/compliance measures separate. In #5, screen S5 honestly: audio is not demonstrated as an independently manipulated factor by the abstract, so it may belong in Track G/context instead of Track E.

**Evidence class / confidence:** **counterevidence and alternative explanation**, primary-study abstract; **high** identity, **moderate** within-domain outcome interpretation, **low-to-moderate** audio-specific transfer. **Expected value:** high; can prevent apparent certainty from replacing evidence quality. **Falsifier:** under matched predictive validity and checkability, a sound-only manipulation produces robust and transportable improvements in correct action despite false alarms.

### Lead X2 — Perpendicular: greater perceived urgency can coincide with worse recognition

**Source:** S6. Sanderson, Wee & Lacherez (2006) tested IEC 60601-1-8 melodic alarms with 33 non-anaesthetist participants. The publisher and PubMed abstracts report that under the study's training conditions high-priority alarms were **perceived as more urgent** yet identified **more slowly and less accurately** than medium-priority alarms; fewer than 30% of participants reached perfect identification of the tested alarm set. The mapping/standard version and particular melodies are context, not evidence that high priority always worsens recognition.

**Counter-position:** a design that maximizes *urgency impression* could degrade *message identification* and therefore action, especially under learned but complex codebooks. MHAC should not treat urgency rating as a surrogate for detection, comprehension, source identity or time-to-correct-action.

**Suggested next action:** #5/#6 may assess formal eligibility; #9/#10 separate urgency ratings from identification and action outcomes. #13 could state the conditional requirement that monotonic urgency ratings alone are insufficient evidence of safe prioritization. Cross-reference protocol novelty matrix N04 and the existing Edworthy/Hellier source family rather than claim discovery of urgency research.

**Evidence class / confidence:** primary-study **counterexample** (study-specific), source abstract; **high** identity, **moderate-to-high** abstract-level inference, **low-to-moderate** external validity. **Expected value:** high as a prespecified negative case. **Falsifier:** the inversion is an artifact specific to the tested melodies/training and does not appear in better-designed candidate message sets.

### Lead P3 — PARALLEL: acoustic location and verbal direction are complementary, not automatically redundant

**Direct evidence (T1):** Oving, Veltman and Bronkhorst (2004) evaluated three-dimensional cockpit warnings with a visual display present. The publisher abstract describes two simulator experiments: the 3-D audio advantage depended on the concurrent error-display arrangement in experiment one; in experiment two spatial audio and spoken direction each reduced warning response time by about 12%, while their combination reduced it by roughly 23% in that particular arrangement. These are **abstract-reported, conditional effects**, not universal improvement factors. T2 (Edworthy et al., 2017) independently compared four prototype medical alarm sets with the IEC 60601-1-8 set; its author-repository abstract reports improvements in *recognizability* and *localizability* with noted exceptions, illustrating that these are separable design properties. T2's accepted-version repository licence is CC BY-NC-ND and does not authorize redistribution here.

**Distinct MHAC connection:** source *identity* (which system asserted a proposition), external *spatial direction* (where an event appears to be), and *requested action* (what to do) are different semantic fields. Spatializing a sound can supply task-relevant direction, but spatial presentation is not evidence of an external physical origin or independent verification of a claim. R012/R013 should test whether spatial plus explicit directional speech yields additive task-action benefit, mere redundancy, or costly inconsistency. This narrows N06, not a new general cue-family result.

**Next action:** for #5/#6 evaluate T1/T2 as candidate study identities only; in #9 distinguish localization error, semantic identification, response latency and correct action. For #13/#14 sketch a synthetic-only factorial stimulus/protocol with mono/spatial sound × verbal direction absent/present × direction congruent/incongruent, plus screen-reader/headphone and hardware limits. Do **not** encode a final implementation rule, recruit pilots, or assert that headphones reliably externalize spatial sound.

**Evidence/confidence/expected value:** **source-reported experimental findings + cross-domain design hypothesis**. High confidence in bibliographic identity; moderate in the abstract-level condition-dependent effects; low-to-moderate transfer to MHAC. Expected value **moderate-to-high**. **Falsifier:** after controlling cue information and playback hardware, added spatial information does not improve correct directional action, or incongruent cues worsen it.

### Lead O2 — ORTHOGONAL: signal-detection decision criterion and message severity are different quantities

**Evidence and model (T3, T4):** Papastavrou and Lehto (1995) use a distributed signal-detection model to compare persistent versus selectively administered product warnings and report experimental comparisons to normative model predictions. This concerns **warning policy**, not validated sound design. Levine's 1966 *Human Factors* auditory-vigilance experiment explicitly manipulated detection values, miss/false-detection costs and signal load; its indexed abstract attributes some observed performance shifts to changes in the listener's decision criterion rather than sensory sensitivity. This source is historical and closed access; do not interpret the abstract as contemporary performance guidance.

**New methodological connection:** separate (1) acoustic discriminability/detection sensitivity, (2) the receiver's choice criterion, (3) upstream alert-triggering threshold, and (4) the *severity and deadline* stated by an alert. A system can have clear audio but a badly calibrated event threshold, or correct threshold logic yet a user response criterion altered by prior alarms/cost. Urgency timbre is not the same as either threshold. This refines first-pass X1 (alarm reliability) by introducing a falsifiable **sensitivity-vs-criterion decomposition** instead of merely another reliability reminder.

**Next action:** for #8/#11 investigate whether a signal-detection/decision-theoretic account explains MHAC's proposed uncertainty/action mapping more economically than cybersemiotics in the remove-Brier comparison; for #12 consider distinct hypothetical fields for event probability, consequence, trigger policy, human decision and action deadline; for #14 rehearse **synthetic-only** cost matrices and threshold/criterion sweeps, including false alarms, misses, action delays, and abstention. No inference that selective warning is always preferable under all costs or temporal conditions.

**Evidence/confidence/expected value:** **methodological analogy and candidate counter-model** based on T3/T4 abstracts; high bibliographic identity, moderate model relevance, low direct transfer to non-human machine audio. Expected value **high** for disentangling semantics from policy. **Falsifier:** the decomposition fails to yield distinguishable hypotheses or adds no explanatory value over existing N06/R012 contracts.

### Lead X3 — PERPENDICULAR: attention can fail before acoustic message recognition is possible

**Evidence (T5, T6):** Dehais, Causse, Vachon, Régis, Menant and Tremblay (2014) examined 28 pilots in a flight simulator. The abstract reports 11/28 pilots did not appropriately report or respond to an alarm during a windshear task; all detected the alarm in the no-windshear scenario, with important order/pre-exposure qualifications. Dehais, Roy and Scannella (2019) describe a related 13-pilot EEG/oddball study with workload contrast; the abstract treats inattentional deafness as an attentional phenomenon with individual variation. Neither sample establishes a rate for other populations, nor that volume/amplitude remedies the failure.

**Counterposition to P1 and O1:** an alert might be acoustically audible in a laboratory, non-overlapping, correctly prioritized and still **not enter conscious awareness** under visual/cognitive task demands. Delaying nonurgent messages (O1) cannot solve all hard-deadline critical alerts, and making an alert more urgent-sounding (X2) may not recover attentional access. Counterfactual awareness/recognition cannot be inferred just from a missed button press.

**Next action:** #5/#6 assess empirical eligibility, respecting that cockpit tasks may or may not fit protocol E1–E6; #9 partition sensory masking, attentional miss, report/recall and correct action, without coding causal mechanism from outcome alone. #13/#14 consider synthetic/protocol-only dual-task conditions and training/exposure controls; do not use EEG or recruit participants without #20 authorisation.

**Evidence/confidence/expected value:** **empirical counterexample in aviation with auxiliary cognitive-method lead**. High identity, moderate-to-high for study-specific reported result, low-to-moderate transportability. Expected value **high** as a negative case for “audible = communicated.” **Falsifier:** under matched acoustic and task conditions MHAC-like messages do not exhibit any attention-dependent detection/response separation, or reported misses were due to a non-attentional mechanism.

### Lead X4 — PERPENDICULAR: the same acoustic cue may be unavailable to part of the intended audience

**Evidence (T7, T8):** Ghosh, Lockhart and Liu (2015) report a lab study of 28 older and 28 younger participants using audiograms and frequency/temporal/noise manipulations in driving-warning scenarios. Their institutional abstract reports higher detection, caution and urgency thresholds in the older group, notably at 4000 Hz, with interaction among frequencies and the outcomes. Berkowitz and Casali (1990) compared different telephone-ringer spectra in quiet versus 65 dBA pink noise for younger and older groups; their publisher abstract reports an advantage for a low/mid-frequency-rich ringer among the older group and near-inaudibility of one high-frequency beeper in the noise condition. These are *specific samples, playback conditions and sound designs*, not an argument that a particular frequency is universally accessible.

**Counterposition:** a “neutral,” “non-human,” well-learned motif cannot communicate certainty or action to someone who does not hear its critical information. Acoustic accessibility is a **precondition** for inference about semantic comprehension, and hearing/age variation may interact with listening environment. The inclusive design angle is not novel as a field (N01 and prior programme accessibility gates already mention it); the useful extension is a **frequency-/environment-specific audibility eligibility stress test** coupled to R013's proposed semantic vocabulary.

**Next action:** for #5/#6 check T7/T8 eligibility (ringer and vehicle warning settings may be context or excluded); for #9 extract listener hearing criteria, noise spectra, output spectrum, audiometry where lawful and reported outcomes; for #13/#14 prepare simulated frequency-response attenuation and ambient-noise cases with explicit limitations that signal processing is **not** a substitute for human-accessibility evaluation. Require non-audio fallback in safety cases as a **design question**, not an accepted prescription; no participant hearing data collection without gate #20.

**Evidence/confidence/expected value:** **empirical counterevidence to universal audibility**, high bibliographic identity, moderate source-reported directions, low transfer to specific MHAC soundsets. Expected value **high** for accessibility and exclusion-risk review. **Falsifier:** proposed MHAC cues remain detectably and semantically usable across a suitably characterized target population and deployment environment; even then no universal claim follows.

### Lead P4 — PARALLEL: a compositional *one-message* acoustic grammar is distinguishable from mixing multiple alarms

**Source basis:** S9 and S10 describe learning/recognition of *structured* earcons representing menu hierarchy; S11 reports an experimental comparison of two component sounds presented in parallel versus serially, with no recognition-rate difference reported for the particular task. ### Lead P5 — PARALLEL: semantic referent congruence is a possible *training-resistant* cost, distinct from urgency and cue family

### Lead O3 — ORTHOGONAL: notification lifecycle should include recovery of the *interrupted task*, not merely delivery and acknowledgement

**Source basis:** S14 (Dodhia & Dismukes 2009; online 2008) reports two experiments supporting the view that failure to resume an interrupted task involves prospective-memory encoding/retrieval demands. **Potential MHAC connection:** even a correctly heard, correctly identified and actioned alert can induce secondary task loss. ### Lead X5 — PERPENDICULAR: whether an alert calls for action is *situated*, not an intrinsic property of its tone or event type

**Source basis:** S16 (Seagull & Sanderson 2001) is an observational study of audible anaesthesia alarms across four surgical procedure types and three phases. ### P15 — PARALLEL: a rising-level "approach" cue changes perceived speed and sustains attention, not necessarily decision accuracy

**S48 Neuhoff 2016** (two behavioural studies, openly accessible) reports perceived speed of looming sounds exceeding equivalent receding sounds; closer sounds were rated as moving faster. **S49 Glatz & Chuang 2019** (two experiments; open full text with experimental controls) contrasts looming, static and receding sounds in continuous manual-tracking plus peripheral visual target tasks. All cue types aided orienting at an early 250-ms cue–target onset asynchrony; the distinctive looming advantage was *sustained* toward 500 ms, while simultaneous cue and target onset showed no benefit. Their second experiment investigated whether end intensity alone explains the late cueing benefit. These are different task-outcome measures, not independent validation of a machine-message grammar.

**MHAC connection:** A non-human rise/fall amplitude dimension can inadvertently imply physical approach, threat growth or imminent deadline even when the source hazard is constant. For R013/R014, compare (i) physically stationary event with rising cue, (ii) physically approaching event with fixed-level cue, and (iii) explicit spoken time-to-action; measure source-state identification, perceived risk, and chosen action separately from detection/orienting. Also compare 0/250/500 ms cue–information offset and ongoing-task performance. Avoid interpreting the *auditory cue* as an intrinsically truthful predictor of time-to-contact.

**Classification:** established source-reported *task-specific perceptual/attention effects*; transfer to MHAC semantic urgency is **plausible analogy**; benefit to action remains **speculative**. **Value:** high; **confidence:** moderate-to-high for within-study results, low for transfer. **Next:** R008 compare ecological-prediction account with conventional attentional orienting; R013/R014 pre-register separate source truth, sound-envelope and perceived urgency contrasts; #31 can consider bias toward urgency independent of calibrated decisions. **Disconfirmation:** no additional urgency inference or action/attention difference with matched spectrum, RMS, duration, order and training, or any advantage restricted to physical approach tasks.

### P16 — PARALLEL: rising level can recruit physiological alerting even when the acoustic message is affect-neutral by design

**S54 Bach et al. 2008** reports in a controlled fMRI/psychophysiology study that rising rather than falling/constant level elicited greater autonomic orienting and phasic alertness, including amygdala activation. The abstract also explicitly states that its tested right-hemisphere phasic-alertness network interpretation was **not** supported. Neural/autonomic results cannot be converted into an MHAC user's felt emotion or correct safety decision.

**MHAC connection:** The design preference "deliberately non-human / no performed machine emotion" does not guarantee *affective neutrality* of an acoustic ramp. Distinguish simulated emotion in the **emitter**, emotional appraisal in the **listener**, acoustic salience, reported urgency and task consequence (prior X6 affect-leakage, S22; prior S19 envelope detection). **Classification:** established source-reported neural/physiological finding, with speculative cross-domain transfer; not evidence that a rising warning is intrinsically truthful or ethically superior. **Value:** medium; **confidence:** high bibliographic identity, moderate task-specific effect, low MHAC transfer. **Next:** R008/R013 candidate negative-control dimensions for arousal, subjective urgency and actual hazard; avoid neuroimaging as a routine MHAC validation requirement. **Falsifier:** in controlled stimulus comparisons, listener affect/urgency remains unchanged and only signal detectability differs.

### O10 — ORTHOGONAL: distraction from a deviant cue is separable from interference by a changing sound stream

**S50 Hughes, Vachon & Jones 2007** supports two mechanisms in three experimental paradigms: deviation/novelty capture versus changing-state competition with serial-order processing. **S51 Hughes, Hurlstone, Marsh, Vachon & Jones 2013** separately reports that greater focal-task engagement or foreknowledge removed the deviation distraction in tested memory tasks but not the changing-state effect. Authors and research lineage **overlap**; these are **not independent replications** suitable for naive effect pooling. Public institutional abstracts are accessible; full study methods and numerical models were not appraised.

**MHAC connection:** Existing #30 distinguishes masking, detection, attention, identification and action, but an audio code could create **two different costs despite being perfectly audible**: single-signal attentional capture and continuous/alternating message-stream order interference. Salience tuning might improve pickup while harming a concurrent memory task; simple habituation/foreknowledge may reduce one mechanism and leave the other. This complements S25's speech streaming (task-specific memory reduction) rather than restating it.

**Classification:** established source-reported mechanistic dissociation in short-term-memory tasks; a **methodological tool** and plausible analogy for MHAC. **Value:** high; **confidence:** high for bibliographic identities, medium for reported dissociation, low for MHAC causal transfer. **Next:** #30/R009 outcome taxonomy should consider deviation versus changing-state as *candidate* confounders; R014 pre-specify interrupted working-memory/order tasks separately from detection, with alternating versus stable backgrounds, deviant onset and foreknowledge. **Falsifier:** removing changing-state modulation eliminates the putative memory interference while keeping deviant cues; or both effects track only acoustic masking/intelligibility under matched controls. Do not infer an executable cognitive model from synthetic state-machine traces.

### X12 — PERPENDICULAR: a looming ramp is not a universally superior location or urgency code

**S52 McCarthy & Olsen 2017** (2016 online, 2017 print; 26 listeners, within-participant categorical sound-localization task) tested rising/falling/steady envelopes crossed with pure tone, vowel-like and broadband-noise spectra. Reported quicker/more accurate location judgments for rising rather than falling ramps, *but* white noise and vowel-like sound conditions outperformed pure tones, contrary to a second proposed hypothesis. This is a critical feature-interaction counterexample, not an MHAC accessibility trial.

**Counterposition:** A simplistic \`ramp = universally better\` inference ignores acoustic spectrum, playback chain (prior S39 hearing aids), room reflection, location cues and the source/event semantic distinction. A sound that captures attention is not necessarily one that identifies its referent accurately; physical approach signals may be actively misleading for nonspatial system messages. **Classification:** primary empirical counterevidence against a spectrum-agnostic cue rule; MHAC-specific generalization is **hypothesis**. **Value:** medium-high; **confidence:** moderate on abstract-reported within-task effect, low on other listeners/devices. **Next:** #34/R013 include crossed ramp direction × spectrum, plus mono/processing and nonspatial countercases, as proposed controls; do *not* lock in a specific modulation profile or claim universality. **Falsifier of the concern:** robust equal action/meaning discrimination across controlled spectral profiles and devices without context-induced false approach inference.

### X13 — PERPENDICULAR: even a "simple" interrupt has a recovery tail; audio is not guaranteed to preempt vision

**S53 Nees & Sampsell 2021** (primary controlled experiment; **62 participants**, 58 female and four male in the publisher/PubMed abstract) compared simple auditory tones and visual icons during continuous visual tracking plus an alert choice-response task. The abstract reports that visual interruptions harmed tracking more; audio produced the lowest dual-task workload **when noise was absent**. Interruption effects on tracking persisted around **1,500 ms** and the authors found **no evidence of auditory preemption**. This is an important *conditional counterexample* to declaring all audio interruptions cost-free or all audio inevitably dominant.

**MHAC connection:** Prior S14/S15 considered task resumption and S44/S45 considered workload-dependent multimodality. S53 adds a **quantified post-interruption observation window and background-noise interaction** for protocol design, without establishing a universal 1.5-s recovery rule. Differentiate alert onset, intended action deadline, response time and recovery tail. **Classification:** source-reported primary empirical findings, contextual counterevidence to blanket modality claims. **Value:** medium-high; **confidence:** moderate task-specific effect, low transfer to complex messages. **Next:** R009/R014 record post-alert disruption as a separate endpoint and sensitivity-window hypothesis, and compare quiet versus background-noise conditions; don't assume synthetic traces can reproduce human resumption durations. **Falsifier:** matched MHAC message/task comparisons show no post-alert decrement beyond immediate response or clear modality-specific reversal.
