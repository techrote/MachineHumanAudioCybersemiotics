### PARALLEL — action-relevant modality comparisons, not cue-family rankings

**P39 / S133 — multimodal alert may help when a response is urgent.** Ho, Reed & Spence (2007) compared auditory-only, tactile-only and audiotactile warnings in a collision-avoidance driving simulator, including background-radio and brake-light manipulations. The **primary publisher abstract** reports faster *brake initiation* for combined cues than either single channel. **Class:** reported within-experiment human behavioral result; **analogy** to MHAC. **Connection:** R013/R014 and #30 distinguish response initiation from semantic comprehension, correct authorized action, workload and workload cost. **Value:** high, because the study uses a consequential action proxy rather than only sound recognition. **Confidence:** high identity, moderate abstract-level result, low transfer. **Next:** R005 eligibility/identity check, R009 outcome taxonomy; possible R014 conditional matched-content/latency design. **Countertest:** redundant cue speeds first movement but increases wrong action or fails deadline-correct action in a new domain. No real collision-safety conclusion for MHAC.

**P40 / S134 — tactile-only can outperform other warning implementations under some timing conditions.** Scott & Gray (2008) studied 16 simulator participants under no-warning, auditory, visual and tactile rear-end collision conditions at two time-to-collision thresholds (3 and 5 s). The publisher abstract reports the *shortest mean* brake response time for tactile warnings and statistically shorter times than **no-warning and visual** conditions; do **not** claim a statistically significant tactile-versus-auditory superiority from this abstract. **Class:** small primary simulator result and **counterexample** to assuming audio is always fastest. **Connection:** R008's non-audio alternative framework, R013/R014 comparative baselines, and S127/S131 on varying reception/tolerability. **Value:** high as a negative-control modality. **Confidence:** high identity, moderate abstract result, low population/setting transfer. **Next:** candidate corpus / R005 cutoff and R006 identity; retain explicit tactile alternative without expanding MHAC into a haptic product.

### ORTHOGONAL — mechanism and statistical discipline

**O37 / S135 — faster redundant responses do not by themselves show integrated information or better understanding.** Gondan & Minakata (2016) provide an expert **methodological tutorial** on redundant-signals race-model-inequality (RMI) tests, highlighting false-positive rate, statistical power, omissions, anticipations and interpretation. A simple fastest-channel race can speed response even without integrated semantic processing. **Class:** methodological tool, not MHAC-specific experimental evidence. **Connection:** challenge strong claims about multimodal synergy in R008/R009; distinguish reaction-time distribution, action error, and comprehension. **Value:** high for avoiding false mechanism attribution. **Confidence:** high for abstract/tutorial metadata, moderate for method implications pending full-text appraisal. **Next:** use an RMI methodological checklist only if real RT distributions are eventually available under authorized R020; a synthetic timing model is only a rehearsal, never a substitute for participants. The already scouted S44's RMI application is a **reuse**, not a new record.

**O38 / S136 — cue-driven action and perceptual attention can diverge.** Ho, Tan & Spence (2006) used spatially predictive auditory and vibrotactile cues under high visual-attention load and an orthogonal cue/task arrangement. In the publisher abstract, **valid auditory** cues facilitated later visual-target discrimination; equally informative tactile cues did **not** in that arrangement, despite earlier cue studies showing improved driving responses. The authors distinguish attentional cuing from motor-response priming and propose a spatial-co-location explanation. **Class:** primary empirical within-task result; alternative explanatory mechanism; setting-limited. **Connection:** #30's causal failure stages and R008's simpler perceptual/response accounts can explain some action improvement without assigning additional semantic meaning to cues. **Value:** high; **confidence:** high identity, moderate abstract effect, low transfer. **Next:** R009 outcome extraction candidate, R013/R014 contrasting spatial cue validity and response mapping. Not evidence tactile warning systems generally fail.

### PERPENDICULAR — extra channels and valid cues may impose costs

**X41 / S137 — redundant warnings' apparent benefit depends on workload and can carry affective cost.** Biondi, Leo, Gastaldi, Rossi & Mulatti (2017) compared auditory, tactile and combined in-vehicle warnings under workload/interpulse manipulations. The primary publisher abstract reports that the **low-workload** multimodal benefit was not seen as overall workload rose; it explicitly evaluated urgency, annoyance and acceptability. **Class:** reported source result and counterposition; no MHAC outcome validated. **Connection:** revisits S44/S45 without recounting them; #31 and R013/R014 should reject unconditional redundant-channel prescriptions. **Value:** high as workload falsifier; **confidence:** high identity, moderate within-task claim, low MHAC transfer. **Next:** R005/R006 candidate search, separate response benefit from annoyance/acceptance and time pressure. Avoid assuming more channels are benign or universally effective.

**X42 / S138 — the *timing* of a directional warning can reverse its apparent benefit.** Tilak, Xholi, Schowalter, Ferris, Hameed & Sarter (2008) manipulated valid/invalid auditory and tactile cues, stimulus-onset asynchrony and simulated driving workload. Their primary conference abstract reports that valid tactile spatial cues helped more at shorter (~100–200 ms) onsets, while auditory validity helped at 500 ms and was **slower at 1000 ms**. These are task-specific cue effects, not an intrinsic auditory latency law. **Class:** empirical counterevidence to monotonic lead-time or modality advantage. **Connection:** #30's timing and attention failure modes, #41's received-channel tests and R014's prospective timing controls. **Value:** high for a falsifiable counterexample, **confidence:** high identity, moderate abstract-level direction, low MHAC transfer. **Next:** compare cue lead, message age, channel congruence, and false spatial guidance separately; never transplant these milliseconds as system defaults.

### Cross-axis question — redundant detection is not redundant meaning

**Hypothesis, not established evidence.** A second sensory channel can accelerate motor initiation through statistical facilitation, spatial orienting or motor priming without improving correct interpretation of the machine's **source**, **event truth**, **addressee**, **permission**, **deadline** and **requested action**. Conversely, a tactile or visual alternative might outperform audio under restricted reception or access conditions; that does not make either modality universally accessible.

**Bounded next method (synthetic only, no issue creation):** Draft 24–32 fictional truth-labelled message/response cases crossing unimodal/combined notification, correct/incorrect cue–target spatial agreement, short/long lead time and high/low primary-task load; include auditory-receive failure and tactile/visual-unavailable negative controls. Represent source/event truth, target actor, action validity, timing/deadline and channel availability **independently**. A deterministic policy oracle may identify logically wrong or stale actions; it **must not invent human response times, perceptual urgency, annoyance, comfort or cognitive integration**. Produce a comparator matrix with explicit *unmeasured* behavioral cells and conditional future R014/R020 measurement protocol: RT distributions with misses, correct action before deadline, semantic interpretation, false actor actions, primary-task costs and tolerability. RMI is only appropriate for actually observed matched-task response distributions, with assumptions, omitted responses and error controls checked.

**Decision consequences:** If an external channel consistently prevents a logical communication failure on fictional traces, retain a **channel fallback/interface contract**, not a production haptic implementation. If the benefit depends only on idealized channel availability or adds contradictory cues, narrow to optional alternatives or reject redundancy as a default. If subsequent authorized human data show only detection/RT gain but null or worse accurate action, reject the *communication-effectiveness* claim while keeping its narrow attentional result. If R008/R013 determine MHAC needs only explicit speech plus optional accessible text in non-critical settings, stop this tangent. Do not broaden into automotive safety certification or a new modality ranking: N01 already marks broad cue-family ranking as covered.

**Risks / limitations / disclosure.** Simulator choice, base rate, auditory masking, handedness, device placement, disability-specific access, visual/tactile availability, coincident alert timing, task load and expectancy can confound between-study comparisons. Studies may share author groups, but independent participants cannot be assumed without R006; no quantitative pooling or cross-study numerical effect estimate.
