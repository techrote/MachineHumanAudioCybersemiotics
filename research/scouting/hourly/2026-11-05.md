### PARALLEL — empirical forecast advice and decisions (S295–S296)

**S295 — Forecast uncertainty plus advice can outperform advice-only in a specific task.** Joslyn and LeClerc's 2012 three-experiment road-salting task compared deterministic temperature predictions, numerical uncertainty and categorical advice. The PubMed abstract reports better decisions with explicit uncertainty and the best performance when uncertainty and advice were combined. **Class:** established *source-reported* experimental result, applied by **plausible analogy** only; the stimuli were weather forecasts and mostly visual/numeric tasks, not MHAC speech. **MHAC connection:** a machine saying “temperature is above threshold” reports an observation, while “temperature may cross threshold within 20 minutes” reports a model-dependent future proposition. Recommendation, predicted hazard, observed hazard and response cost must not be collapsed. **Expected value / confidence:** high for opening a distinct semantic falsifier; high bibliographic confidence, moderate task-specific result confidence from abstract, low direct auditory transfer. **Next:** candidate R008 comparison/R013 hypothesis only if MHAC plans future-state predictions; check R005/R006 identity/eligibility before any accepted record.

**S296 — Forecast use is conditioned by the decision, not a generic probability display.** Möhrlen, Bessa and Fleischhut (2022) report a workshop decision-game with 105 energy-sector participants comparing deterministic and probabilistic wind-production forecasts for a stylized trading decision. The publisher HTML describes the decision task and supplies an OSF replication-data link. **Class:** primary empirical decision study; *analogy* to machine-generated action advice, not an audio test. **Connection:** forecasting energy availability differs from announcing actual current machine output; a horizon, loss/benefit function and reliance on model forecasts need explicit identities. **Value/confidence:** moderate-high; original full-text HTML accessible but this scouting pass checked the abstract/description, not the entire methods/data. **Next:** candidate evidence-identity check, then R008 cost–loss comparator if scope survives its gate. Do not invent numerical effect sizes.

### ORTHOGONAL — forecast verification versus impact/action provenance (S297–S298)

**S297 — A calibrated *prediction* and a true *current observation* answer different questions.** Gneiting and Raftery (2007) develop strictly proper scoring rules for probabilistic forecasts. A forecast's scoring and calibration require a defined prediction target and later realizing outcome; they do not confer a presently observed hazard or justify a particular action. **Class:** mathematical/methodological tool, not evidence about listener behaviour. **Connection:** #31 considers alert validity and decision criteria; this increment is the *temporal target* (issue time, predicted valid interval, model version, event actually observed, and forecast revision). **Value/confidence:** high conceptual leverage, high bibliographic certainty, article-level mathematical details not re-derived or independently proved here. **Next:** consider forecast scoring and forecast-vs-observation truth-table fields in R012/R014 only conditionally.

**S298 — Hazard forecasts, impacts and recommended protection are distinct service layers.** WMO's 13 May 2020 institutional notice about its 2015 *Guidelines on Multi-Hazard Impact-Based Forecast and Warning Services* describes why predicting environmental conditions alone is insufficient for recipient-centred protective decisions, with exposure/vulnerability and cross-agency context. **Class:** authoritative *normative/methodological guidance*, not an experimental outcome; 2015 original report and later edition were **not appraised** in this pass. **Connection:** R008 and R012 should distinguish forecasted state, affected recipient, expected consequence and authorized action, rather than copying weather warning scales or adopting emergency-service operating rules. **Value/confidence:** moderate conceptual transfer, high institutional provenance, low MHAC-specific validation. **Next:** use as a comparator in an applicability memo; do not implement a weather-alerting service.

### PERPENDICULAR — forecast communication can systematically mislead (S299–S301)

**S299 — A one-sided worst-case boundary may anchor action judgments.** Joslyn, Savelli and Nadav-Greenberg (2011) tested single worst-case bounds against balanced intervals or no uncertainty information in four weather-related decision studies. The publisher abstract reports biased estimates and, in many conditions, more precautionary decisions under the single extreme bound. **Class:** primary empirical *counterevidence* against “maximum-risk phrasing is always safest”; not proof of a specific spoken-audio effect. **Connection:** R013's candidate speech warning should not turn a forecast quantile into a certain present fact or silently omit that it is one side of a distribution. **Value/confidence:** high conceptual counterexample; abstract-level access, uncertain auditory transfer. **Next:** fictional worst-case versus two-sided-future-bound control; retain consequences of being over- versus under-cautious.

**S300 — “Cry wolf” cannot be treated as a universal linear rule.** LeClerc and Joslyn (2015) manipulated advice false-alarm histories in repeated weather decisions. The journal abstract reports poor decisions at both very high and very low false-alarm rates and **no significant improvement from a small rate reduction**, while probabilistic uncertainty improved decisions in their task. **Class:** primary experimental *counterposition* to a simplistic “every false warning destroys trust” narrative, not clinical alarm performance. **Connection:** #31's credibility/reliance controls and future forecast updates; avoid claiming that a predicted but unrealized event is necessarily a defective warning without the issuance-time forecast target and decision-cost context. **Value/confidence:** moderate-high, abstract read, full article not appraised. **Next:** use as a counterexample in R008/R013; check overlap with S295 (same research lineage, not independent replication).

**S301 — A forecast may be calibrated yet require an explicit cost-of-action decision.** Durage, Wirasinghe and Ruwanpura (2015) propose a probabilistic decision-tree analysis for tornado-warning and evacuation decisions, distinguishing false-warning and missed-event costs. **Class:** formal/decision-analysis *alternative framework*, not empirical proof that listeners obey advice; study's Canadian regional assumptions are not transferable. **Connection:** a machine's forecast score alone cannot establish “must act now”; #49 still owns mode validity, #43 actor authority, #47 deadlines, #56 post-emission correction. **Value/confidence:** moderate; publisher abstract and DOI checked, detailed model and input data not critically reproduced. **Next:** conditional R008 comparison, not a production decision policy.

### Cross-axis discriminating question — forecast issued now is not an observed event now

**Exploratory hypothesis.** If MHAC ever voices predictive warnings, it must test whether a rendered utterance inadvertently promotes a future conditional projection into a presently observed machine-state claim or an unconditional authorized instruction. Retain distinct candidate fields: `observed_state_at_t`, `forecast_target`, `prediction_issued_at`, `forecast_valid_from/until`, `forecast_probability/interval`, `model_and_evidence_version`, `recipient_consequence`, `action_deadline`, and `authorization_status`. These are *comparison variables, not adopted schemas*. A revised forecast is neither the same event as an observed hazard nor automatically a cancellation of an already-issued action obligation.

**Stage-0 applicability / dedup stop:** first determine whether R012–R014 actually envisage *prospective machine forecasts* rather than observed-condition announcements. Recheck #31 (uncertainty/calibration), #34 (field binding), #36 (persistent state), #47 (freshness/deadline), #49 (mode permission), #54 (historical references), #56 (post-emission retraction), and newly opened #58 (co-occurrence/source independence). **If no prospective forecast use case exists or accepted work already tests forecast-vs-observation authority, stop; do not promote another issue or build a new oracle.**

**Bounded candidate synthetic test if the gate passes:** independently label 24–32 fictional timestamped cases spanning present observed threshold, forecast-only crossing in 5/20/60 minutes, false projected crossing, correct forecast later verified, forecast withdrawn/revised, stale prediction whose valid interval passed, model/telemetry missing, different action costs/deadlines, inaccessible recipient and known contradictory current measurements. Compare (A) naive voice treating prediction as observed state, (B) present-state-only reporting, (C) typed prediction/provenance with explicit horizon and uncertainty, and (D) prediction plus consequence-specific conditional advice. Predeclare false current-state assertion, unsupported action obligation, lost valid warning, forecast/observation conflation, revision binding, total message length, and time until actionable deadline. **A present-state-only solution that avoids all false claims is a valid null winner.** Synthetic truth tables cannot demonstrate listener interpretation, real forecasting skill, human action, or operational safety; those require separate protocol/ethics gates.

**Decision implications:** a demonstrated logical conflation warrants consideration of explicit forecast-target fields only if in-scope. If typed temporal fields offer no benefit over simple present-state speech, reject the extension. If action costs dominate, prioritize transparent decision support over complex sonic encodings. If source studies conflict or prospective forecasts are absent, record narrowing/no-go. No acceptance into Track E/G, R012 programme, actual speaking policy or participant research occurs here.
