**Overlap alert for the applied track.** Chapter 10, Jacques, "Human-Computer Interaction Design and the Cybersemiotic Experience" (pp. 241–281), applies Brier's framework to user-interface design and interactive environments. Its abstract and visible notes show no treatment of sound or alerts and no user evaluation, but it is an existing cybersemiotic HCI application. The R002 overlap matrix has been updated (row N10) and #8 must read it.

Horta's chapter is titled as a critical review of cybersemiotic observation; Cobley's review raises the constructivism and terminology issues that Brier's 2009 Constructivist Foundations article appears to address. These are the first targets for #4's criticism search and #8's comparison.

## 9. Coverage limits

- No bibliographic database search and no forward citation tracing; reception coverage is therefore thin and biased toward what web search surfaces.
- English-language searching only; Brier's Danish-language publications are not covered.
- 11 of the 28 inventory rows carry candidate or conflicting metadata.
- Tool-mediated paraphrase is the only reading route used; no record asserts a hashed reading.
- The inventory is not exhaustive: Brier's PhilPeople profile counts 34 publications whose list could not be opened, and other work (e.g. in *Cybernetics and Human Knowing*, which Brier edits) was not systematically sought.
