# Rival mechanisms and matched-truth caveat

| Mechanism | Plausible benefit to test, not established | Failure modes and baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Direct positive (`DO B`) | Short, simple instruction | Unwarranted paraphrase if B merely permitted; falsify with explicit policy truth. |
| Direct prohibition (`DO NOT A`) | Explicit forbidden-action prevention | Negator masked/lost, wrong target A, interference; compare text/non-audio repeat. |
| Contrastive (`DO B NOT A`) | May correct prepotent A, reduce later corrections | Longer utterance and initial latency, scope errors, A not truly forbidden; no-extra-contrast comparator. |
| Factual negative/status | Separate hazard facts from requested action | “Not cleared” mistaken for “do not act,” false all-clear and unknown masquerading as inactive. |
| Accessible repeat/non-audio | Redundancy and reinspection | Competing visual workload, hearing/assistive-device or linguistic conditions; cannot assume universal superiority. |

**Not always truth-equivalent:** one cannot force a between-mechanism experiment to compare a genuine prohibition against a *false* affirmative command for a matched trial. Test wording variants within a state supporting both facts, or stratify by genuinely different decision states. Duration, prior expectation, information payload, negator location, semantic scope, training, voice style, speech-in-noise, listening aids and primary-task disruption are rival mechanisms. S61 supplies context-specific counterevidence; S62 cautions that faster initial responses may accompany more downstream action errors. No selected ranking or cross-study numerical effect estimate is justified here.
