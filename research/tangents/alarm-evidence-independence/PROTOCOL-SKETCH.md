## Question, design and falsifiers

A future preregistration-ready matched-information comparison could manipulate **upstream dependence** (one observation, common cause, independently supported, unknown), **acoustic/temporal proximity** (near versus separated) and **cue-identity distinctness** (one symbolic cue or two distinct cues); balance truth, action, consequence and urgency independently. Present participants with permitted fictional nonhazardous decisions only after lawful human approval. Separate device source labels from observation IDs in an explicit comprehension check. Control apparent source count and avoid unannounced text clues.

**Possible hypotheses:** Proximity may increase *perceived* evidence independence even when the same input is relayed (H1), and device/cue grouping may cause omission of a second independently justified action (H2). **Null:** no effect in comprehension, first/final choice or independent source attribution. **Opposite effect:** explicit dependence context may actually increase reliance on appropriate independent messages or slow/overload decision making. Comparisons must include separate reporting D and source-aware C, not only obviously defective heuristics.

## Measure without conflation

Predefine whether each individual recognizes the alarm, identifies its source/observation lineage, judges event likelihood, understands requested action, chooses first versus final action, and meets its independent fictional deadline. Record confidence, confusion, false corroboration, false suppression, reaction time with censored non-actions, total cue exposure, memory and workload separately. Use trial-level logging, order randomization, item-level counterbalancing and negative no-action cases; treat chance/unknown responses as legitimate outcomes. Account for clustering by listener and scenario, and participant effects; **do not** choose a sample size or power target from the current 40 fictional cases.

## Confounds, access and competing explanations

Audio masking, gain, directionality, listening device/hearing variation, training with learned codes, language/culture, motor task cost, urgency, base rate, source authority, modality (speech/visual), working-memory load, and false or omitted provenance labels could explain or reverse results. Any hearing/accessibility provision requires explicit approved design and accessible alternatives; no claim of universal sound semantics. A simple audio overlap effect may not be an epistemic-corroboration effect. The experiment must distinguish a listener's belief from correct action, a simulated computer's state, a subject's self-report and actual observation independence.
