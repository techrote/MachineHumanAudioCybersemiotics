# T003 — future empirical comparison, not executed

**Status:** preregistration-ready **outline only**. No formal preregistration, human study, actual sounds, participants, permission or ethical approval. MHAC-R020 (#20) is an explicit separate human gate, after #14/#18 and authorized ethics/consent/privacy/safe-listening/accessibility planning.

## Questions and primary estimands

For [arms A–D](COMPARATORS.md), under matched hypothetical machine–human messaging tasks, estimate differences in **proportion of all trials with correct action by the stated action deadline** (count omissions and late/wrong actions as failure) and **correct source/event/action comprehension**. Do not condition latency only on successful trials; define separate time-to-action/omission estimands and report cumulative incidence or an appropriate predeclared alternative.

Secondary outcomes: detection, delivery/response failure, source attribution, stale-version acceptance, correct action type, request count, repair attempt/success, interruption cost, independent task performance, workload, perceived urgency/trust, delayed retention and access-related differences. Self-report of understanding is *not* a substitute for scored interpretation.

## Discriminating hypotheses and alternatives

- H1: C improves objective source/event comprehension over B for semantic ambiguity when enough time exists. **Null/alternative:** B/D equal C with less interruption; abandon rich repair.
- H2: C increases missed deadlines under short slack. **Counterposition:** it may prevent more wrong action than it delays; measure **net correct action by deadline**.
- H3: Version-bound replies lower acceptance of stale answers. **Alternative:** simple serial updates/visible current-state display suffice. Reject C if participants accept stale information anyway or version cues cause errors.
- H4: D improves some accessibility outcomes but can worsen visually or manually demanding tasks. Do not assume one channel is universally accessible.

## Bounded, prospective method

1. Before participant exposure, specify materials and a controlled fictional desktop/workshop event scenario with no physical hazard or device actuation. Determine whether within- or between-participant testing is justified by carryover/training. Predeclare randomized/counterbalanced order, source/event ambiguity type, workload, deadline information and explanatory-message content.
2. Match message semantics, training dose, referent difficulty, response opportunities, lexical specificity, duration, delivery latency, loudness/intelligibility and environmental noise as feasible. Keep non-ASR button/text alternatives available; assess hearing/language/motor/visual accessibility limits.
3. Check manipulation validity first: can participants hear/discriminate messages, identify source/event, understand what repair controls do, distinguish deadline from severity and recognize when the system cannot answer? Use objective scored outcomes.
4. Predeclare trial and person-level missingness, non-response, late action, accidental success, multiple hypotheses, clustering and sensitivity analyses. Report all denominators, abstention/unknown and condition failures.
5. Set a smallest decision-relevant difference from a justified evidence base, then perform power/precision sensitivity analysis with plausible ranges; **do not derive human effect sizes, sample sizes or confidence from fictional deterministic cases**.
6. Review rights, consent, privacy, recruitment, safe listening/calibration, stop rules, accessible alternatives and the actual authorized oversight under #20. Until then no recruitment, real recordings, testing or external submission.
