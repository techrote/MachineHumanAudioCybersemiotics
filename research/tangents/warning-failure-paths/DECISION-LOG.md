## Strongest direct source contrasts and counterinterpretations

- Lacherez, Seah and Sanderson (2007), *Human Factors* 49(4), DOI 10.1518/001872007X215719: abstract reports very difficult identity recognition under overlapping melodic alarms in 14 nurses. Crucially, concurrent arithmetic reportedly affected **response time but not accuracy** in those conditions, contrary to an unqualified workload-causes-identity-error claim. Full text restricted.
- Bolton, Zheng, Li, Edworthy and Boyd (2020), *Human Factors* 62(6), DOI 10.1177/0018720819862911: publisher abstract says 28 nursing students judged the **presence** of sounds, with higher misses and lower sensitivity under model-predicted masking. It does **not** test action interpretation or consequential intervention.
- Dehais et al. (2014), *Human Factors* 56(4), DOI 10.1177/0018720813510735: abstract reports 11/28 pilots not reporting or appropriately reacting in a windshear scenario; scenario order/pre-exposure changes the result. This does not prove universal inattentional deafness rates, nor isolate decision from sensory reception.
- Dehais, Roy and Scannella (2019), *Behavioural Brain Research* 360, DOI 10.1016/j.bbr.2018.11.045: PubMed abstract describes 13 pilots, a high-workload oddball paradigm and EEG associations. Classification in a small task sample is not mechanistic identification of every nonresponse nor evidence of independent participant samples relative to other reports.
- Sanderson, Wee and Lacherez (2006), *Anaesthesia* 61(2), DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2044.2005.04502.x: publisher HTML Summary/Results/Discussion describe 33 non-anaesthetists; the tested high-priority melodies sounded more urgent but were named slower and less accurately than medium-priority melodies. Mapping, training and sample limits are substantial; this does not establish MHAC speech performance.

These are **source-attributed observations** and comparison hypotheses, not independently appraised empirical claims. See [candidate CSV](CANDIDATE-EVIDENCE.csv) for the exact accessible locators and retrieval limits.

## Falsifiable rival models

**M0: action-only consequential benchmark.** Model task-appropriate action and latency from observed context and acoustic exposure. Stage probes may be expensive or redundant. Prefer M0 if additional stage instruments do not improve predeclared outcome prediction, calibration or remedial design choices.

**M1: stage-separated instrument.** Distinguish emission/exposure, detection, identity, requested-action interpretation, choice and deadline-sensitive completion. Prefer M1 only if independent measurements disclose materially different treatment contrasts or produce an actionable improvement on held-out conditions after accounting for added measurement burden.

Possible discriminators: overlap degrades identity while detection stays constant; masking degrades detection; workload alters action time but not identity. Missing observables or shared confounders make neither model identifiable. A null result or no incremental predictive utility supports parsimony, not a post-hoc rescue of M1.
